# Initial exploratory result and decision

The complete raw evidence and machine-checked empirical manuscript counts are
in [the pilot summary](../evidence/pilot-summary.json). Independent local audit
verified every file hash, complete planned trial census, and full trajectory
equality for the fixed-rate and recurrence policies.

The Hénon positive control contains verified attracting cycles. The coarse tanh
training scan contains unresolved and divergent runs and no verified cycles
within its specified horizon, tolerance, grid, tasks, and starts. The separate
utility panel contains no recurrence-triggered intervention, so that policy
follows exactly the fixed-rate trajectories while incurring diagnostic work.
Every policy retains the divergent cases; no successful-only mean is presented
as an overall benefit.

This does not show that neural networks lack shrimps. It shows that the initial
regime has not yet supplied the periodic objects needed for the proposed
geometry experiment. Nor is the inactive detector a meaningful comparison of
rescue strategies on an established neural cycle. The appropriate next gate
is a neural periodic positive control, followed by a better-qualified search;
see [the explicit next experiments](next-steps.md).

The practical question remains whether geometry tells us where to move, when
to stop, or which robust setting to select more cheaply than ordinary loss,
recurrence, local stability, or a performance scan. A stable cycle is neither
automatically useful nor automatically harmful. Its task quality, prevalence,
accessibility, intervention response, and measurement cost determine that.

All initial pilots used the local CPU. No RunPod rental or paid review was made.
The six-figure diagrams of large fractal scans are not necessary to qualify the
next instrument gate, so no paid run is proposed here.
