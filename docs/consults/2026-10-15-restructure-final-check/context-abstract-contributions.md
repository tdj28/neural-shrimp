# Context: title, abstract and contributions as they stand (verbatim, LaTeX stripped)

Title: Chaos and bifurcations in continuous language-model feedback

## Abstract

Feeding a language model the expected embedding of its own next token, as Soft Thinking does, turns inference into a nonlinear dynamical system. In Qwen2.5, OLMo-2 and Llama-3 models of 0.5B to 72B parameters, temperature and steering move this loop between repeating text and chaos, with positive Lyapunov exponents that exact tangents reproduce. The chaos has practical consequences. Rerun with only the batch size changed, chaotic float64 loops separate until their most probable tokens differ, after about the number of steps the Lyapunov exponent sets, while periodic loops mostly stay together, in four models of two families up to 70B. In frozen tests on arithmetic, the batch layout changed 56% of the answers of bfloat16 soft-thinking inference in a 0.5B model and 55% in a 7B model, far more often than those of a forced greedy baseline. Exactly repeating text can hide a chaotic state. Along the sampled orbits, steering creates the chaos mainly by making next tokens compete, which turns the softmax readout from a contraction into an expansion; a logit bias alone suffices. Earlier injection carries chaos to lower temperature, two steering vectors at different depths create chaos neither causes alone, and two loops writing one text lock each other into periodic orbits that neither has alone. Beyond first-token uncertainty, the loop's regime does not mark the accuracy of ordinary answers, and we find no edge-of-chaos advantage. The loop obeys quantitative laws of low-dimensional maps: doubling ratios approach Feigenbaum's constant (4.6688 against 4.6692), periodic windows accumulate on folds with a 1/N^2 law, fold normal forms predicted 23 of 24 frozen passage times within one iterate, and interval arithmetic proves an attracting period-8 orbit. In the recurrent-depth model Huginn, a frozen test confirmed a spectrally predicted accuracy loss. The loop is not ordinary sampled generation.

## Contributions

The results fall into four groups.
- Chaos in released models, and what it does to inference (Sections [sec:maps]   and [sec:consequences]; Appendix [sec:scale]). Temperature and steering move the loop of Qwen2.5, OLMo-2 and   Llama-3 models between repeating text and chaos with embedded windows. The regime decides whether a run can be   reproduced. Run twice with only the batch layout changed, 190 of 192 chaotic float64 loops of Qwen2.5-0.5B grow past   a relative distance of 10^{-2} after a median of 102 steps, in line with the Lyapunov exponent (median ratio   0.91), and at 173 of them the most probable readout token comes to differ; 182 of 192 periodic loops stay within   10^{-2}. Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B without steering (24 of 24 and 20 of 20 chaotic loops diverge, 0 of 24 and 1   of 20 periodic ones) and Llama-3.3-70B with steering (24 of 24, none of 24) behave alike. In bfloat16 nearly every   loop diverges, and in a frozen test on 96 additions the batch layout changed the answer of a soft-thinking pipeline   in 56% of problems in Qwen2.5-0.5B and 55% in Qwen2.5-7B, against never and 10% for a forced greedy   baseline. Exactly repeating text can hide a chaotic state (Section [sec:organization]). Beyond the model's   first-token uncertainty, the loop's regime carries no detectable information about the accuracy of ordinary sampled   answers, and we find no advantage at the edge of chaos.
- Why the loop is chaotic, and how steering makes it so (Section [sec:theory]). The loop's derivative   factorizes into the inverse temperature, the covariance of token embeddings under the model's own next-token   distribution, and the network's Jacobian; without the network the map would be a modern Hopfield update, which   cannot cycle. The factorization proves contraction at sufficiently high temperature, the zero-temperature limit is   eventually periodic, and chaos in our sweeps occupies intermediate temperatures. Stability belongs to the loop, not   to its steps: along a period-8 orbit every step expands some direction while the cycle contracts. Along the sampled   orbits, steering makes next tokens compete. Steering into chaos raises the readout factor of each step's growth by   2.58 nats per step relative to nearly unsteered cells, while the network factor changes by only -0.07 (interval   [-0.23,0.12]); a logit bias alone, with the network untouched, makes 3.5% of the plane chaotic; and an   orbit-preserving crossover attributes the expansion at fixed states to steering's change of the readout (+0.81   nats per step in the 0.5B model), not of the network, whose change damps it (-0.49). Random directions also steer   into chaos. Earlier steering carries chaos to lower temperature, in Qwen2.5-0.5B and Llama-3.3-70B, and two vectors   at two depths create chaos where neither alone does (Section [sec:consequences]).
- Low-dimensional organization, predicted from derivatives (Sections [sec:adding]   to [sec:organization]). The windows carry concrete texts: a period-adding family gains one ``is X'' pair per   period step. A fold's normal form, computed from derivatives, predicts without fitting how long trajectories linger   near the vanished orbit: at two new folds, 23 of 24 frozen forecasts fell within one iterate (the all-row criterion   failed by one row), and windows accumulate on the fold with a 1/N^2 law. On the seven fold curves we continued,   all nine tangent reversals are located cusps, and near them local unfoldings, frozen before solving, matched the   orbits that Newton solves recovered at twelve parameter offsets in number and at eleven in stability. A doubling   cascade has superstable points through period 768 and finite-order Feigenbaum ratios that approach \delta (the   last, 4.6688, against 4.6692). The shrimp-like windows are built from bent fold curves with cusps and flip   curves; where a one-dimensional map would have a shrimp's centre, their orbits' leading multiplier meets the second   instead. An interval-arithmetic proof shows that a primitive period-8 orbit exists and is locally attracting, to our   knowledge the first proof of a periodic orbit of a full transformer language-model map   (Appendix [sec:evidence]).
- Beyond the one-token loop (Sections [sec:beyond] and [sec:dialogue]). The chaos is not an   artifact of the one-token window: with a memory of 8 or 16 soft tokens Qwen2.5-7B is chaotic at more temperatures   than with one, and in Soft Thinking's growing context a tiny early perturbation grows exponentially inside its   one-token chaotic band, though not in every model. The organization recurs across families, and size does not set   the chaos: across fifteen models from 0.5B to 72B, a larger model's chaos can move to higher temperature instead of   vanishing. Two loops writing one text lock each other into periodic orbits: 60% of pairs of differently steered   0.5B agents that are each chaotic alone are periodic together (twelve such orbits verified by Newton's method). In a   second system, the recurrent-depth model Huginn, a spectral estimate ranks held-out questions by the size of their   residual under relaxation, and a frozen generation test confirms the predicted accuracy loss at relaxation 1.20 and   noninferiority at 1.05.
