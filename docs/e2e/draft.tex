\section{Where the windows come from}
\label{sec:e2e}

The maps of Section~\ref{sec:maps} show three structures: a region where the loop repeats `` is it'', a fan of
stripes that accumulates on its edge, and a narrow band that crosses the stripes. This section follows one chain of
causes from the readout to these structures and to the one-humped return maps behind the chaos
(Figure~\ref{fig:e2e}). Every link is a computation on the full map; where a simpler model is used, it is compared
with the full map and the discrepancy is stated.

\paragraph{The 2-cycle is made by temperature, not inherited from greedy decoding.} At zero temperature the loop is
greedy decoding with a one-token memory, $g(t)=\arg\max_s z_s(\text{prefix},e_t)$. We computed $g$ for the tokens of
the windows over $0\le\alpha\le0.12$ (one forward pass per token and steering strength). Greedy decoding has no
`` is it'' loop anywhere in this range: after `` it'' the most probable next token is ``'s'', not `` is'', for every
$\alpha<0.105$, and after `` is'' it is `` there'' from $\alpha\approx0.064$ to $0.077$. At $\alpha=0.070$ the greedy
loops reached from these tokens are `` is there'' and ``\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe question''. The 2-cycle
exists because the fed-back token is a mixture. At $\alpha=0.070$, $\log_{10}T=-0.19$, the soft token after `` is''
mixes three nearly tied continuations, `` it'' (0.26), `` there'' (0.24) and `` a'' (0.21), and the model continues
this mixture with `` is'' (0.74) rather than ``'s'' (0.10). Continued in temperature at fixed steering, the 2-cycle is
stable only in a band of temperature (Figure~\ref{fig:e2e}a). Toward higher temperature it ends in the fold that
bounds the region, at $\log_{10}T=-0.1741065$ (from $(1-\mu)^2$ on the stable branch; the continued fold curve of
Appendix~\ref{sec:curves} passes $\alpha=0.070$ at $-0.1741065$, the same to $10^{-8}$). Toward lower temperature it
loses stability in a flip, at $\log_{10}T=$ TBD (critical multiplier real, $-1$; next modulus TBD). Below the flip the
loop sits ever closer to the tie between `` is'' and ``'s'': their probabilities are $0.72/0.28$ at the flip and
$0.50/0.50$ below $\log_{10}T=-1.1$, and the multiplier grows as the temperature falls, to $-27$ at
$\log_{10}T=-1.127$, where we stopped. The states stay far from token embeddings (relative distances 0.39 and 0.57).
The orbit does not connect to a superstable greedy loop. It is a temperature-made compromise between continuations
that greedy decoding would separate, stable only where $1/T$ is large enough to resolve the mixture and small enough
not to amplify the `` is''/``'s'' decision.

\paragraph{Beyond the fold, the ghost.} Past the fold the 2-cycle no longer exists, but trajectories pass slowly
through the bottleneck it leaves behind (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). Each passage adds laps of `` is it'' before
the loop escapes through ``. This is there is a'' and is reinjected. The number of laps fixes the period of the
window, and the passage law sets where the windows lie, accumulating on the fold as
$\alpha_N-\alpha_c\propto N^{-2}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:e2e}b). The stripes are therefore images of the fold curve: they
run parallel to it because they are counted from it.

\paragraph{The band is a doubling cascade of a different competition.} The band that crosses the stripes near
$\log_{10}T=-0.14$ is the period-6 orbit ``\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe question\textbackslash
n\textbackslash nThe existence''. On the cut $\alpha=0.070$ it is bounded above by the flip of the period-3 loop
``\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe existence'', at $\log_{10}T=-0.1392937$, where the period-6 orbit merges into
it, and below by its own flip into period 12, at $-0.1414292$ (both crossing multipliers real and equal to $-1$; next
moduli 0.20 and 0.55). It is the first doubling of the crescent's cascade (Section~\ref{sec:cascade}), continued to
larger steering. Its tokens compete at a different phase and in a different part of state space from the stripes:
the noun after ``The'' (`` existence'' against `` question'', probabilities $0.17$ and $0.14$ at $\alpha=0.070$),
against `` it'', `` there'' and `` a'' after `` is'' for the stripes. Each family's bifurcation curves are set by its
own logit gaps, so the curves of the two families cross instead of avoiding each other. Where both attractors are
stable, the loop is bistable: TBD (census).

\paragraph{Why the return maps are one-dimensional.} The derivative of the loop is
$DF=T^{-1}\Sigma_pJ_{\tilde h}$ \eqref{eq:gain}. A natural guess is that $\Sigma_p$ is nearly rank one where two tokens
compete, so that each step stretches only along $e_A-e_B$. The computation does not support that guess step by
step. Along the orbits we examined, the second eigenvalue of $\Sigma_p$ is 0.40 to 0.87 of the first (TBD over all
cells), as at the period-8 orbit of Section~\ref{sec:theory}, although the leading eigenvector is aligned with the
difference of two competing embeddings (cosine 0.77 to 1.00; for the crescent `` existence'' and `` question'',
0.94). One dimension appears in the composed loop instead. The monodromy of the period-3 loop has multipliers
$-0.608$ and $0.130$ (next modulus TBD), and the chaotic attractor next to it lies on a curve: pushed once through
the three-step return map, states interpolated along the attractor land back on it within $2\times10^{-5}$ of the
curve's length (median; at most $1.8\times10^{-3}$), and the four leading Lyapunov exponents give a Kaplan--Yorke
dimension of TBD. Read in the log-odds of the two competing nouns, $s=(z_{\rm existence}-z_{\rm question})/T$, at
the phase where they compete, the return map has a single minimum (Figure~\ref{fig:e2e}e). Along part of the
attractor $s$ is not a valid coordinate: the curve turns back in $s$ near its lower end, so a second, short branch
appears; in the arclength along the curve the map is single valued.

\paragraph{A sigmoid composed with a smooth response.} If only the share $q=p_A/(p_A+p_B)$ of the two nouns varied,
with the rest of the distribution frozen, the loop would reduce to
\begin{equation}
  q_{n+1}=\sigma\!\big(h(q_n)/T\big),\qquad h(q)=z_A-z_B\ \text{after the remaining steps of the network},
  \label{eq:e2emap}
\end{equation}
a logistic sigmoid with slope $1/T$ composed with the network's response to a blend of the two tokens. Its multiplier
at a fixed point is $q(1-q)h'(q)/T$: the readout's variance $q(1-q)$ of the competing pair, amplified by $1/T$ and by
the network's sensitivity to the blend. The sigmoid is monotone, so the hump comes from $h$: after either clean noun
the network next prefers `` existence'', after a blend it prefers `` question''. Built from the network with the rest
of the distribution frozen at a state of the chaotic attractor, \eqref{eq:e2emap} reproduces the shape of the full
return map over the attractor's range of $s$ and places the first flip of the crescent at $\alpha=0.06476$ along
$\log_{10}T=-0.156$, against $0.0644767$ in the full map (a shift of $2.9\times10^{-4}$, half of the next interval
of the cascade). It does not reproduce the cascade: its period-6 orbit stays stable to $\alpha=0.06525$, where the
full map is chaotic. The depth of the hump depends on how the rest of the distribution moves along the attractor,
which the frozen model leaves out. For the 2-cycle the competing pair at low temperature is `` is'' and ``'s'';
with it, \eqref{eq:e2emap} follows the 2-cycle's multiplier within TBD on $-0.70\le\log_{10}T\le-0.43$ and puts its
flip at $\log_{10}T=-0.5081$, against TBD in the full map. Near the fold, where three continuations compete after
`` is'', neither pair reduces the 2-cycle (with `` it''/`` there'' the reduced map is superstable, $|\mu|<10^{-3}$,
where the full orbit's multiplier is $-0.68$ to $-4.5$).

\paragraph{Why chaos shows up.} Taken together: steering moves a near-tie between continuations into the range of
temperatures where the readout neither ignores it nor decides it, $q(1-q)/T$ of order one. There the loop's response
to a blend is folded, more of one token leading back to the other, which gives a one-humped return map. The fold of
the 2-cycle and the flips of the crescent's loop are the two ways such a map loses a stable orbit, and the stripes
and the cascade are what follows them. Strong contraction in all other directions keeps the dynamics on a curve, so
the universal behavior of one-humped maps, including Feigenbaum's ratios, applies.

\paragraph{Where the windows come from (short version).} The periodic windows are temperature-made compromises
between nearly tied continuations, not inherited greedy loops: with a one-token memory, greedy decoding at the same
steering has no `` is it'' loop (after `` it'' it writes ``'s''), but at $T\approx0.67$ the fed-back blend of
`` it'', `` there'' and `` a'' is continued with `` is'', and the 2-cycle is stable between a fold at high
temperature (the edge on which the stripes accumulate, as the ghost of the fold) and a flip at low temperature,
below which the `` is''/``'s'' tie, amplified by $1/T$, destabilizes it. The band crossing the stripes is the first
doubling of a second loop, ``\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe existence'', whose nouns compete in another part
of state space, so its curves cross the stripes'. The derivative $T^{-1}\Sigma_pJ$ is not rank one at single
steps, but the composed loop contracts every direction but one: the chaotic attractor lies on a curve, and its
return map, read in the log-odds of the competing tokens, has one hump. A two-token model, a sigmoid of slope $1/T$
composed with the network's response to a blend, reproduces the hump and the first flip, but not the whole cascade.
