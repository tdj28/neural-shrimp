## Track A: period-resolved trainability maps (headline)

**Question.** In the plane of two per-layer learning rates for deterministic
full-batch gradient descent on small neural networks, do bounded
non-convergent training regimes contain verified attracting periodic orbits of
the full parameter-space update map, organized into connected isoperiodic
domains with shrimp boundary organization? If so, does that organization
predict training outcomes beyond local stability and edge-of-stability
diagnostics?

The state is the complete parameter vector; learning rates are fixed controls
during a run. A training run is the autonomous map
`theta <- theta - P grad L(theta)`, with `P` the block-diagonal learning-rate
matrix. Its Jacobian is `I - P H(theta)`, using the exact Hessian.

**Stages and gates.**

| Stage | Content | Gate to continue |

Hénon and analytic gradient-descent positive controls; agreement with the scalar reference implementation in `src/neural_shrimp/models.py`. | All tests pass; positive controls reproduce known analytic boundaries |
| A1 existence pilot (EXP-028) | Period/Lyapunov maps for a Sohl-Dickstein-style network and the earlier 13-parameter regressor; mechanically selected candidates certified by Newton refinement | At least one verified attracting primitive cycle of period >= 3 inside a connected same-period domain that borders chaotic candidates; otherwise report the null and reassess |
| A2 organization | Continuation of fold (multiplier +1) and flip (multiplier -1) curves bounding selected domains; nested refinement; basin sampling for multistability | Boundaries continued and consistent with the domain edges; only then may "verified shrimp" be used for that domain |
| A3 consequence | Prospectively frozen tests on held-out tasks and initializations. Candidates: loss of orbit-averaged iterates versus chaotic and convergent regions; survival of windows under minibatch noise; organization of the convergence boundary; predictive value of the period map beyond sharpness and local stability | Frozen endpoints and baselines, chosen before outcomes |
| A4 generality | A real-data network, the learning-rate x momentum plane, a second architecture | Separate freeze per system |

**Claim boundary.** Track A can support statements about the specified
deterministic maps, architectures, datasets, initializations, horizons,
precision and tolerances. It cannot by itself support claims about stochastic
large-scale training, latent reasoning or practical hyperparameter selection.
Finite recurrence is a candidate; certified closure plus multipliers is an
orbit; continued bifurcation boundaries are organization. Neutral directions
from symmetries or manifolds of minima must be removed (ridge or quotient
coordinates) or reported as non-hyperbolic, never thresholded away.

**Range rule.** Learning-rate ranges are set mechanically from curvature at
initialization (the layer-block Gauss-Newton maximum eigenvalue), not from
training outcomes. A coarse map is still an outcome; it can motivate a later
frozen refinement but cannot silently redefine the pilot.

## Track B: does window geometry predict computation? (companion)

**Question.** In the da Silva and Rech three-neuron network, driven by input
streams and read by learned linear readouts, does geometry (membership in the
shrimp, distance to its boundary, boundary type, location in the spiral
organization) predict memory and information processing capacity beyond local
dynamical quantities: dominant transverse Floquet multiplier, finite-time and
driven conditional Lyapunov exponents, period, and input gain?

**Circularity warning.** Distance to a bifurcation boundary and the dominant
multiplier share a mathematical factor (the multiplier reaches modulus one at
the boundary). The confirmatory question is therefore incremental prediction
**beyond** those local quantities, never geometry versus an arbitrary baseline.

| Stage | Content | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|
| B0 instrument | Driven-network simulation with unknown initial phase, linear memory capacity and degree-2 capacity with held-out input streams, consistency and conditional Lyapunov exponents. Synthetic validation on systems with known capacities | All synthetic checks pass |
| B1 maps and split | High-resolution period and Lyapunov maps of the autonomous network; spatial blocks assigned to development or held-out sets, stratified only by autonomous geometry, frozen before any capacity outcome | Split frozen and pushed |
| B2 development pilot | Capacity maps on development blocks only, including distant ordinary-periodic windows and distant chaotic regions with explicit chaos diagnostics | Exploratory; informs B3 design |
| B3 confirmatory test | Held-out blocks; incremental prediction of capacity by geometry beyond local quantities; frozen practical-effect threshold and failure criterion. An embargoed OSF registration is optional and requires human sign-off | Single frozen analysis |
| B4 generality | A second independently chosen recurrent-network family | Separate freeze |

**Failure criterion for specificity.** An effect reproduced by distant
ordinary-periodic or chaotic controls, or explained by local quantities at the
frozen threshold, is generic. Adjacent periodic/irregular contrasts alone
cannot establish a shrimp-specific contribution.
