# Analytic addendum: perfect scalar agreement does not establish a one-dimensional conjugacy

Let f(u)=4u(1−u) on [0,1], let F(x,y)=(f(x),f(y)), and observe only p(x,y)=x. Then p∘F=f∘p exactly: the observed return relation is perfectly single valued and unimodal, with a quadratic maximum. Under the product of the standard logistic invariant measures, both full-system Lyapunov exponents equal ln 2, while the observed scalar exponent also equals ln 2. Thus the scalar exponent agrees exactly with the full system's largest exponent even though there are two expanding dimensions and the observation discards y. This is an analytic example, not a target computation: the logistic measure can be obtained from u=sin²(πθ), and ∫₀¹ log|4−8u|/[π√(u(1−u))] du=ln 2. It demonstrates that even zero fit error plus perfect leading-exponent agreement would establish neither an injective coordinate nor an invariant curve for the Qwen trajectory. Such agreement remains useful empirical evidence for a scalar factor or approximation, with its actual finite-record scope.
