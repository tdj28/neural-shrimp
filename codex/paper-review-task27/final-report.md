## Suggested abstract

Feeding a pretrained language model the expected embedding of its next-token distribution defines a continuous feedback map. We study this map with a fixed prefix and position in released Qwen, OLMo and Llama models spanning 0.5B to 72B parameters. Temperature, steering and prompt change the observed prevalence of periodic behavior and positive finite-time Lyapunov estimates. In selected long-loop panels, batch-layout perturbations frequently separate trajectories classified as chaotic and change their argmax readouts, while most periodic trajectories remain close. In separate frozen 96-problem arithmetic tests, batch layout changes approximately 56% and 55% of bfloat16 soft-thinking answers in 0.5B and 7B models; these are finite pipeline comparisons with a specified forced-decoding baseline. An exact derivative factorization separates network and softmax-readout contributions. Fixed-trajectory crossover comparisons support a positive conditional readout contribution, with contrast-dependent network effects and retained failures of the original sufficiency criteria. Continued fold and flip curves, numerically located cusps and finite period-doubling sequences reveal low-dimensional organization within the full transformer state. Prospective normal-form tests predict many local passage and coexistence outcomes, with failed all-row criteria reported. Interval arithmetic certifies one locally attracting primitive period-8 orbit of the exact feedback map. In Huginn, an independently reproduced saved-data analysis links estimated spectral features to finite-depth residual growth, and a frozen relaxation test satisfies its decline and noninferiority criteria. The results characterize constructed continuous feedback systems and their finite numerical and behavioral consequences; they do not establish a universal accuracy advantage or describe ordinary sampled generation.
