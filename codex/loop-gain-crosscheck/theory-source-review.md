## Task 18

The factorization is correct for the stated fixed-prefix, fixed-parameter expected-embedding map. With input embeddings E, tied output weights E, final normalized hidden state h(x), and p=softmax(Eh/T), the chain rule gives DF=Eᵀ(diag(p)−ppᵀ)E Jh/T. The temperature and steering/prefix are held fixed in this state derivative. The hidden map must include final normalization. An output bias that is constant in x changes p but not the derivative formula; producer rejection of all output biases is stronger than necessary, but immaterial for the requested unbiased model. For untied weights W, the corresponding factor is Eᵀ(diag(p)−ppᵀ)W, generally not a symmetric positive covariance.

The original producer implementation checks tied storage, takes forward-mode columns in the correct orientation, and left-multiplies successive step matrices to form the return derivative. Its identity error is an Euclidean relative directional error, not an elementwise relative-error maximum. Source alone does not establish the reported error or eigenvalues. Independent computation should preserve all eight directional errors, all eight step spectra, full closure, signed multipliers, and exact phase ordering. Compare the reported monodromy norm only after matching cyclic phase: nonzero multipliers are unchanged by cyclic phase, but Euclidean singular values need not be. A tiny second monodromy singular value describes local linear compression at that return phase, not a proved one-dimensional invariant nonlinear attractor.

Paper lines 273–283 are substantially sound if the actual numbers reproduce: every step has at least one expanding direction while the composition contracts. The contribution sentence at lines 108–109 should retain “expands some direction”; “every step expands” alone can suggest uniform expansion. “Stability cannot be read from the steps” is too absolute: uniform one-step contraction can suffice, and the ordered product is computed from the step derivatives. Prefer “The individual step norms do not determine stability here; their ordered composition does.” The second quoted multiplier should retain its negative sign, or both values should explicitly be called moduli. Source documentation saying covariance rank is below the number of probability-carrying tokens is exact only for actual support: finite-temperature softmax has full positive support. Sharp probabilities imply approximate, not literal, rank deficiency.

**Material correction: low temperature.** The paper's lines 95–107 and 256–265 currently promote a pointwise asymptotic to a global exclusion of low-temperature chaos. At a fixed state with a positive largest-logit gap Δ, bounded weights and Jh do give gain O(exp(−Δ/T)/T). This is not uniform near ties or along an attractor that moves with T. Greedy T=0 dynamics has finite-image eventual periodicity after a deterministic tie rule, but that discontinuous limit does not rule out chaos at every sufficiently small positive T. A uniform positive-gap condition on the relevant forward-invariant sets would support the stronger conclusion. Without it, say low-temperature suppression is expected away from ties and supported on the sampled sweeps. High-temperature contraction is sound for fixed steering/prefix: compact embedding hull, bounded continuous Jh, bounded embedding covariance/cross-covariance, and the 1/T factor give a sufficiently-large-T contraction. The finite sweep does not identify that global threshold.

**Hopfield / general positive-semidefinite derivative argument.** The useful claim can be retained with explicit hypotheses and a short derivation. If F is C¹ on a convex invariant set K, DF is symmetric positive semidefinite there, and K is compact, then F=∇Φ for a convex potential on K (or its affine span). Define H(x)=||x||²/2−Φ(x). Convexity with y=F(x) gives H(y)−H(x)≤−||y−x||²/2. Thus no nonfixed periodic orbit exists; every accumulation point of a forward orbit is fixed. This excludes a nontrivial recurrent chaotic attractor in this setting. It does not by itself prove uniqueness, convergence to one point when there is a continuum of fixed points, or absence of a positive transverse exponent at an unstable fixed point. The particular identity-hidden-map Hopfield connection is directly supported by Ramsauer et al., [Hopfield Networks is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217), update Eq. (3) and the energy argument; do not confuse energy convergence with unconditional uniqueness of the state limit.

**Single-valued prediction is not established smooth conjugacy.** The paper lines 1242–1245 and `return_maps.py` lines 12–13 state that all low-residual phases must have the same exponent. Even exact scalar closure p_{n+1}=g(p_n) can be a many-to-one factor that discards an unstable direction. Low residual alone also does not prove exact closure. A sufficient one-dimensional coordinate statement would require an appropriate invertible smooth observable with controlled derivative/inverse along the invariant dynamics. In one dimension, g=φ∘f∘φ⁻¹ implies the finite-time exponent difference [log|φ′(x_N)|−log|φ′(x_0)|]/N; it vanishes under the required sublinear boundary-term condition, not necessarily over these short samples. The standard diffeomorphism result explicitly uses nonsingular, bounded coordinate Jacobians and inverses; see [Kuznetsov, Alexeeva and Leonov, section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.2016). Here the phase median is a reasonable descriptive aggregate; agreement among phases and with twins is evidence consistent with a scalar approximation, not a consequence already guaranteed by a theorem.

**The current CV objective does not validate derivative exponents.** Source lines 63–81 randomly split adjacent return pairs into five folds, choose degree by the same CV squared-value errors, then compute mean log|g′| on an all-data refit. This is a data-interpolation check on dependent samples, not an independent forecasting test; selecting and reporting the same minimum CV error can be optimistic. A small value error need not imply a small derivative error, especially near a turning point where log|g′| is sensitive. Record the complete degree/fold and per-phase results, derivative/exponent sensitivity, short-sample counts, and invalid/undefined cases. A separate blocked or contiguous held-out comparison is a useful bounded sensitivity check; it should be fixed before inspecting the independent reducer's outcomes and retained even if less favorable. No interval proof or model replay of all 62 cells is needed for the descriptive claim.

**One critical point is not necessarily one quadratic maximum.** `return_maps.py` lines 40, 80 and 126 count real derivative roots without checking curvature or change from positive to negative derivative. Consequently “59 cells have a phase whose map has a single interior maximum” is stronger than this code establishes. Check the derivative signs and negative nonzero curvature, or call it one interior stationary point of the fitted polynomial. Even a successful fitted check establishes the polynomial's property over the sampled range, not the exact return map's global unimodality or Feigenbaum universality. The single-cell plotting source does save curvature at fitted roots, which supports the narrower fitted-quadratic-maximum wording if the raw values are authenticated.
