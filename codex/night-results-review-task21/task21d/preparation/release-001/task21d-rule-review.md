## Chronology and interpretation

The 02:24 run was explicitly exploratory and selected candidate lines using existing maps. It froze a 10% constancy criterion over at least four windows and a free lap offset, but left selection and spread details open. The 03:03 entry, before the new line results according to the log, fixed maximal runs of at least two periodic cells, neighbouring-half-period exclusion, nearest run per period, fold-side grid edges, and `(max−min)/mean` spread. It also added the bound δ≤1, while preserving the original free-fit result. These are prospective operational refinements relative to the new line results, not a claim of a wholly fresh, map-blind confirmatory experiment.

The 03:27 entry explicitly says the selector was changed after the first scoring. The first implementation stopped when an intervening window lay outside the period progression. The amendment instead starts at the nearest retained window and follows p,p−q,p−2q,… whenever the next period exists farther from the fold, skipping other periods. That is a principled progression-based selector: it maintains a fixed step q and cannot choose an arbitrary favourable subsequence. However, the terse earlier phrase “from the fold outward, as long as their periods step by q” also admits a stopping interpretation. Calling the amendment a correction to the intended progression is reasonable; claiming the whole implemented selector was fixed before outcomes is not. The log says nearest-four scores are unchanged and only farther windows are added. The independent audit should check that statement, not assume it.

The δ≤1 rationale is conditional on the proposed lap decomposition: x=p/q=N+k, k≥0, and a residual lap offset less than one. It is a useful pre-line restriction against an unconstrained offset mimicking a different power over a short range. It is not a theorem that any empirical reinjection/period decomposition must satisfy this bound. The free fit is not literally unrestricted: the source searches only δ≤10. Both searches must be reported under their actual domains. The source's toy example itself shows why a four-window fit alone does not uniquely identify an inverse-square exponent or prove the fold mechanism.
