## Why matrix-free first, then chord reuse

The root supplied an existing measured P6 dense cost: 2472 seconds for 896 columns at one CPU thread. That is an arithmetic average 2.759 seconds per column, not a measured scalar-JVP throughput, not a six-thread speedup and not a launch estimate. At that historic rate, one 192-column Newton cap corresponds to roughly 530 seconds versus 2472 seconds for a single initial dense Jacobian. A successful matrix-free solve may require far fewer than 192 columns; a difficult nonnormal solve may exhaust the cap. Two R spectra cost about 4944 seconds at the old rate; six spectra (R plus both body points for both orbits) cost about 14832 seconds, before residuals, eigensystems, gates, I/O and setup. Six fully consumed Newton caps add about 3178 seconds under the same crude scaling. Fresh first-unit timing is required before committing to the full sequence.

Dense-chord from the outset costs an initial 896-column matrix **plus** a final one for each R seed. That is wasteful if short GMRES works and should be a separately disclosed fallback only after measured matrix-free failure. At body points, a final matrix already exists from the preceding point, so chord reuse has no additional matrix-construction cost and is attractive. No target dense calculation is launched by this package.
