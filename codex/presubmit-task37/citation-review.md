### F1. Scope the absence of an energy guarantee to the feedback operators studied here [should-fix]

Where: `main.tex`, Discussion, “Beyond the one-token loop,” lines ~1180–1182 at `18490e2`.

Claim: “Feedback creates a dynamical system but does not by itself imply convergence or chaos: energy-based guarantees of convergence require specific structure and update rules, as for the tied readout of Sec. […] , and none is established for a complete transformer update.”

Evidence: In its grammatical context, “none” refers to **energy-based** convergence guarantees, not to every possible convergence theorem. The article proves energy descent only after replacing the network with the identity (`main.tex` ~363–374); composition with the full transformer is different. That limited point is sound. The unqualified literature-absence formulation is broader than the evidence supplied. [Hoover et al., Energy Transformer, NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/57a9b97477b67936298489e3c1417b0a-Abstract-Conference.html), deliberately constructs a recurrent block containing normalization, attention and an MLP analog from an energy function. Its [full text, Sec. 2, Eqs. (6)–(8)](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.07253v2#S2), proves non-increasing energy for its continuous-time dynamics; the numerical implementation discretizes those dynamics. The construction changes conventional attention, ties MLP weights and applies the attention and MLP analogs in parallel. These are **not** convergence guarantees for arbitrary discrete standard-transformer updates or the Qwen/Huginn operators here. They do make “a complete transformer update” ambiguous enough to warrant narrowing. This audit has not proved the absence of all applicable literature.

Correct value / wording: “Feedback creates a dynamical system but does not by itself imply convergence or chaos. The energy-descent argument for the isolated tied readout in Sec. […] does not establish convergence of either complete feedback operator studied here.” If retaining a literature contrast, distinguish deliberately energy-based architectures from these released pretrained operators. Do not describe Energy Transformer as a general discrete-time convergence theorem.

### F2. Tie the safety citation to CoT monitorability rather than latent-state monitoring generally [should-fix]

Where: `main.tex`, Discussion, “Steering and predictability,” lines ~1172–1175 at `18490e2`.

Claim: “These findings motivate testing whether interventions in continuous-feedback systems, including steering for safety, alter finite-horizon sensitivity or task-relevant decisions as well as immediate outputs, alongside the monitoring of latent computation \cite{korbak2025monitorability}. We tested no safety property: dynamical instability is neither misalignment nor evidence of it.”

Evidence: [Korbak et al., arXiv:2507.11473v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11473v2), is a position paper about monitoring natural-language chains of thought. Section 2 (“Novel architectures”) and Section 3 (“What types of architectures reduce monitorability of CoTs?”) discuss the loss of visibility when reasoning occurs in continuous latent spaces and propose studying when some visibility survives. It therefore supports a motivation about **monitorability under architectural changes**, but does not establish a general method or effectiveness result for monitoring continuous latent computation. The article's phrases “motivate testing whether” and “We tested no safety property” correctly separate a proposed experiment from evidence. There is no demonstrated claim here that instability is misalignment, that steering improves safety, or that safety interventions were tested. The fix is citation precision, not withdrawal of the proposed research question.

Correct value / wording: “These findings motivate testing whether interventions in continuous-feedback systems, including steering for safety, alter finite-horizon sensitivity or task-relevant decisions as well as immediate outputs. Such tests would complement research on how latent-reasoning architectures affect chain-of-thought monitorability \cite{korbak2025monitorability}. We tested no safety property: dynamical instability is neither misalignment nor evidence of it.”

## Completed architecture check

Where: Introduction, first paragraph, lines ~89–93 at `18490e2`.

Claim: “This fixed-prefix, fixed-window soft-token map differs both from ordinary autoregressive generation, whose context grows, and from recurrent-depth (looped) transformers, which repeatedly update hidden states through a shared block […] ; dynamical methods apply to both kinds of loop, but their update operators, and therefore their derivatives and stability conditions, differ.”

Assessment: Supported; no correction required. The article's own map definition (~168–184) fixes the prefix, positions and one-token input state, and explicitly limits the greedy-decoding analogy (~178–180) to that fixed-window system. [Universal Transformers, Sec. 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.03819v3#S2.SS1), shares transition weights across recurrent depth steps. [Giannou et al., ICML 2023, Sec. 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/giannou23a/giannou23a.pdf), repeatedly applies a frozen transformer to its sequence/state. [Geiping et al., Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v2#S3.SS1), explicitly has `s_i = R(e, s_(i-1))`, with the coda producing probabilities only after the recurrent steps. Thus the readout is not inside that depth recurrence. The different derivative/stability operators are a mathematical inference from these different recurrences, not an empirical result attributed to the cited papers.

Qualification: Universal Transformers includes step embeddings and optional adaptive halting. Shared parameters alone do not make every cited model an autonomous recurrence on hidden states without augmenting the state or fixing those inputs. The reviewed sentence says dynamical methods apply, not that every cited architecture is identical to this autonomous map, so this is a scope note rather than an error. The distinction also does not say recurrent-depth models cannot generate autoregressively; they can combine the inner depth loop with an outer generation loop.
