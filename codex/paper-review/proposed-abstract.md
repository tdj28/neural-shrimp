> What dynamical structures organize repeated inference in a pretrained language model? We study a precisely defined autonomous system: the expected next-token embedding of a frozen Qwen model is fed back at a fixed position after a fixed prefix. Its temperature–steering plane contains periodic text loops among numerically chaotic regimes. Numerical continuation links a period-adding family to the saddle-node of a primitive two-cycle. Derivatives of this fold predict controlled bottleneck passages without fitting; at two independently computed folds, 23 of 24 prospectively frozen forecasts are accurate within one iterate, failing the all-row criterion. Fold and flip curves reveal candidate shrimp organization, while superstable points through period 384 yield finite-order ratios approaching Feigenbaum's constant. Related dynamics occur at larger model sizes and in the native recurrence of Huginn, where spectral estimates predict relaxation responses in development experiments. In a separate sampled-dialogue setting, hostility steering produces dose-dependent persistence and history dependence. Replacing classifier-flagged replies before they enter the context reduces subsequent original-classifier hostility with substantially less steering dose than constant prevention. A frozen confirmation finds a small increment in advance warning from the model's in-context state, while finite Monte Carlo committor estimates leave the attainable warning ceiling unresolved. These results connect explicit inference dynamics to testable mechanisms and practical control experiments without identifying the constructed map with ordinary autoregressive generation.
