## Attribution and proposed paper correction

The experimental variable is batch layout for a specified implementation, budget and task set. The primary operational effect, if independently reproduced, does not require a chaos explanation. The float64 perturbation subset covers six problem instances, three fixed perturbation sizes and 31 distance-growth transitions. Its observed signs and answer changes can support a finite contrast with bf16; they cannot categorically exclude every dynamical contribution to low-precision sensitivity or establish that argmax generally absorbs rounding error.

In addition, this growing-context, cache-dependent computation is not the autonomous one-soft-slot map from which the temperatures' “nominally attracting/expanding” labels were selected (design lines 13 and 17). Those labels do not classify the arithmetic-prompt pipeline's regime. The near-zero float64 layout discrepancies also do not establish asymptotic stability.

Replace the categorical attribution in paper excerpt lines 35–38 and log line 37 with wording of this form, after the saved numerical results pass the independent replay:

> In the fixed 32-step growing-context pipeline, the bf16 answers were sensitive to batch layout, while the specified greedy-plus-cue comparator was reproducible. The float64 perturbation subset showed only the reported finite-horizon growth and no observed answer changes at the tested sizes. This supports an implementation-level finite-precision sensitivity result; it does not establish that the effect is the chaos of the autonomous feedback map. The fixed greedy comparator does not measure ordinary early-stopped greedy accuracy.

These are source and claim-scope corrections. No additional model experiment, general proof or producer-code rerun is required to audit the saved practical-consequence result.
