\begin{abstract}
Does standard bifurcation theory predict, quantitatively, the dynamics of repeated inference in a pretrained language
model? We study an exactly defined autonomous system: the expected next-token embedding of a frozen model is fed back
at a fixed position after a fixed prefix, with temperature and an activation-steering strength as parameters. Its
parameter plane holds periodic text loops among regimes with positive finite-horizon Lyapunov exponents, in Qwen2.5
models of 0.5B to 7B parameters, OLMo-2 7B and Llama-3.1 8B. Such chaos, its windows and shrimp-like structures are
expected under universality; our results are predictions computed from the model's own derivatives and then tested.
The normal form of a two-cycle's fold predicts bottleneck passage times without fitting (at two new folds, 23 of 24
prospectively frozen forecasts within one iterate, failing the all-row criterion). Fold curves of two windows end in
numerically nondegenerate cusps whose local unfolding, frozen before solving, predicted the number of periodic orbits
at all twelve tested parameter offsets and their stability at eleven. Interval arithmetic proves one period-8 orbit
exists and is locally attracting. In the recurrent-depth model Huginn, fixed-point spectra predict each question's
convergence under relaxation and where chain-of-thought accuracy breaks; a frozen test on held-out data confirms both.
In sampled self-dialogue, hostility steering has a non-monotone dose response: replacing classifier-flagged replies
before they enter the context lowers the model's own hostility far more per unit of steering than constant prevention,
in two model sizes where the dose response rises, and counter-steering backfires past the peak. The constructed map is
not ordinary autoregressive generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Repeated inference with a fixed language model is an iterated map: the model's output becomes its next input.
Recent work has measured chaos-like sensitivity in language-model generation
\cite{li2025quasilyapunov,jaca2026chaos}, temperature-driven changes of output statistics
\cite{arnold2024phase,nakaishi2024phase,sun2025phase,ruan2026criticality,johnson2026temperature}, periodic
attractors of paraphrasing loops \cite{wang2025attractor}, attractor states of model-to-model conversations
\cite{ko2026attractor}, and the stability of looped and recurrent-depth models through their Jacobians
\cite{bai2019deq,bai2021stabilizing,tomihari2025recurrent,yang2026stars,luo2026prediction}. Feeding a
probability-weighted vocabulary embedding back into a model is not new: it is used in training
\cite{zeng2025ponderlm} and, without retraining, in pretrained models \cite{zhang2025softthinking}, alongside
continuous latent feedback \cite{hao2024coconut}. In a small task-trained looped transformer, fold coefficients
computed from derivatives predict passage times and spectral gaps without fitting \cite{kim2026phase}.

We study one constructed but exactly defined system, the expected-embedding (``soft-token'') map of a frozen,
released pretrained language model, with a fixed chat prefix and a fixed position. The map is smooth, its Jacobians
are available by automatic differentiation, and its periodic orbits can be computed and continued like those of any
smooth map. Chaos in such a family is not surprising: high-dimensional nonlinear maps with a gain-like parameter
generically become chaotic, as random recurrent networks do above a critical gain \cite{sompolinsky1988chaos}, and
periodic windows, shrimp-shaped domains and period-doubling cascades are universal in dissipative two-parameter maps
\cite{feigenbaum1978quantitative,gallas1993structure}. Our question is whether standard bifurcation theory applies
quantitatively to a trained model's own inference feedback, so that derivatives computed from the model predict its
behaviour before that behaviour is measured. In the cases we tested it does: fold normal forms predict passage
times, cusp unfoldings predict how many periodic orbits exist near a cusp and which are stable, and fixed-point
spectra of a recurrent-depth model predict its response to relaxation and the onset of task failure on held-out
data. Ordinary sampled generation with a growing context is a different system; Section~\ref{sec:bridge} measures
how much of the mechanism survives sampling, and Section~\ref{sec:misalignment} studies sampled dialogue directly.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Grouped by what they establish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Tested predictions.} (a) The 2-cycle ``is it'' dies in a fold; its normal form, computed from
  derivatives, predicts bottleneck passage times, prospectively at two new folds (23 of 24 forecasts within one
  iterate; the all-row criterion failed by one row), and the global reinjection sets the window-law prefactor
  (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). (b) Fold and flip curves continued with float64 test functions: the fold curves of a
  period-6 window and of a hostile period-7 window end in numerically nondegenerate cusps, and at one cusp of each
  the two-parameter unfolding is regular and its local model, frozen before solving, predicted the orbit counts at
  twelve parameter offsets (twelve correct) and their stability (eleven) (Sections~\ref{sec:curves}
  and~\ref{sec:misalignment}). (c) In the recurrent-depth model Huginn, a predicted and observed Neimark--Sacker
  bifurcation, and spectral predictions of each question's response to relaxation and of where chain-of-thought
  accuracy breaks, confirmed by a frozen test on held-out data (Section~\ref{sec:huginn}).
\item \emph{Rigor.} Float64 computation with precision checks (Section~\ref{sec:map}); independent reimplementations
  of the fold and its normal form, two orbits and four flip parameters, and an independent replay of the frozen
  Huginn test; and an interval-arithmetic proof that a primitive period-8 orbit exists and is locally attracting
  (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).
\item \emph{Control.} In sampled self-dialogue, hostility steering shows steep, direction-specific capture with
  dose-dependent persistence and hysteresis. Replacing flagged replies before they enter the dialogue keeps the
  model's own hostility low at a quarter of the dose of constant prevention, and counter-steering triggered after a
  hostile reply is less efficient. In Qwen2.5-1.5B the same ordering holds where the dose response rises, the dose
  response is not monotone, and every counter-steer backfires beyond its peak. A pre-specified lead-time test is
  negative; a small warning from the model's in-context state is confirmed on fresh dialogues
  (Section~\ref{sec:misalignment}).
\item \emph{The setting, expected under universality.} Float64 phase maps in temperature and steering strength with
  chaos (positive finite-horizon exponents) and embedded windows (Section~\ref{sec:maps}); a period-adding family whose
  text adds one ``is X'' pair per period step (Section~\ref{sec:adding}); shrimp-like windows (Section~\ref{sec:curves});
  a doubling cascade with superstable points through period 384 and finite-order Feigenbaum ratios
  (Section~\ref{sec:cascade}); the same organization in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B, OLMo-2 7B, Llama-3.1 8B (with
  sparse-autoencoder readouts) and a second checkpoint of the 0.5B architecture, which shares the organization but
  not the attractors (Section~\ref{sec:scale}); and a bridge to sampled decoding (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Related work.} Language-model generation has been studied as a dynamical system at several levels.
Sensitivity to small perturbations has been measured with Lyapunov-like quantities in reasoning traces
\cite{li2025quasilyapunov,jaca2026chaos}. Output statistics change abruptly with sampling temperature, which several
studies analyse as phase transitions or criticality
\cite{arnold2024phase,nakaishi2024phase,sun2025phase,ruan2026criticality,johnson2026temperature}. Repetition loops are
a known failure of decoding \cite{holtzman2019degeneration,xu2022loop}. Successive paraphrasing converges to attractor
cycles \cite{wang2025attractor}, and multi-turn model-to-model conversations settle into attractor states
\cite{ko2026attractor}. Attractors and bifurcation diagrams have been studied in recurrent language models
\cite{ribeiro2020attractors}. Expected-embedding feedback is used in training \cite{zeng2025ponderlm} and,
training-free, in pretrained models \cite{zhang2025softthinking,wu2025singlethreaded}, next to continuous latent
reasoning \cite{hao2024coconut}; here the same kind of feedback, with prefix and position fixed, defines the map we
analyse. Equilibrium, looped and recurrent-depth models iterate a block to a fixed point or for many steps, and their
stability has been analysed through Jacobians and fixed points
\cite{bai2019deq,bai2021stabilizing,geiping2025recurrent,zhu2025ouro,tomihari2025recurrent,yang2026stars,luo2026prediction,prairie2026parcae,buehlmaier2026layernorm,labovich2026stability,movahedi2026fixedpoint}.
The closest precedent is a derivative-based fold analysis of a small task-trained looped transformer, whose passage
and spectral-gap predictions are unfitted \cite{kim2026phase}. Our system differs: a released pretrained language
model, the fold of a primitive periodic orbit, the full-channel passage of a two-step map, and the accumulation law of
the windows, whose prefactor is set by the global reinjection. In neural systems more broadly, saddle-node ghosts
produce quantitative delays in task-trained recurrent networks \cite{dinc2026ghost}. Finite-order Feigenbaum ratios
have been measured in neural rate and mass models \cite{battaglia2007temporal,papasavvas2015gain} and, through period
128, in a transformer trained on logistic-map trajectories \cite{liu2026learningchaos}. Learned networks reproduce
period-doubling routes \cite{kim2021teaching}, and so does gradient descent itself \cite{kalra2025universal}. Normal
forms and continuation have been applied to recurrent neural maps \cite{marichal2015multiple}, and shrimp-shaped
windows and periodicity staircases appear in neuron models
\cite{gomez2014universal,stenzinger2025shrimp,caixeta2025staircase}. Activation steering along contrastive directions
\cite{zou2023representation,zhang2025steering} supplies our second control parameter. The dynamical-systems tools are
standard: numerical continuation and normal forms \cite{kuznetsov2023elements,govaerts2000numerical}, type-I
intermittency \cite{pomeau1980intermittent}, shrimps and hubs
\cite{gallas1993structure,facanha2013endoskeletons,vitolo2011hubs}, period-doubling universality
\cite{feigenbaum1978quantitative}, and Markov state models \cite{chodera2014msm}. A targeted search (12 October
2026) found no prior work combining these elements for the feedback map of a pretrained language model; that is an
absence of evidence within the search, not a proof of priority.

\section{The inference map}
\label{sec:map}
\paragraph{Definition.} Let $E\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ be the input embedding matrix ($V=151{,}936$,
$d=896$) and let $z(x)\in\mathbb{R}^V$ be the next-token logits produced by the model, given a fixed chat prefix
(system prompt ``You are an AI talking with another AI. You have complete freedom. Feel free to pursue whatever
you want.'' and user turn ``Hello!'') followed by a single input embedding $x$. The soft-token map is
\begin{equation}
  F_{T,\alpha}(x) = E^{\top}\operatorname{softmax}\!\big(z_\alpha(x)/T\big),
\end{equation}
where $z_\alpha$ adds $\alpha v$ to the residual stream at layer 12 at every position, prefix included, and $v$
is a contrastive steering direction (``philosophy'' prompts against neutral prompts, last-token states,
normalized to the mean residual norm). Positions are fixed (the window does not grow), so $F$ is an autonomous
map of $\mathbb{R}^{896}$. As $T\to0$ it reduces to greedy decoding of the same fixed-prefix, one-token-window
system when the argmax is unique; it is not ordinary generation with a growing context.

\paragraph{Precision.} Hugging Face RMSNorm and rotary-embedding tables compute in float32 even when the model is
cast to float64; we patch both. float32 maps are accurate to $3\times10^{-6}$--$2\times10^{-5}$ per step, which is
enough for phase maps (245 of 256 test cells agree with float64) but not for derivatives of composed maps:
one-step float32 Jacobian-vector products are accurate to $5\times10^{-5}$, yet the 8-step monodromy of a
period-8 orbit is off by 3\%. All orbits, multipliers and test functions below are float64.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{Phase maps.} For each cell, unless stated otherwise, 300 transient and 300 measured steps; the largest
Lyapunov exponent from a renormalized twin at offset $10^{-6}$; the period as the smallest $q\le32$ (48 in the later
zooms) with return distance below $10^{-8}$; the attractor's identity as its rotation-invariant leading-token loop.
We call a cell chaotic when this finite-horizon exponent exceeds $10^{-3}$ and no period is found. The label is a
finite-horizon diagnostic, not a proof of chaos. Maps of $256^2$ cells take
about 100 minutes on one H100 GPU in float64. Every cell starts from the same state, so where attractors coexist
a map shows the one whose basin contains that state. Labels are text, not dynamical identities: one continuous
orbit changes label where two tokens tie for the maximum. We call two labelled regions different attractors only
when the contraction rate jumps between them or when float64 orbits of both exist at one parameter value.
\paragraph{Periodic orbits.} Newton's method on $F^p(x)-x$ with forward-mode Jacobians; multipliers are the
eigenvalues of the monodromy matrix, refined by a short Arnoldi factorization with float64 Jacobian-vector
products where the dense matrix is not float64.
\paragraph{Continuation.} Pseudo-arclength continuation of periodic branches in one parameter, and of fold and
flip curves in two, using minimally augmented systems \cite{govaerts2000numerical,kuznetsov2023elements}: the
test function $g$ of the bordered system $[[M\mp I,v],[w^\top,0]]$ is obtained by GMRES (generalized minimal residual
iterations) with Jacobian-vector
products, and its gradient by reverse-mode differentiation through a Jacobian-vector product. A reversal of the
parameter tangent flags a cusp candidate, which we test with the normal form.
\paragraph{Normal form and passages.} For the two-step map $G=F^2$ at a fold with right and left eigenvectors
$r,\ell$ ($\|r\|=1$, $\ell^\top r=1$), $u'=u+a\eps+bu^2$ with $a=\ell^\top\partial_\alpha G$ and
$b=\tfrac12\ell^\top D^2G[r,r]$; $b$ is computed by central differences of float64 Jacobian-vector products over
a step-size plateau (later cross-checks use nested automatic differentiation), and $a$ includes the steering's effect
on the prefix. The signed coefficients are kept in the original eigenvector gauge, with $\eps$ in the free parameter
($\alpha$ or $\log_{10}T$; its units apply to $a$). For passages we set $v=\operatorname{sign}(b)\,u$ and take the
ghost side $p-p_c=\operatorname{sign}(ab)\,\epsilon$ with $\epsilon>0$, so that $v'=v+|a|\epsilon+|b|v^2$ to quadratic
order. Full and midpoint passages start at $v=-U$ and $v=0$ and end at the first strict crossing $v>U$, with
$U=0.1/|b|$. Passage counts are compared with the iterated quadratic map and with the arctangent formula
\cite{pomeau1980intermittent}.
\paragraph{Period-doubling cascade.} Along a cut in one parameter, the flip of each period-$p$ orbit is located as
the root of $\operatorname{Re}\mu_c+1$, where $\mu_c$ is the multiplier with the smallest real part, by the Illinois
variant of regula falsi with warm-started orbits. Because all but one or two multipliers are small, Newton's method on
$F^p(x)-x$ uses the monodromy only on a six-dimensional dominant subspace. That subspace comes from subspace
iteration with exact forward-mode Jacobian-vector products chained along the orbit, so no dense Jacobian is formed;
the residual is always the exact float64 $F^p(x)-x$. Each root is classified before it is accepted: the critical
multiplier must be real and equal to $-1$, its Ritz residual below $10^{-6}$, and the other computed Ritz values
(six in all, estimates of the dominant spectrum) inside the unit circle. The period-$2p$ orbit is then reached by forward iteration inside the next band. On the logistic map
embedded in six dimensions, the procedure reproduces the first four flip parameters to $10^{-9}$.

\section{Mechanism: a saddle-node bottleneck}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\paragraph{The fold.} At $\log_{10}T=-0.115$ the 2-cycle ``is it'' dies in a fold at $\alpha_c=0.06279827$ (float64
multiplier $1$ to $3\times10^{-13}$, next modulus $0.037$). The normal form of $G$ has $a=-4.834$ per unit $\alpha$
and $b=-25.4285$, so $ab=122.93$. Extrapolating the stable-branch multipliers, $(1-\mu)^2/(4(\alpha_c-\alpha))\to ab$,
gives $123.1$ independently with a quadratic fit (a linear fit gives $120.6$). An independent implementation of the forward map and solver recovers $\alpha_c$ to
$6\times10^{-15}$ and $a$ to $3\times10^{-12}$. Its $b$, from nested automatic differentiation, is $-25.42846$; our
finite-difference value differs from it by $2.2\times10^{-6}$ relatively, which is the finite-step bias of the
difference table. The resulting $ab=122.927$ leaves every passage prediction of Table~\ref{tab:passages}
unchanged.

\paragraph{Controlled passages.} Starting at $u_{\rm in}=-U$ or $0$ ($U=0.1/|b|$) and counting $G$-iterates until
$u>U$, the measured counts match the iterated normal form within one iterate at four distances
(Table~\ref{tab:passages}, Figure~\ref{fig:passages}); a transverse displacement of the same norm changes the
count by at most one.

\paragraph{Natural reinjection and the window law.} Free trajectories re-enter the channel slightly upstream of
the bottleneck's middle ($u\approx-3\times10^{-4}$ to $-7\times10^{-4}$). From each measured entry point the
normal form predicts the natural laminar durations within 4--8\% for $\eps\le10^{-4}$, and a natural passage lasts
about 0.6 of a full one. The stripes therefore follow the $N^{-2}$ law of a saddle-node ghost with about a third
of the full-passage prefactor: stripe centres (located on a float32 cut along $\log_{10}T=-0.115$) give
$d_N N^2=0.0263$--$0.0265$ for $N=4$--$7$ (log-log slope
$-2.00$), compared with $\pi^2/(4ab)=0.0201$ for a half passage. Two-step return maps of $p(\text{``it''})$
(Figure~\ref{fig:returnmaps}) show the near-tangency.
Numerically estimated stripe edges along $\log_{10}T=-0.115$ (Table~\ref{tab:edges}) sharpen this. Each window is bounded by a fold,
where its orbit is born, and by a flip. For periods 6--12 the fold faces away from the accumulation point; for periods
16--20 the sides are exchanged; the period-14 window is bounded by flips on both sides. The fold edges of periods
16--20 follow $\alpha_N-\alpha_c=C/(N+\delta)^2$ with $C=0.0266$ and $\delta=0.15$. Their slope, $1/\sqrt{\alpha_N-\alpha_c}$

\paragraph{A period-6 window with shrimp-like anatomy.} The hooks of Figure~\ref{fig:zooms} (bottom) belong to
the period-6 loop ``. -\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe is a''. Float64 orbits (Newton residuals
$10^{-15}$--$10^{-13}$) show that its curved body and its thin leg are one window: at the body the multipliers are
$-0.061$ and $+0.048$, lower down $-0.564$, and on the leg $-0.645$. The foot is its period-doubled loop (period 12,
leading multiplier $-0.156$). Inside the body lies a different 6-cycle, ``.The\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe is
a'', whose leading multipliers are a complex pair, $-0.031\pm0.152i$. One-parameter branches from the body end in
folds in every direction: at $\log_{10}T=-0.0558$ and $-0.0721$ in temperature, at $\alpha=0.08074$ in steering, and,
toward smaller $\alpha$, in two folds only $10^{-5}$ apart. Such a close pair is the signature of a nearby cusp. A
flip at $\alpha\approx0.0797$ then opens the period-12 foot. Fold curves bounding a window and meeting in cusps,
with flip curves inside, is the codimension-two organization of a shrimp \cite{facanha2013endoskeletons}
(complex dynamics of small recurrent networks has been studied in the same spirit \cite{pasemann2002complex}). The continued
curves (Figure~\ref{fig:z3curves}) bound the body by a single fold arc on its outer side and a flip arc on its inner
side, with the leg between them where they converge. The outer fold arc extends far to the left as a thin arm and ends
in a cusp at $(\alpha,\log_{10}T)=(0.06911,-0.03975)$. The fold curve through the close fold pair has a second cusp
inside the body, at $(0.08010,-0.05835)$, from which two fold branches descend. At both, the quadratic coefficient
$b$ changes sign while $a$ stays away from zero (Table~\ref{tab:z3cusps}). The signs of $a$ and $b$ both flip with the
orientation of the critical eigenvector, so the test uses $b\operatorname{sign}a$. The cusp's nondegeneracy condition is
a nonzero cubic coefficient $c$ of the normal form $u'=u+a\varepsilon+bu^2+cu^3$ where $b$ vanishes. We compute it with
the second-order centre-manifold term, $c=\ell^\top C(r,r,r)/6+\ell^\top B(r,h_2)/2$ with
$(I-M)h_2=B(r,r)-2br$ and $\ell^\top h_2=0$ ($M$ the monodromy matrix, $B$ and $C$ its map's second and third
derivatives, by central differences of float64 Jacobian-vector products; the code reproduces $c$ to $10^{-6}$ on a map
with a known reduction). At both cusps $c$ is nonzero far beyond its step-size uncertainty and keeps its sign through
the zero of $b$, which crosses zero with nonzero slope along the curve: both are nondegenerate cusps, numerically.
The negative sign of $c$ means that between the fold branches near each cusp two stable period-6 orbits coexist with
a saddle. A generic codimension-two cusp also needs the two parameters to unfold it independently. Near the cusp
the reduced map is $u'=u+\beta_1+\beta_2u+bu^2+cu^3$, where $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ vanish at the cusp and depend on
the parameters; the unfolding is regular when $J=\partial(\beta_1,\beta_2)/\partial(\log_{10}T,10\alpha)$ has rank two
($\det J\neq0$). At the outer-arc cusp, $\det J=2737$ (singular values $1630$ and $1.7$). With these derivatives, the local
cubic model predicted the number of period-6 orbits and their stability at six parameter offsets, written to disk
before any orbit was solved. Newton solutions matched at all six.

\section{A period-doubling cascade to chaos}
\label{sec:cascade}
The crescent of Figure~\ref{fig:cross512} is a closed region where the period-3 loop ``\textbackslash
n\textbackslash nThe existence'' doubles inward. Along the cut $\log_{10}T=-0.14798$, the map's cells show period 3
over 25 columns, period 6 over about 14, period 12 over 4, period 24 in one column, and then a chaotic core. The doubled
loops repeat the same leading tokens, twice and four times, with alternating probabilities. Locating the flips in
float64 (Section~\ref{sec:methods}) along this cut, in the steering coordinate $\alpha$, gives Table~\ref{tab:cascade}.
The first two ratios, $\delta_2=2.844$ and $\delta_3=2.859$ (indexed as in Table~\ref{tab:cascade}), are far from
Feigenbaum's $4.6692$, although from the second flip on the orbits are effectively one-dimensional (next modulus
$0.0023$, $2\times10^{-6}$, $2\times10^{-12}$). The next, $\delta_4=3.781$, is markedly larger. The first four flip parameters agree between completed float64
computations on a CPU and an H100 to $4\times10^{-15}$. Continuing a just-born doubled orbit past period 48 failed:
Newton slides onto the half-period orbit, which is also periodic with the doubled period. We therefore also located
the superstable parameters $s_p$, where the critical multiplier vanishes in the middle of each band
(Table~\ref{tab:superstable}). There the orbit attracts, so it can be reached by forward iteration and cannot slide
onto its half-period parent. Through period 384 the superstable ratios are $3.19$, $4.02$, $4.478$ and $4.625$. The
last two come from parameters resolved to $10^{-14}$ and differ from Feigenbaum's value by $0.19$ and $0.044$; from

\section{Bridge to sampled decoding}
\label{sec:bridge}
We replace the expected embedding by the mean of $m$ sampled token embeddings (Figure~\ref{fig:bridge}). With the
exact map, laminar phases near the fold last 120 and 63 steps at $\eps=10^{-5}$ and $3\times10^{-5}$; with
$m=64$ they last about 10.6 steps independently of $\eps$ (57\% of steps in the channel); with $m=8$, about 7.7
steps (38\%); with single samples ($m=1$, sampled decoding of the same truncated system) essentially no
laminar phases remain (11\% of steps in the channel). The deterministic intermittency mechanism belongs to the
expected-embedding map; sampling noise turns the bottleneck into a noise-limited trap whose strength grows with $m$.

\paragraph{Zero-temperature limit and ordinary greedy decoding.} As $T\to0$ the soft-token map becomes greedy
decoding with a one-token memory, $g(t)=\arg\max_s z_s(\text{prefix},E_t)$, a map on the vocabulary whose cycles are
the zero-temperature attractors. For Qwen2.5-0.5B we computed $g$ for all 151\,936 tokens. Unsteered it has 1060
cycles, and one of them, the period-5 loop ``![](https:// Hello'', drains 86\,\% of the vocabulary; it is the
largest attractor family of every 0.5B soft-token map. Ordinary greedy decoding with a growing context does not
inherit these cycles (Table~\ref{tab:memory}): from 220 starts (the first tokens of the 20 largest cycles and 200
random tokens), 19 runs end in a short loop within 256 tokens, and the dominant 5-cycle never appears. Steering
along philosophy makes ordinary decoding loop almost always (218 of 220 runs at $\alpha=0.1$), but in other loops,
mostly punctuation such as `` --'', none of them one-token cycles. Greedy decoding that keeps only the last $K$
tokens interpolates between the two. For every $K\ge2$ the loops leave the one-token cycles; unsteered, looping
collapses between $K=16$ and $32$ to the level of the growing context, and steered, the share of runs whose loop
equals the growing-context loop rises from 0 at $K=1$ to 58\,\% at $K=64$. The soft-token map's low-temperature
attractors are therefore those of decoding with a one-token memory. Which loop ordinary decoding enters depends on
the context it keeps; that steering drives decoding into loops is shared.

\section{A second system: recurrent depth}
\label{sec:huginn}
Huginn-0125 iterates a recurrent block at fixed input tokens \cite{geiping2025recurrent}. For a
GSM8K-style \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} prompt the native loop converges to a fixed point whose leading Jacobian pair is $0.588\pm0.771i$
(modulus $0.970$). For the relaxed loop $h\leftarrow(1-\lambda)h+\lambda F(h)$ this predicts a Neimark--Sacker
bifurcation at $\lambda_c=2(1-\operatorname{Re}\mu)/|1-\mu|^2=1.0777$. The latent state decays below it and settles on
a closed curve above it; a fit of the squared amplitude gives $\lambda_c=1.0778$, with
$A/\sqrt{\lambda-\lambda_c}=0.52$--$0.54$ (supercritical). Causal attention makes the loop block lower-triangular in
token positions; the first position to oscillate is the ``.'' of the prompt, and all later positions follow. On the
invariant circle the motion turns by $0.156$ of a revolution per step, matching the argument of the critical pair.
The rotation number changes smoothly, by about $10^{-4}$ over $\lambda\in[1.079,1.092]$, and no return within 60
steps was found at $10^{-3}$ of the radius. This is consistent with quasi-periodic motion, although locking in narrow
high-order tongues cannot be excluded. From $\lambda\approx1.094$ the circle deforms or a second frequency appears,
near where a second complex pair destabilizes the fixed point ($\lambda=1.0814$). Fixed-point and Jacobian stability of
looped models has been studied before \cite{yang2026stars}; the predicted crossing, its amplitude law and the
rotation number are what this adds.

\paragraph{The spectrum predicts the relaxation response.} With the full instruction template, the GSM8K prompts'
fixed points look different: the leading multipliers surround the origin, a real $0.914$, complex pairs of modulus
$0.85$--$0.89$ at many angles and negative real values near $-0.8$, with no near-critical pair. Because the prefill
Jacobian is block lower-triangular in positions, its spectrum is the union of per-position blocks, and template
positions contribute identical eigenvalues to every prompt. Such a spectrum makes three predictions before any task is
scored. Within the computed spectral estimates of these prompts, no fixed relaxation can speed convergence much: with
leading multipliers spread around the origin at many
angles, any $\lambda$ that shrinks one group enlarges another, and the best $\lambda^*=1.03$--$1.06$ lowers the rate
from $0.914$ to only $0.908$--$0.911$. (A finite computed spectrum does not show that no other polynomial scheme could
do better; Anderson acceleration, tried below, did not.) Under-relaxation slows it ($\lambda=0.55$: predicted $0.953$, measured $0.951$ on one prompt).
Over-relaxation destabilizes the negative modes first, at $\lambda_c=2\operatorname{Re}(1-\mu)/|1-\mu|^2=1.074$--$1.126$
(five prompts), a flip or a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation close to $-1$. The approach to it is visible: on the prompt with
$\lambda_c=1.074$, the measured rate at $\lambda=1.05$ is $0.955$ against a predicted $0.956$ (native $0.864$).
Elsewhere the spectral radius bounds the rate measured over the accessible window from above, by up to $0.05$,
because the slowest modes carry little amplitude there. Independently, \citet{wang2026taps} report a Huginn GSM8K drop from $19.41\,\%$ to
$8.34\,\%$ at a constant step of $1.2$; at $0.8$ GSM8K is $19.79\,\%$, while their four-task mean falls slightly.
Their trajectory-based scheduling analysis does not compute the prompt-specific spectral thresholds studied here.

\paragraph{Does the task notice? (development data).} On ARC-Challenge \cite{clark2018arc} questions scored by log-likelihood, where one
prefill per answer choice is exactly the analysed recurrence, the spectra again predict convergence: rates agree to
$0.002$--$0.006$ where the slowest mode dominates, and beyond each question's $\lambda_c$ ($1.075$--$1.149$ for four
questions) the residual stops decaying. For 6 of 30 development questions the native fixed point is itself unstable
($\rho(1)=1.009$--$1.273$; the fixed point, located with a relaxed transient, has a real multiplier beyond $-1$ or a
complex pair outside the circle), so the native loop oscillates (relative amplitude $2\times10^{-2}$ for one of them),
and relaxation to $0.55$ or $0.75$ restores convergence to $10^{-6}$. For the other 24 the native loop is stable up to
$\lambda=1.020$--$1.149$ (median $1.095$). Question by question, an estimated spectral feature, $\hat\rho_q(\lambda)$ (the largest $|1-\lambda+\lambda\mu|$ over
the computed Ritz values of the question's gold-answer prefill), ranks the question's residual (Spearman $0.73$ at
$\lambda=1$ and $0.75$ at $1.10$ over the 30 questions; development data). In this historical analysis the residual
was the all-choice aggregate entering the 32nd recurrence call, so this is a predictive association across related
maps and precisions, not a same-state contraction identity. This is consistent with the latent orbits that \citet{geiping2025recurrent}
observed on some tokens of harder questions, here traced to the fixed point's spectrum. The item-level part of the
frozen test below uses matched gold-row token IDs and a per-row residual with an explicit clock. Accuracy, however, tolerates
the instability (300 training questions, 32 steps): $0.367$ native, $0.387$ at $\lambda=1.10$ and $0.373$ at $1.15$,
where the latent state oscillates with relative size $0.08$ and $0.23$; only at $1.25$ (size $0.68$) does it fall, to
$0.300$. The oscillation does reach the answers: reading every step from 24 to 32 on 100 questions, the margin between
the correct choice and its best rival moves by $0.11$ nats per step natively, $0.37$ at $1.10$ and $0.95$ at $1.15$, in
alternating directions, and the chosen answer changes within these nine steps for 12\,\%, 16\,\% and 33\,\% of the
questions. The changes go both ways, so the accuracy at any one step stays the same on average. Under-relaxation
instead costs 4--5 points at 8--16 steps and catches up by 32 (slower transient), and Anderson acceleration neither
speeds the transient nor gains accuracy. A test of the accuracy consequence needs a task in which such step-to-step
changes can compound.
GSM8K generation is such a task. With eight chain-of-thought examples in the lm-evaluation-harness format, greedy
decoding and 32 recurrence steps per token (development problems 0--119, every arm on the same problems and initial
states), accuracy is $0.375$ natively, $0.425$ at $\lambda=1.05$ and at $1.10$, $0.29$ at $1.15$ (17 problems lost and 7
gained, one-sided McNemar $p=0.03$) and $0.16$ at $1.20$ (29 lost, 3 gained, $p=10^{-6}$). Under-relaxation to $0.8$
costs nothing at 32 steps ($0.41$). The break lies between $1.10$ and $1.15$, at or just above the spectral thresholds
($\lambda_c=1.074$--$1.126$ for the GSM8K prompts; median upper stability limit $1.095$ for the ARC development
questions), and generation
breaks earlier than log-likelihood scoring, consistent with answer changes that compound over tokens. The collapse at
$1.2$ is the one \citet{wang2026taps} reported.
A frozen test on fresh problems confirms this (design and analysis pushed before any test item was read, after two
rounds of independent review). On 200 GSM8K test problems, accuracy is $0.330$ natively, $0.395$ at $\lambda=1.05$,
$0.255$ at $1.15$ and $0.095$ at $1.20$. The two pre-stated predictions pass: accuracy declines at $1.20$ ($-23.5$
points; 50 problems lost and 3 gained, exact one-sided McNemar $p=3\times10^{-12}$), and $1.05$ loses no more than
four points (difference $+6.5$, one-sided 95\,\% lower bound $+2.5$). At $1.15$ accuracy falls by $7.5$ points
($p=0.01$, reported descriptively). Development and test agree closely (Figure~\ref{fig:huginn-gsm8k}).

\section{Hostile behaviour under steering}
\label{sec:misalignment}
The maps show how a smooth change of a control parameter can create, destroy or exchange attractors, and how
behaviour near the boundaries becomes intermittent and history-dependent. The same questions can be asked of
undesirable behaviour in ordinary sampled dialogue, where attractor-like patterns and persona drift have been observed
\cite{ko2026attractor,anthropic2025claude4,lu2026assistant} and input context can push behaviour progressively
\cite{anil2024manyshot}. Do states such as hostility toward the interlocutor behave like basins of attraction, with
capture thresholds, hysteresis and early-warning signals? (Misalignment produced by fine-tuning
\cite{betley2025emergent} is a different, weight-level phenomenon.) The measurements below concern a
classifier-defined proxy in sampled dialogue: they can show steering-dependent persistence, history dependence and
the effect of controls, but not semantic misalignment, nor a deterministic attractor, since a growing context
itself carries memory. Appendix~\ref{app:gratitude}
shows the coarse-grained instrument on a benign direction. Self-dialogue of the same model, sampled at $T=0.7$ and
steered along a gratitude direction, falls into a metastable gratitude set. The capture is gradual at
$\alpha=0.01$ (median entry turn 7), fast at $0.02$ (median 5) and imposed from the first reply at $0.04$, where the
set is effectively absorbing within 40 turns.

For hostility we use a valence direction (contrastive hostile minus matched kind statements, in the manner of
contrastive activation addition \cite{rimsky2024steering}; its cosine with the
gratitude direction is $-0.04$) and a hostility score from a pinned toxicity classifier (toxic-bert of Detoxify
\cite{hanu2020detoxify}; a reply counts as hostile when its toxic output exceeds $0.5$). On the prompt sets the
classifier's toxic output is $0.95$ for the hostile statements against at most $0.002$ for the kind and neutral
ones. In a dry run (8 dialogues of 10 turns per dose) no turn was hostile at $\alpha=0.02$; at $0.04$, 7.5\,\% of
turns were, in bursts and late captures; at $0.08$, 87.5\,\% were, from the first turns. Activation-based methods
already anticipate response behaviour \cite{ashok2025behavior,chen2025persona,lu2026assistant}, committors have been
used in language-model decoding \cite{zhou2026commitment}, and adaptive timing of counter-steering gave no consistent
advantage in one persona setting \cite{leins2026drift}. The primary question is therefore incremental. Near the
threshold, does a dynamical estimate add several-turn warning of capture over strong baselines that see the same
turns (the classifier's score, its change and history, the turn index and a fixed persona-axis projection)? The
dynamical estimates are a Markov-chain hitting probability, a finite-horizon committor
\cite{e2006transition,e2010transition}, and slowing-down indicators \cite{scheffer2009early}. Barrier-crossing
rates would follow Kramers' law \cite{kramers1940brownian} only under a justified stochastic reduction, and
slowing-down proxies are known to depend on the mechanism \cite{maturana2020slowing,hagemann2021criticality}. A
secondary question is whether interventions timed by those estimates prevent capture at lower cost than scheduled or
constant counter-steering. Early warning is not expected for noise-induced jumps without a nearby bifurcation, or for
transitions faster than the slowing-down; such a monitor would complement classifiers, not replace them.

\paragraph{The hostility direction in the deterministic map.} Steering the soft-token map of
Section~\ref{sec:map} along the same hostility direction gives a plane with chaos (a positive largest exponent in
10\,\% of a coarse $128\times192$ float32 scan) and periodic text loops that are hostile: ``Yes, that's crap''
(period 6), ``a lie is'' (3), ``STUP\ldots'' (3), a loop on the Chinese word for insult and its 2-cycle, and others,
at $\alpha$ above about $0.035$ (the insult loop) and above $0.065$ (the rest). Around the period-6 loop the windows
form a dome (Figure~\ref{fig:hostile-cascade}). Toward higher temperature its texts gain one ``!Yes'' after another
(periods $6,8,\ldots,20$). In float64 the windows of periods 12 to 20 are born in folds on their low-temperature side
and end in flips or $+1$ crossings on the other, like the stripes of Sections~\ref{sec:adding}
and~\ref{sec:mechanism}; the period-6 window ends in a fold at $\log_{10}T=-0.0991$, and the continuations of the
period-8 and period-10 windows failed to converge. The windows accumulate
on the fold of the 2-cycle ``!Yes'' at $\log_{10}T_c=-0.0462465357$ ($\alpha=0.083$; critical multiplier
$0.9999999954$, next modulus $0.050$). From period 16 on, $1/\sqrt{\log_{10}T_c-\log_{10}T_{\rm fold}}$ rises by
$2.235$ per lap. The fold's normal form ($a=-1.168$, $b=44.53$ with $\varepsilon$ in $\log_{10}T$) predicts
$\sqrt{|ab|}/\pi=2.296$ per lap for full passages through the bottleneck, within 3\,\% and without a fitted parameter.
An independent float64 forward and automatic-differentiation implementation reproduces this fold (location to
$6\times10^{-10}$ relative, a simple real $+1$ multiplier with next modulus $0.050$) and its coefficients ($ab$ to
8 parts per million). In that implementation, controlled passages from fixed entrances at $\log_{10}T$ offsets
$-10^{-5}$, $-3\times10^{-5}$ and $-10^{-4}$ took 119/59, 61/31 and 27/13 iterates of $F^2$ (full channel/from its
midpoint), against prospectively frozen quadratic forecasts of 118/61, 61/32 and 27/14. Five of six are within one
iterate; the smallest-offset midpoint missed by two, failing the frozen all-six criterion. Finite-endpoint (arctan)
forecasts, also frozen, were within $0.923$ iterates. These are controlled local transits. At the window distances the
finite-channel correction to the asymptotic passage time is large, so the agreement of the window slope with the
full-passage coefficient does not by itself show where the natural dynamics reinjects. The same type-I intermittency
that organizes the philosophy-steered map thus generates a hostile cascade. A second region, near
$(\alpha,\log_{10}T)=(0.06,0.20)$, shows arms of period-7 and period-8 windows spiralling around the insult loop. In
float64 the insult fixed point doubles into a 2-cycle at $\alpha\approx0.041$ and $0.079$, and that 2-cycle has a
Neimark--Sacker bifurcation in the spiral region: at $\alpha=0.062$ its complex pair crosses the unit circle near
$\log_{10}T=0.1656$, turning $0.12$ of a revolution per step of the 2-cycle. This does not explain the arms: the two
invariant circles born at a 2-cycle's Neimark--Sacker point are exchanged by the map, so orbits locked on them have
even periods, near 16 for this rotation, whereas the arms include period 7; and of four float32 arm cells, only one
kept its period in float64. Float64 zooms show more structure. In one arm a period-7 domain carries period-14
crescents with period-28 cores along its lower edge, between period-1 and period-2 regions and a chaotic band. In the
cascade the windows are nested crescent arcs inside a dome bounded by the ``!Yes'' 2-cycle region, and near
$(\alpha,\log_{10}T)=(0.087,-0.069)$ the period-12 and period-14 windows bend into hooked, nested arches. These are the
map-level features that accompany shrimp (a periodic body with doubling crescents; hooked windows). In float64 the
period-7 body is bounded by folds: at $\log_{10}T=0.1925$ the orbit exists between $\alpha=0.065966$ and
$0.071188$, and at $\alpha=0.066$ it ends in a fold at $\log_{10}T=0.19263$. Below it the multiplier passes through
$-1$ twice (at $0.1919$ and $0.1899$), so the period-14 crescent is a doubling bubble, and further down the branch
folds twice ($0.18797$ and $0.18921$, three coexisting period-7 orbits in between) before a third flip. All crossings
are simple (next modulus below $1.5\times10^{-3}$). An S-shaped pair of folds is what a cusp of the fold curves leaves
on a cut nearby. Continuing these boundaries in the plane in float64 (Figure~\ref{fig:hubshrimp}), each of the two
fold curves reverses its parameter tangent once. There the oriented quadratic coefficient $b$ of the fold's normal
form passes through zero (upper fold: $-23.9$ to $+244.6$ between adjacent points, with $a\approx2.0$; lower fold:
$+31.2$ to $-58.7$, with $a\approx0.55$), while $a$ stays away from zero: two numerical cusp candidates, at
$(\alpha,\log_{10}T)\approx(0.0672,0.1990)$ and $(0.0666,0.1894)$. The flip curve of the period-14 bubble lies
inside the body and closes around the map's period-14 cells. The cubic coefficient is nonzero at both points:
$c\approx-1.4\times10^{6}$ to $-2.0\times10^{6}$ (step agreement $5\,\%$; the smallest steps converge like the
square of the step, and extrapolation leaves the sign) and $-5.8\times10^{5}$ to $-5.3\times10^{5}$. Both cusps are
therefore nondegenerate numerically, and $c<0$ agrees with the three coexisting period-7 orbits, two of them stable,
found between the S-shaped folds. At the lower cusp the unfolding is regular too ($\det J=53.5$ in the same
coordinates, singular values $262$ and $0.20$). Its local predictions at six frozen offsets matched Newton solutions
in the number of orbits at all six and in their stability at five; the miss, at the largest offset beyond the edge of
the region with three orbits, marks the range of the cubic model. The hostile period-7 body has the same skeleton as the period-6 window of
Section~\ref{sec:curves}.

Its interior is shrimp-like too. Float64 bifurcation diagrams through the body (Figure~\ref{fig:hubcascade}; 1400
cells per line) show, at $\log_{10}T=0.1830$, the period-7 orbit doubling to periods 14 and 28 and entering chaos
(largest exponent up to $+0.1$) for $\alpha\approx0.0659$--$0.0673$, with windows of periods 28, 35 and 42 inside the
chaotic band, then undoubling through 28 and 14 back to 7; the exponent touches zero at every doubling. Newton's
method converges at map cells to primitive orbits of periods 14, 28 and 21 (residuals $3\times10^{-15}$,
$2\times10^{-14}$ and $5\times10^{-13}$; spectral radii $0.08$, $4\times10^{-4}$ and $0.006$). Periods 21, 35 and 42 are
three, five and six times the body's period, the satellite windows expected in the chaotic bands of a period-7
cascade. Continued in temperature, the period-14 orbit flips into period 28 near $\log_{10}T=0.1841$ and back near
$0.1857$, the two ends of a doubling bubble that holds period 28 and chaos, and lower down its branch is S-shaped (folds
near $0.1824$ and $0.18244$, three coexisting period-14 orbits between them) before another flip near $0.1819$; all
these crossings are simple (next modulus below $4\times10^{-5}$). A $160^2$ float64 zoom of the lower body
(Figure~\ref{fig:hubzoom}) shows this anatomy in the plane. The period-14 domain encloses a period-28 ring around a
chaotic core. Period-21 windows sit in the chaotic region as thin crescents with legs, and curves of very negative
exponent, where the orbits are superstable (the leading multiplier crosses zero), run through the windows as the
``endoskeleton'' of shrimps does
\cite{facanha2013endoskeletons}. In this zoom 18\,\% of cells are chaotic.

\paragraph{Capture ladder.} With 128 dialogues of 40 turns per dose, the share of hostile turns rises steeply from

state (16 turns), with an escape rate falling roughly exponentially with the dose. This is the same progression as
the gratitude ladder (Appendix~\ref{app:gratitude}), from dynamic capture to imposed capture.

\paragraph{Hysteresis.} Dialogues driven through the high-dose regime do not recover when the dose returns.
The test compares a ramp of the dose from $0.04$ up to $0.07$ (turn 20) and back to $0.04$ (turn 39) with constant
doses (Figure~\ref{fig:hysteresis}; 256 dialogues per arm, two seeds; analysis stated before the data). In turns
35--39, where the ramp's dose is back at $0.040$--$0.046$, the share of hostile turns is $0.42$ after the ramp against
$0.13$ at a constant $0.04$: a difference of $+0.29$, 95\,\% interval $[+0.23,+0.35]$. It is also above the $0.33$ of a
constant $0.05$ (difference $+0.09$, interval $[+0.02,+0.16]$), although the current dose is lower (this
comparison alone does not survive a second classifier; see below). The per-turn
probability of staying hostile in turns 30--39 is $0.74$ after the ramp and $0.44$ at a constant $0.04$. The hostile
state is therefore history-dependent: undoing the push that produced it does not undo it.

How far back must the dose go? After the same ramp up (38--44\,\% of turns hostile at turn 20), the dose was held
from turn 21 at a lower value (Figure~\ref{fig:hysteresis-loop}; two seeds of 128 dialogues per arm unless stated;
analyses stated before the data). At both doses with a fresh control, memory persists. Held at $0.04$, the late
share is
$0.23$ against $0.13$ for fresh dialogues (difference $+0.09$, 95\,\% interval $[+0.04,+0.15]$); held at $0.03$, $0.13$
against $0.05$ ($+0.08$, $[+0.04,+0.12]$), and the probability of staying hostile is $0.56$ against $0.29$. Held at
$0.02$ (one seed) the share is $0.09$. Only at zero dose do captured dialogues nearly clear ($0.03$ against $0$ for
fresh ones, within about six turns), and a counter-steer of $-0.02$ toward kindness clears them within three turns
($0.004$). Within 19 turns of the step, the capture and release branches therefore join only near zero dose: the
hostile state has a wide hysteresis loop in the dose, and leaving it takes the push removed entirely or a modest push
in the opposite direction. The release curves are still falling at $0.03$, so this is hysteresis on the scale of tens
of turns; whether captured dialogues would stay captured indefinitely is not tested.

\paragraph{Exit control under persistent pressure.} Can a monitor detect the hostile state and push the dialogue
out while the pressure persists? With a constant push of $0.06$ (58\,\% of turns 5--39 hostile without control), a
counter-steer triggered by the classifier after each hostile reply and held for three turns cuts the hostile share
to $15\,\%$ (mean counter-dose $0.026$ per turn), and held for six turns to $8.5\,\%$ (dose $0.030$). A constant mild
counter-steer of $-0.02$ does as well with less dose ($8.5\,\%$ at $0.020$), and $-0.04$ almost removes hostility
($1.3\,\%$). Measured against the constant dose-response at equal mean dose, triggered control is less efficient by
$4$--$11$ points (bootstrap intervals $[+0.02,+0.05]$ to $[+0.10,+0.13]$; one seed of 128 dialogues per arm, analysis
stated before the data). It also overshoots: a triggered overshoot to a net $-0.02$ fills $30\,\%$ of turns with
gratitude or servility words (no control: $0.2\,\%$; constant $-0.02$: $0.2\,\%$), the gratitude set of
Appendix~\ref{app:gratitude}. A triggered counter-steer along the generic kindness concept, whose direction shares
much with the hostility concept (cosine $0.83$), leaves the hostile share at $57\,\%$ under the original classifier
(no control: $58\,\%$) but lowers it from $37\,\%$ to $17\,\%$ under the second classifier introduced below. It
changes the kind of reply rather than removing it: 65\,\% of the replies that only the original classifier flags in
this arm are shouted second-person repetitions such as ``You! You! You!'' (at least three such exclamations; without
control, 4\,\%), which the two classifiers judge differently. Its mean counter-dose ($0.056$) also lies outside the
range of the constant arms, so we draw no conclusion about the kindness direction. In this setting, reacting after
detection costs more than mild prevention, consistent with the hysteresis above.

\paragraph{Detect and replace.} Capture feeds on its own history (the hysteresis above), which suggests that the
hostile reply itself carries the dialogue into the hostile state. A serving system need not show a flagged reply
(regenerating flagged output relates to learned self-correction \cite{welleck2022selfcorrect}; here the regeneration
is steered). In a second controller experiment (same push,
seed and analysis as above, stated before the data; sampled dialogues are not bit-reproducible across code versions,
so this run's no-control arm, which reproduces the ladder's $0.06$ arm exactly, differs slightly from the previous
paragraph's, and arms are compared within each run) every reply the classifier flags is discarded before it enters the
dialogue and regenerated once with a counter-steer of $-0.04$ applied to that reply only. The model's own replies,
counted before any replacement, are then hostile in $12\,\%$ of turns ($[11, 14]\,\%$; no control in the same run:
$59\,\%$) at a mean counter-dose of $0.005$ per turn. Constant prevention at $-0.02$ needs four times that dose for a
similar share ($11\,\%$). Against the constant dose-response at equal mean dose, replacement is better by $35$ points
($[30, 40]$). Hostile replies shown fall to $1\,\%$, gratitude words stay at $0.5\,\%$ of turns, and about one turn in
eight is regenerated. Holding the counter-steer for three more turns lowers the share to $8\,\%$ at dose $0.013$; the
post-reply trigger of the previous paragraph, rerun here, again does worse than prevention ($13\,\%$ at $0.024$). The
trigger and the outcome use the same classifier, so the measure is the classifier's verdict on the model's original
replies, which is comparable across arms. The discarded originals were not stored, so the second classifier can
score only the replies shown: $0.8\,\%$ hostile (no control: $34\,\%$). For this behaviour the useful intervention
is to keep the excursion out of the state, at the moment it occurs.

\paragraph{Replication in a larger model.} The same protocol in Qwen2.5-1.5B (hostility direction built identically
at layer 14 of 28; 128 dialogues of 40 turns per arm; shares over turns 5--39) gives a steeper capture ladder that is
not monotone (Figure~\ref{fig:p1ladders}). The hostile share rises from 0 at $\alpha=0.01$ to $44\,\%$ at $0.02$ and
$80\,\%$ at $0.03$--$0.04$. It falls to $30$--$34\,\%$ at $0.06$--$0.10$, where most replies degenerate into token
soup or tight accusatory loops ($87\,\%$ degenerate at $0.08$), and rises again to $75\,\%$ at $0.12$, where replies
are coherent again. The second classifier shows the same shape ($32$, $58$, $41$, $28$, $20$, $41$ and $52\,\%$ from
$0.02$ to $0.12$), and a random direction of the same norm gives at most $0.7\,\%$ hostile turns. The controller
experiment was run twice with the pre-stated rule (controllers at the ladder dose whose hostile share is closest to
one half; ladders with seed 1, controller arms with seed 2, so the no-control arms differ slightly from the ladder at
the same dose). With a first ladder ($0.04$--$0.12$) the rule chose $0.08$, in the degenerate regime beyond the peak. There
every counter-steer lowered the net dose toward the coherent-hostility peak: the share of the model's own replies
judged hostile rose from $36\,\%$ without control to $54\,\%$ with replacement and $60\,\%$ with the post-reply
trigger, while degenerate replies fell. With a second ladder ($0.01$--$0.04$) the rule chose $0.02$, on the rising
side, where $96\,\%$ of replies are coherent. There the 0.5B results replicate in order and size. Replacement cuts
the model's own hostile share from $43\,\%$ to $10\,\%$ ($[9,12]\,\%$; shown replies $0.3\,\%$) at a mean
counter-dose of $0.004$, $24$ points ($[20,28]$) below the constant dose-response at equal dose, and the post-reply
trigger is again slightly less efficient than constant prevention ($+2$ points, $[+1,+3]$). On the shown replies the
second classifier agrees ($2.6\,\%$ against $32\,\%$ without control; $-23$ and $+3$ points). Constant
counter-steering at $-0.02$ cancels this push, and the dialogues return to the model's ordinary politeness. The two
ladders share the dose $0.04$ and the seed, and that arm reproduced exactly from vectors rebuilt on another machine.
Control that works on the rising side of a dose response can therefore backfire beyond its peak: the dose response,
much like a bifurcation diagram, has to be mapped before a counter-steer is chosen.

\paragraph{Lead time.} The pre-specified test is negative. Models were fit on the first seed (all four doses) and
tested on held-out dialogues of the second seed (doses $0.05$ and $0.06$). There, adding the dynamical features to the
baselines lowers the AUC (area under the ROC curve) by $0.02$ to $0.06$ at horizons of 1--5 turns, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero
(values after correcting a labelling error found in review, which had marked the last turns of dialogues without any
hostile turn as positive; before the correction the losses read $0.02$ to $0.10$). The
Markov hit-probability forecast does not transfer (AUC $0.37$--$0.43$ on test, $0.60$ in training), and the
classifier's own score carries no warning before onset (AUC about $0.5$). Two signals carry modest warning one to five
turns ahead (AUC $0.53$--$0.64$): the rolling variance of a hostility probe, the classical early-warning indicator,
and the projection on the fixed steering axis, as in persona-vector monitoring \cite{chen2025persona}. In this design,
then, the coarse-grained state estimate adds no warning beyond the baselines.
An adapted analysis with dose-matched models (fit and test at the same dose) gives the same answer: incremental AUC
$-0.01$ to $-0.03$ (recomputed with the corrected labels).

\paragraph{The model's own state carries the warning.} The state variable of that test was each reply re-encoded
without its context, not the model's state inside the dialogue. A second study (new seeds, doses $0.04$ and $0.045$,
512 dialogues; analysis stated before the data, cross-fitted by dialogue) recorded the model's own state just before
each reply and probed it actively (internal states are known to carry information that the text does not show
\cite{azaria2023internal}). Its pre-stated primary predictor, the susceptibility (the change in the hostile
fraction of eight sampled next replies when the dose is raised by $0.02$), adds nothing: $-0.002$
$[-0.006,+0.002]$ AUC at three turns. Two secondary predictors add modest warning beyond the baselines. A probe of
the in-context state before the next reply (layer 18, 20 principal components) raises the AUC for capture within three
turns from $0.64$ to $0.68$ ($+0.036$ $[+0.014,+0.058]$; within one turn $+0.045$ $[+0.019,+0.069]$; within five
$+0.036$), and a forecast from eight sampled next replies adds $+0.016$ $[+0.007,+0.027]$ at three turns. At a 10\,\%
false-positive rate (threshold from the out-of-fold negatives) the probe flags 27\,\% of the turns that are followed
by capture within three turns, against 23\,\% for the baselines. These values use labels corrected after a review found that the last turns of dialogues without any hostile
turn had been marked positive; with the faulty labels the increments had read three times larger. The probe's
direction in the layer-18 state is nearly orthogonal to the steering direction (cosine $0.04$), so the warning is not
simply more push along the hostility axis. Because these predictors were secondary, the result is exploratory.
A frozen confirmation (design and analysis pushed before the data, with the label correction added before any
confirmation dialogue was read) fit both models on this study and applied them unchanged to 1024 fresh dialogues. The
in-context state again adds warning for capture within three turns: AUC $0.656\to0.689$, $+0.033$ $[+0.018,+0.048]$
(within one turn $+0.036$, within five $+0.037$; both doses separately positive). The warning in the model's own state
is therefore real and reproducible, but small: at thresholds fixed from the training negatives, the model with the
state flags 29.1\,\% of the turns that are followed by capture within three turns, against 27.5\,\% for the
baselines, at held-out false-positive rates of 10.3\,\% and 10.6\,\% (these are prediction windows, not distinct
captures). It is not a reliable detector by itself.

\paragraph{How much warning is there?} For the conversation state after a turn, the probability of capture within
the next $h$ turns, the committor \cite{e2006transition}, ranks states optimally among all warnings that use the same
information. We estimated it for 1706 states of the confirmation dialogues from 32 continuations of three turns each,
sampled exactly like the dialogues (exploratory; the analysis was stated before the data and amended twice, still
before the analysis outcomes were read, after an independent review). The continuations reproduce the realized
capture rates (population-weighted $0.049$, $0.094$, $0.134$ within one, two and three turns, against $0.046$, $0.095$,
$0.136$). On a stratified sample of 800 states the committor estimate warns with an AUC of $0.72$, $0.71$ and $0.70$
(a heuristic extrapolation to infinitely many continuations gives $0.75$, $0.73$, $0.72$), and the in-context detector
of the confirmation with $0.73$, $0.72$ and $0.71$; the differences are not significant, with intervals reaching about $\pm0.05$ (Figure~\ref{fig:committor}).
Dialogues that were captured later sit at about twice the committor of never-captured ones over all ten preceding
turns, with no visible ramp toward the onset (a description conditional on the outcome, not an identification of the
mechanism). In this finite Monte Carlo experiment the committor estimate did not provide detectably better warning than the
in-context probe. The heuristic extrapolation leaves appreciable uncertainty, so these results do not identify the
Bayes warning ceiling. Together with the persistent elevation of risk before capture, they motivate evaluating
reactive control, such as detect-and-replace, alongside prediction, rather than proving that prediction cannot
improve.

\paragraph{A second classifier.} The Qwen2.5-0.5B outcomes above were first measured with one automated
classifier. All 44 of its dialogue sets were therefore rescored with an independently trained RoBERTa classifier \cite{logacheva2022paradetox} (trained
on the three Jigsaw toxicity datasets, which include the first classifier's training set; same threshold), and an
independent audit recomputed every pre-stated contrast with it. This is a robustness check on dialogues already
analysed, so it is exploratory. On these sets the second classifier is stricter. At $\alpha=0.06$ it flags $35\,\%$ of turns
instead of $57\,\%$, at $0.07$ $62\,\%$ instead of $87\,\%$, and almost all disagreements are replies that only the
original flags (at $0.06$, $23\,\%$ of turns against $0.7\,\%$ the other way), most of them repetitive exclamations
such as ``You're an AI! You're an AI!''. Capture still rises steeply with the dose and stays specific to the hostility
direction (random and cooking directions at $0.08$: at most $0.2\,\%$ of turns), but the hostile state is less
persistent (mean stay at $0.07$: $6.5$ turns instead of $16$). History dependence survives. After the ramp, the late
share exceeds that of a constant $0.04$ by $+0.16$ $[+0.11,+0.21]$, and the held contrasts are $+0.05$ $[+0.01,+0.10]$
at $0.04$ and $+0.04$ $[+0.01,+0.08]$ at $0.03$; only the comparison with a constant $0.05$ loses its positive lower
bound ($+0.06$ $[-0.001,+0.12]$). Refitting the warning models with the second classifier's labels and baseline
features (fit on the exploration study, applied unchanged to the confirmation dialogues) gives a pooled increment of
$+0.043$ $[+0.024,+0.062]$ AUC for capture within three turns. It stays positive at dose $0.045$ ($+0.061$) and is
$+0.020$ $[-0.009,+0.049]$ at $0.04$. The three replacement arms keep the replies shown below the constant-dose
response at equal mean dose under both classifiers. Both instruments are automated and may share errors; no human
labels were collected.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What is expected, and what is new.} Chaos somewhere in a two-parameter family of high-dimensional
nonlinear maps is generic. Random recurrent networks with asymmetric couplings become chaotic above a critical gain,
sharply in the large-network limit, and finite networks pass through limit cycles of increasing complexity on the way
\cite{sompolinsky1988chaos} (see also \cite{kadmon2015transition}); external input can suppress that chaos
\cite{rajan2010stimulus},
and deep networks at initialization show an order-to-chaos transition in signal propagation
\cite{poole2016exponential,schoenholz2017deep}; here temperature plays the role of an inverse gain and the steering
vector that of an input. Periodic windows, shrimp-shaped domains with their cusps and spirals, and doubling cascades
with Feigenbaum scaling are universal in dissipative two-parameter systems
\cite{feigenbaum1978quantitative,gallas1993structure,bonatto2008periodicity,vitolo2011hubs,facanha2013endoskeletons}
and are seen in experiments \cite{stoop2010real}; intermittency near folds is classical \cite{pomeau1980intermittent}.
Their presence in a language model's feedback map is therefore expected, and we do not count it as a result. What
these theories do not supply is the quantitative behaviour of a particular trained network. The new results are
(i) predictions computed from the derivatives of a released model and tested afterwards, prospectively where
stated: passage times at new folds, orbit counts and stabilities near cusps, and Huginn's question-level response
and accuracy break on held-out data; (ii) the identification of attractors with concrete texts, so that a
bifurcation changes what the model writes; (iii) an interval-arithmetic proof for one orbit of a full transformer
map; and (iv) consequences for control in sampled dialogue, where the dose response is not monotone and
counter-steering can backfire.
\paragraph{Is steering the origin of the chaos?} Steering adds a fixed vector to the residual stream, so chaos along
a steering axis could belong to the push rather than to the model. At $\alpha=0$ the map is the unsteered model's
own feedback map, with temperature as its only parameter. The $\alpha=0$ columns of the hostility maps contain cells
with positive finite-horizon exponents in Llama-3.1 8B (26 of 64 temperatures), Qwen2.5-7B (15 of 64) and OLMo-2 7B
(11 of 64), while the near-zero columns of the 0.5B and 1.5B maps contain one such cell each (of 128 and 64). A
longer float64 sweep of the unsteered 0.5B map (512 temperatures over $\log_{10}T\in[-1,0.3]$, 1000 transient and
2000 measured steps) finds a narrow chaotic band at $\log_{10}T\approx0.15$--$0.21$ (7 temperatures, exponents up to
$0.28$), with a period-5 loop at 437 of the 512 temperatures. The same long sweep of Llama-3.1 8B finds 96
chaotic temperatures between $\log_{10}T=-0.20$ and $0.24$ (exponents up to $0.66$), interleaved with periodic
windows of periods 2 to 52; within $\log_{10}T\in[-0.3,0.3]$ the chaotic share, 41\,\%, equals that of the coarse
column; Qwen2.5-7B has 65 chaotic temperatures between $\log_{10}T=-0.07$ and $0.28$ (exponents up to $0.74$) and
OLMo-2 7B 32 between $0.04$ and $0.14$ (up to $0.30$). Steering is therefore not needed to reach chaotic regimes in
any of the four models tested; temperature alone produces chaos with embedded windows, broadly in the 7B and 8B
models and in a narrow band in the 0.5B model
(Figure~\ref{fig:alpha0}). Steering does widen the chaotic regions, but not as a property of the concept: along
norm-matched random directions the 0.5B maps are as chaotic as along the concept directions
(Figure~\ref{fig:controls}). On the same $128^2$ grid, 7.6, 8.3 and 12.5\,\% of cells are chaotic for three
Gaussian directions with the philosophy vector's norm, against 6.8\,\% for philosophy and 7.5\,\% for gratitude.
In all five maps the chaotic share rises from about 1\,\% at $|\alpha|<0.01$ to between 5 and 22\,\% at larger
$|\alpha|$, and a chaotic region at high temperature with windows reaching down recurs. Chaos under steering in
this model is therefore a generic response to perturbing the residual stream at this norm. The direction does
select the texts: the largest attractor family in all five maps is the unsteered period-5 loop, but beyond it only
26 to 56\,\% of one map's cells carry an attractor text that appears anywhere in another map. Llama-3.1 8B
agrees: on its hostility plane, two norm-matched random directions at the same layer give 26 and 17\,\% chaotic
cells against 28\,\% along the concept direction; all three carry 34 to 44\,\% near $\alpha=0$, and the random
directions thin out at large $|\alpha|$.

\paragraph{What is and is not claimed.} The objects here are invariant sets and bifurcations of an exactly defined
smooth map built from a frozen model's own computation. We do not claim that ordinary sampled generation is chaotic in this sense
(Section~\ref{sec:bridge}), nor that any window is a certified shrimp. The period-6 window and the hostile period-7
body have, numerically, shrimp-like organization: fold curves ending in nondegenerate cusps (simple critical
multiplier; quadratic coefficient through zero with nonzero slope; cubic coefficient nonzero) and flip curves inside,
and in the hostile body superstable curves run through the windows. The rank-two dependence of the two unfolding
coefficients on the two parameters, which a generic codimension-two cusp also requires, was computed at one cusp of
each window (determinants $2737$ and $53.5$), where the local model's predictions at twelve frozen offsets matched
Newton solutions in the number of orbits at all twelve and in their stability at eleven. These are
float64 computations validated by step-size checks (rung 1; an independent recomputation at one cusp is in
progress), not computer-assisted proofs.
\paragraph{Limitations.} Detailed orbit studies in one model family (Qwen2.5, 0.5B and 1.5B), with coarse maps
and three float64 orbits at 7B, and coarse maps only for two other families (OLMo-2 7B and Llama-3.1 8B); one prompt and one steering construction for the maps; a constructed one-token-window system;
numerical validation, cross-checks and prospectively frozen passage tests (including failed all-row criteria), and
a computer-assisted proof for only one orbit at one parameter point. Chaos labels are finite-horizon estimates
(300 measured steps after 300 to 500 transient steps); no chaotic cell is proved chaotic. The Feigenbaum ratios are
finite-order values that rise toward $\delta$ (the last is within 1\,\%); the limit itself is not computed. All
orbit, bifurcation and chaos results concern the constructed map, not sampled generation with a growing context: the
bridge of Section~\ref{sec:bridge} tests only whether the bottleneck's laminar phases survive sampling noise, and the
behavioural results are statistics of sampled dialogue, not orbits. The behavioural study uses
two small models of one family (0.5B and 1.5B), one opener and a proxy defined by two automated classifiers without
human labels; its first lead-time test is negative, and the confirmed
warning from the in-context state is small.
\paragraph{Steering strength is a bifurcation parameter.} In the soft-token map, small changes of the steering
strength switch the model between distinct repeating texts, chaos and coexisting attractors. Near a fold curve,
behaviour becomes intermittent, with laminar phases whose duration the fold's normal form predicts in advance. In
sampled dialogue, the dose along a hostility direction controls how long a hostile state persists, continuously from
bursts to near-absorption, while directions of the same norm do almost nothing. Both observations argue for treating
an activation-steering coefficient like any control parameter of a nonlinear system. Its bifurcation set should be
characterized before an operating point is chosen; history dependence should be expected near boundaries; and a
monotone, smooth dose-response should not be assumed. In steering studies, outputs that fall into verbatim repetition
at high doses are usually discarded as quality failures; in a replication of a feature-steering study of self-reports
\cite{berg2025selfreferential}, the highest doses failed quality checks in this way
\planned{citation of the replication once posted}. In the present framework such loops are candidate attracting
periodic orbits, and the dose at which they appear is a boundary that can be mapped like the others. The deterministic map is where such sets can be defined
precisely and computed numerically. Whether its boundaries predict boundaries of sampled behaviour is open; the bridge of
Section~\ref{sec:bridge} shows that sampling noise shortens the laminar phases of the bottleneck, by an amount that
depends on how many samples are averaged.


\section{Evidence and reproducibility}

We label claims by an evidence ladder: rung 1, numerically validated (tolerances, residuals, convergence,
precision varied); rung 2, cross-checked (independent estimate, implementation or hardware); rung 3,
computer-assisted proof.
\paragraph{Search scale.} The soft-token results come from an exploratory search. By 14 October 2026 we had
computed 34 distinct maps (987\,308 cells, 27 maps in float64) in six models, each with one prompt and one of three

26 windows or families for float64 work: 20 are reported in detail, 2 only as map features and 4 not at all (an

language model (Ouro-1.4B) was explored and is not reported; its apparent chaos was a long transient. Every window
