Fed back through its own soft next-token distribution, as in Soft Thinking, a pretrained language model defines a discrete-time map with continuous states, whose dynamics can be mapped, predicted from derivatives at selected transitions and in places proved, and whose consequences for inference depend on the feedback architecture and the numerical regime. In Qwen2.5, OLMo-2 and Llama-3 models of 0.5B to 72B parameters, temperature and steering move this loop between repeating text and chaos, with positive finite-time Lyapunov exponents that exact tangents reproduce; 74 to 92% of sampled chaotic labels persist for 10^5 steps. The chaos has practical consequences. Rerun with only the batch size changed, chaotic float64 loops separate on the schedule the Lyapunov exponent sets, while periodic loops stay together. In tests fixed before their data, Soft Thinking with its published settings changed 21% of the answers to fresh GSM8K problems with the batch layout alone, and reasoning models that answer 86 to 90% of them correctly changed 14.5 and 19.5%. In Qwen2.5-0.5B, along recorded orbits, steering creates the chaos mainly by making next tokens compete, which turns the softmax readout from a contraction into an expansion. Whether a prompt's loop is chaotic does not predict the accuracy of ordinary sampled answers beyond the model's uncertainty about its first answer token. The loop obeys quantitative laws of low-dimensional maps: finite-order doubling ratios approach Feigenbaum's constant (4.6688 against 4.6692), periodic windows accumulate on folds close to a 1/N^2 law, passage times predicted in advance from fold normal forms came within one iterate in 42 of 48 cases at four new folds, and interval arithmetic proves an attracting period-8 orbit of the full transformer map. The loop is not ordinary sampled generation.
