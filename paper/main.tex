\begin{abstract}
A conspicuous structure in a neural network's dynamics should have a dynamical consequence. Which consequences reach predictions, task accuracy, or compute cost is a separate, measurable question. Shrimp-shaped parameter windows organize stable periodic behavior and have been reported in small recurrent neural networks. We develop a program to test whether their geometry supplies useful control information: which intervention direction helps, how large a change is required, and how robust the destination is. An exact tied-weight toy yields alternating predictions and lower squared error for averaged predictions than for random-phase readout, with residual bias. Initial exploratory pilots verify attracting nontrivial cycles in \HenonCycles{} of \HenonTrials{} H\'enon cells, but find \TrainingCycles{} such cycles in \TrainingTrials{} tiny-network training cells, with \TrainingUnresolved{} unresolved and \TrainingDivergent{} operationally divergent. A recurrence policy never intervenes in \PolicyTrials{} runs and reproduces fixed-rate trajectories exactly; each of four policies has \PolicyDivergent{} divergent cases. Ten figures distinguish pilot evidence, post-run diagnostics, exact algebra, and proposed mechanisms. The next experiments first verify a neural periodic regime, then measure its expression in outputs and task behavior, and finally test causal interventions against matched loss, recurrence, stability, and ordinary performance-map controls. Utility includes discovery costs and failed cases; it is not inferred from visual prominence.
\end{abstract}

\section{The useful question behind a striking pattern}
A shrimp structure is evidence that changing controls changes the long-run dynamics in an organized way. Its period, attraction strength, and bifurcation boundaries describe real properties of the update rule. The practical opportunity is to turn that organization into a decision: stay in a region, leave it, move to a more robust interior, or stop a computation whose answer is already stable.

A \emph{shrimp} conventionally denotes a characteristic organization of periodic windows in a \emph{control-parameter plane}. The associated periodic orbit lives in the evolving state space (Figure~\ref{fig:spaces}). Gallas described such domains for the H\'enon map \citep{gallas1993}. Da Silva and Rech report them in a three-neuron continuous-time Hopfield-type system with tanh activations \citep{dasilva2022}. Neural existence therefore has a specific precedent; usefulness for learning or reasoning remains the question here.

Sohl-Dickstein's preprint and companion article map training success across hyperparameters \citep{sohldickstein2024,sohldicksteinblog2024}. Lai and colleagues map transient dynamics over slices of initial latent states in recurrent reasoning models \citep{lai2026}. These observations motivate the search, but neither map as presented identifies the primitive periodic orbits and parameter bifurcations required here. Oscillatory optimization and period-doubling routes to chaos also have prior neural-training precedents \citep{cohen2021,kalra2025}. Our proposed contribution is a test of the \emph{incremental operational value} of parameter-window geometry.

\section{What must manifest, and what a readout can hide}
\subsection{A real internal signature need not change the answer}
Let $z_{t+1}=F_\eta(z_t)$ be the complete autonomous state update, and let $q_t=R(z_t;x)$ be the output used by a task. A verified attracting period-$p$ orbit is an internal manifestation: nearby trajectories return to the orbit and repeat after $p$ steps. A shrimp organizes where that behavior exists as $\eta$ varies. Neither statement requires the task readout to separate its phases.

If $R(z_0;x)=\cdots=R(z_{p-1};x)$, the entire internal cycle is invisible to that readout. Distinct network states may implement the same prediction through symmetries, inactive units, or directions to which the output is insensitive. A decoded answer is still coarser: changing logits can retain the same argmax. Figure~\ref{fig:readout} makes the distinction explicit. An internally strong signal can therefore be harmless for accuracy while still affecting computation, noise response, or sensitivity to perturbations. It can also have no measurable effect on a chosen endpoint at the tested resolution. The experiment must determine which case occurs.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.79\textheight,keepaspectratio]{conceptual-readout.pdf}
\caption{\figstatus{Algebraic illustration} (A) The constructed map $F(h)=-h$, initialized at $h_0=1$, alternates between $+1$ and $-1$. (B) The identity readout preserves that oscillation. (C) The squared readout hides it completely. This exact example makes no attraction, correctness, or shrimp claim: the two-step multiplier is $+1$. It demonstrates why a real internal signal need not survive a many-to-one readout.}
\label{fig:readout}
\end{figure}

For a verified orbit, define state and output amplitudes
\begin{equation}
 A_z^2=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\|z_k-\bar z\|^2,
 \qquad
 A_q^2=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\|R(z_k;x)-\bar q\|^2.
\end{equation}
The first detects state motion; the second measures its visibility through a specified readout. Their units differ, so neither their ratio nor a universal common threshold is assumed meaningful. Report phasewise gradients, task loss, answer disagreement, and predictive margins alongside them. These are proposed follow-up measurements, not measurements already made for a verified neural orbit.

\subsection{Three mechanisms worth testing}
\paragraph{Recovery and robustness.}
Attraction suggests a local ability to recover from perturbations. It does not guarantee a large basin or a correct output. Measure the probability of returning to the same orbit or answer under a frozen perturbation distribution, recovery time, and the change in task quality. Across a window, ask whether interior locations remain useful under larger control perturbations than locations near its boundary.

\paragraph{Intervention direction.}
A measured boundary supplies a possible direction and scale for changing controls. If an incorrect cycle wastes a budget, crossing that boundary may release it; if a useful regime is fragile, moving inward may improve robustness. Compare either action with equal-size changes in random, tangential, reverse, and ordinary performance-informed directions. Leaving a stable cycle is an intervention, not an improvement until a task endpoint improves.

\paragraph{Stopping and aggregation.}
If predictions agree over a complete cycle and are correct, additional iterations may be unnecessary. If predictions differ across phases, phase averaging or phase selection may help or harm. Compare a cycle-aware rule with ordinary output agreement, residual, and stagnation rules, charging the cost of detecting a cycle. Test aggregation against matched numbers of evaluations and existing averaging controls. Stability alone provides no accuracy guarantee.

\section{From recurrence to verified parameter structure}
\subsection{State and controls}
For deterministic training on $\mathcal D$, the pilot uses
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{t+1}=F_\eta(\theta_t)
 =\theta_t-D_\eta\nabla L_{\mathcal D}(\theta_t),\qquad
 D_\eta=\operatorname{diag}(\eta_1 I,\eta_2 I).
\end{equation}
All weights and biases are state variables; positive layer learning rates are controls. For latent inference, a fixed model and problem instead define $h_{t+1}=G_\alpha(h_t;x)$. An autonomous description must include every evolving cache, auxiliary latent, momentum variable, and schedule phase.

Continuous gradient flow obeys
\begin{equation}
 \dot\theta=-\nabla L(\theta),\qquad
 \frac{dL}{dt}=-\|\nabla L(\theta)\|^2.
\end{equation}
It cannot support a nonconstant periodic orbit: integrating over a period forces the gradient to vanish throughout. Finite-step descent need not preserve monotonic loss, allowing the periodic-orbit search without guaranteeing a shrimp.

\subsection{The verification ladder}
A periodic point has $F_\eta^p(z)=z$ and no smaller positive period. Scalar loss recurrence is insufficient because different states can share the same loss. The pilot screens complete-state tails over repeated cycles, checks nonzero amplitude relative to numerical tolerance, rejects smaller periods, and independently checks orbit closure. For an orbit $z_0,\ldots,z_{p-1}$, its period Jacobian product is
\begin{equation}
 M_p=DF_\eta(z_{p-1})\cdots DF_\eta(z_0).
\end{equation}
A spectral radius $\rho(M_p)<1$, with a margin exceeding numerical error, establishes local attraction of an isolated orbit. It does not estimate basin size. Flat or symmetry-related directions may produce neutral multipliers; near-unit values require nonhyperbolic or unresolved status rather than discarded directions.

Periodic pixels alone do not establish shrimp geometry. A later stage must continue branches and locate saddle-node and period-doubling boundaries around connected isoperiodic regions. Longer horizons, changed precision, and multiple initial states assess long transients and multistability. Coordinate choices also matter: nonlinear axis transformations alter widths, normals, and apparent shape. Freeze meaningful coordinates such as log learning rates and distinguish coordinate-dependent distances from bifurcation organization.

\section{The exploratory experiment and evidence record}
The initial target is full-batch training of a tanh network with two inputs, three hidden units, one scalar output, and biases in both layers. All thirteen parameters evolve. The objective is half mean squared error plus $0.001\|\theta\|^2/2$, including biases in the penalty; predictive MSE excludes the penalty. A random tanh teacher defines independent standard-normal input splits with 24 training, 48 validation, and 96 test examples. Teacher metadata, splits, and initial states were prepared before the freeze, bound by manifest hashes, and verified on loading. This is inspired by the fractal-training construction, not an exact replication.

Result-free manifests specify a 65-cell H\'enon control at 3,000 updates per cell and a $9\times9$ layer-learning-rate scan for seeds 101 and 102 at 2,000 updates per cell. Each seed defines both a task and its one initialization; these sources of variation are not separately estimated. Scans retain up to 512 tail states, screen periods through 16, and use float64. All requested cases remain in the census. A bounded, apparently aperiodic tail is not automatically chaos.

The policy comparison uses four task/initialization seeds, 201--204, and six initial rate pairs: 96 runs across fixed rates, predetermined decay, scalar stagnation-triggered decay, and recurrence-triggered decay. Each has at most 1,200 gradient updates and two interventions, each halving both rates. Scheduled decay acts at updates 400 and 800; adaptive rules inspect every 40 updates from update 200. Recurrence screens full weights without online orbit certification. Both adaptive detectors wait at least two check intervals after a rate change and use history only from the current constant-rate segment. Stagnation compares two loss windows from that segment.

Every generated state, pre-update regularized loss, realized learning rate, and trigger is retained. Test and validation outcomes do not trigger actions. The descriptive endpoints are terminal test MSE and divergence fraction; the report provides a policy mean only if all corresponding runs are finite. It provides no inferential p-values or confidence intervals. Equal maximum update counts do not imply equal total cost: divergent runs stop early, diagnostics consume resources, and wall time includes common instrumentation.

The initial runs used revision
\href{https://github.com/tdj28/neural-shrimp/commit/bd3d733b51e9f1ae9247ba712633aca023e7afac}{\texttt{bd3d733b51e9}}
after the full code, plan, and input revision was pushed to the private repository. Receipt verification checks hashes and the exact trial census. \section{An exact neural toy: a cycle changes predictions}
A scalar example makes the manifestation question concrete without a new numerical experiment. Consider a two-layer linear network with its two scalar weights tied, $\widehat y=\theta^2x$, trained on $(x,y)=(1,1)$ using $L(\theta)=\tfrac12(\theta^2-1)^2$. Gradient descent gives
\begin{equation}
 F_\eta(\theta)=(1+2\eta)\theta-2\eta\theta^3.
\end{equation}
This is a deliberately simple neural parameterization. Its exact cycles are not a shrimp, a result for the tanh pilot, or evidence of generalization.

Set $r=1/(2\eta)$. For $\eta>1/2$, a positive period-two orbit has
\begin{equation}
 u,v=\frac{\sqrt{1+3r}\ \mp\ \sqrt{1-r}}{2},\qquad
 uv=r,\qquad (u+v)^2=1+3r.
\end{equation}
Substitution gives $F_\eta(u)=v$ and $F_\eta(v)=u$. Its two-step multiplier is
\begin{equation}
 \mu=F_\eta'(u)F_\eta'(v)=9-2(1+2\eta)^2,
\end{equation}
so the nontrivial cycle attracts locally for
$1/2<\eta<(\sqrt5-1)/2$. The endpoints have $\mu=1$ and $\mu=-1$, respectively, and are excluded from that attraction claim.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.79\textheight,keepaspectratio]{analytic-training-cycle.pdf}
\caption{\figstatus{Exact analytic illustration} (A) Phase predictions, (B) three squared-error readouts, and (C) the local multiplier for the tied scalar network $\widehat y=\theta^2x$ on the single training sample $(1,1)$. Green shading marks local attraction; the continuation beyond its right boundary is unstable. Averaging predictions lowers error relative to uniformly selecting a phase, but retains bias. Zero-error fixed points remain available at other stable rates. Curves evaluate equations in the text, not a new pilot, a parameter sweep with fitted outcomes, or a verified shrimp.}
\label{fig:analytic-cycle}
\end{figure}

The outputs $u^2$ and $v^2$ differ. Their mean is $(1+r)/2<1$, making the task consequence explicit. Three squared-error quantities on this one sample are
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{random\ phase}}&=(1-r^2)/2,\\
 \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{mean\ prediction}}&=(1-r)^2/4,\\
 \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{mean\ weight}}&=9(1-r)^2/16.
\end{align}
The first averages squared errors over uniformly sampled cycle phases; the second squares the error after averaging predictions; the third evaluates the network at $(u+v)/2$. Thus the same internal cycle supplies both a visible output oscillation and distinct aggregation choices (Figure~\ref{fig:analytic-cycle}). Prediction averaging improves on random-phase evaluation here, as the squared-loss variance decomposition predicts. It does not eliminate bias and does not beat the zero-error solutions $\theta=\pm1$, which are locally attracting for $0<\eta<1/2$.

This calculation establishes a concrete consequence of neural periodicity and a conditional aggregation benefit. It establishes neither a net compute gain nor additional value from shrimp geometry. Its proper experimental role is a transparent control for measuring phasewise outputs and separating prediction averaging from weight averaging.

\subsection{Establish a regime before asking it to improve a model}
First use the analytic scalar cycle to check orbit and output measurements, then reproduce a pinned published small-neural-system periodic regime with its equations and parameter conventions independently checked. Separately specify a targeted successor to the training scan: longer horizons, parameter refinement, and complete-state orbit verification. Preserve the empty scan and every failed candidate. A known neural positive control demonstrates instrument capability; it is not a new discovery or evidence that the current training system has a shrimp.

Once a neural periodic orbit is verified, continue its branch and relevant bifurcations across two controls. Retain stable cycles that lack shrimp geometry as a comparison group. This separates value supplied by periodicity from any value supplied by the window's organization. Boundary uncertainty must be propagated into any proposed boundary direction or width; a coarse visual outline is insufficient.

\subsection{Map consequences at the same parameter settings}
For each verified regime, pair the dynamical map with a task-observable map: predictive loss or exact correctness, phasewise output disagreement, recovery probability and time under perturbations, and compute to a declared quality threshold. Include matched nearby nonperiodic settings, fixed points, and ordinary periodic windows. The primary question is whether the same boundaries organize changes in these observables, and whether those changes survive changes in task and initialization.

Measure complete-state motion and output motion separately. Include a readout-insensitive or symmetry-controlled case to test whether the analysis mistakenly converts internal structure into a claimed output effect. If a clear state-space signal has no task-visible effect under the tested interventions, report that as a mechanism result. It bounds the effect detected through that readout on the tested inputs, horizon, and precision. An exact invisibility claim requires a separate invariance argument, such as a demonstrated symmetry.

\subsection{Branch checkpoints to test intervention effects}
At common checkpoints, apply a frozen set of equal-size control changes. Compare boundary-informed directions with random, tangential, reverse, shuffled-map, and ordinary locally sampled performance-informed directions in prespecified log-learning-rate coordinates. Include a zero-dose sham with identical diagnostic overhead; it controls observation and timing, not intervention magnitude. Match intervention doses, opportunities, state access, and tuning budgets. Test both escape from harmful cycles and movement toward useful robust regimes. Measure post-intervention task utility, recovery, and failures, rather than treating boundary crossing as success.

A predictive comparison uses identical fitted models and tuning budgets. Baseline features include recent loss, progress, gradient and update norms, recurrence residual, estimated period, cycle amplitude, and local stability, including available orbit multipliers. The augmented model adds boundary distance, local width, boundary direction, and measured window organization. Period information is shared so its value cannot be misattributed to geometry. Labels are future task utility after a specified action, not recurrence itself. Hold out whole windows or spatial blocks as well as tasks, so local interpolation does not masquerade as transfer.

\subsection{Demand a practical advantage over simpler controls}
Required controller baselines are a predetermined schedule, stagnation, recurrence, local stability, and ordinary performance maps with matched scan costs. Include output agreement for stopping and matched averaging procedures for phase aggregation. An inactive detector, as in the current pilot, cannot answer the relative-effectiveness question.

The proposed primary endpoint is paired error on untouched evaluation/test data at a fixed total resource budget. These data are separate from any training or development-validation data used for map selection, controller tuning, or online actions. A complementary endpoint is compute to a quality threshold, with unsolved cases budget-censored. Reasoning experiments require exact correctness at equal total inference cost. The exact aggregation, practical-effect threshold, divergence handling, cluster bootstrap, primary contrast, and multiplicity rule must be frozen before evaluation. A synthetic teacher or data-generating problem is the independent task unit; pixels and time points are not independent replications. Keep seed variation within tasks.

Charge map construction, tuning, failed probes, Jacobians, state storage, transfers, and extra validation. Report cold-start and explicitly amortized costs for a declared number of future runs. Reused experimental prefixes are charged to every hypothetical deployed policy. Report the unselected prevalence of harmful regimes: a high rescue rate in a deliberately enriched set of traps does not establish population-level savings.

\section{A controlled extension to latent reasoning}
Stable repetition and transient delay call for different interventions. The Lai et al. preprint uses decoded settling, discusses transient structure, and describes capped or inconsistent slices and slim fractals in its appendices \citep{lai2026}. Our extension would retain failures and measure both decoded correctness and complete-state recurrence.

Positive relaxation offers an interpretable intervention:
\begin{equation}
 G_\alpha(h)=(1-\alpha)h+\alpha F(h),\qquad \alpha>0.
\end{equation}
It preserves the fixed-point equation: $G_\alpha(h)=h$ exactly when $F(h)=h$. At a fixed point, an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $DF$ becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mu=1-\alpha+\alpha\lambda.
\end{equation}
Thus positive relaxation cannot stabilize a real $\lambda>1$ direction, although it can change oscillatory stability. This local algebra does not guarantee global correctness or faster convergence; relaxation can change basins, transients, and nontrivial cycles.

A two-control experiment could use relaxation and momentum, including the previous iterate in the full state. Feedback gain is another control, but generally changes the fixed-point equation and therefore the solver itself. Compare damping, restarts, and geometry-informed changes against residual, output-agreement, and stagnation policies on untouched tasks at matched total inference budgets. Distinguish removing internal motion from rescuing an incorrect answer.

\section{Interpretation and limits}
The most concrete potential contribution of shrimp geometry is an intervention map: where a desired dynamic persists, which direction changes it, and how much perturbation it tolerates. Three operational outcomes would justify pursuing it: lower error at a fixed total cost, more reliable recovery under disturbances, or earlier correct stopping. A beautiful atlas alone establishes none of them.

The right action depends on the task. An incorrect attracting cycle is a candidate trap. A correct, output-stable cycle may admit earlier stopping. A useful but fragile regime may benefit from an inward move. A cycle invisible to the chosen readout may need no intervention. For every case, the relevant comparison is with a simpler diagnostic given the same information and resources.

Several negative outcomes would still answer the question. If ordinary recurrence matches an active geometry method, a cheaper observable may suffice. If mapping costs erase the per-run gain, there is no net advantage at that deployment scale. If a change improves training loss but harms validation, both must be reported. If an internally strong pattern is removed by the readout, the study should identify that mechanism without claiming a hidden task benefit. The current pilot establishes neither a neural shrimp nor a control advantage, and limits its negative evidence to the tested settings, horizons, precision, and initialization choices.

\section*{Reproducibility and research status}
The private repository contains executable pilots, frozen inputs and configurations, immutable raw attempts, descriptive reports, figure-generation code and provenance, a research plan, a claim ledger, and reference records. Figures are rebuilt by \path{tools/build_evidence_figures.py} and \path{tools/build_concept_figures.py}. Each \path{paper/figures/*.receipt.json} records its sources, hashes, selection or construction, and plotted values where applicable. The gallery in \path{docs/visual-revision.md} maps figures to their evidence and claim scope. The initial program is exploratory and has no registry record. Private Git history supports internal chronology, not public prospective verification. Confirmatory work requires a fresh frozen design, retained failures and amendments, full cost receipts, and outcome-linked claims. Human editorial review and release decisions remain pending; routine local audits do not require paid Pro review.
