\noindent\textbf{Private exploratory working draft.} EXP-004--005 reproduce and probe a published small recurrent network. Connectivity is fixed during each trajectory; no training, reasoning benchmark, or task-accuracy benefit is claimed.

\begin{abstract}
We numerically reproduce a shrimp-shaped periodic window in the connection-weight plane of the three-neuron tanh network studied by da Silva and Rech. A frozen experiment measures 39,322 grid settings and a separate 201-point transect. Independent shooting verifies two attracting nine-peak orbits and a nearby eighteen-peak orbit. At the core, the complete state and activation waveform repeat every $37.63637538$ time units; the dominant transverse Floquet multiplier is $0.06506$, alongside the required neutral flow mode. A separately frozen follow-up uses independent initialization and 10,000 units of settling at two integration steps. Its two distant controls retain absent short recurrence and positive finite-time exponents. After a $10^{-6}$ state perturbation and 300 time units, activation-vector separation is $1.53\times10^{-6}$ at the core versus $1.41$ and $2.13$ at those controls. Thus the geometry accompanies a large, directly measured difference in neural waveform repeatability. The original near-edge exterior fails its intended interpretation and is retained: refinement makes it periodic, and the follow-up independently verifies that orbit. These are finite-horizon numerical observations in a fixed-weight recurrent network. The next utility test is whether window geometry predicts tolerance to connection-weight drift beyond local cycle diagnostics; task benefit has not yet been measured.
\end{abstract}

\section{The measured object}
Figure~\ref{fig:shrimp} is a map of the neural network itself. Its controls are two connection weights, and its colors describe recurring sequences of the complete three-neuron state. The focused window has a broad nine-peak body and narrow extensions; selected nine- and eighteen-peak trajectories pass independent orbit checks. This is a numerical reproduction of the small-network precedent of \citet{dasilva2022}, not a first report of neural shrimps. The term refers to periodic-window organization in a control plane \citep{gallas1993}; the corresponding trajectories evolve in state space.

Our question is what this geometry means for the parent network. We therefore place recurrence, sensitivity, neuron potentials, and actual tanh activations side by side. Modern training maps and latent-reasoning basin maps motivate the broader question \citep{sohldickstein2024,lai2026}, but the present result concerns the fixed-weight continuous-time system below.

\section{Network, scan, and orbit verification}
\subsection{The parent network and its readout}
The model is the asymmetric three-neuron system in \citet{dasilva2022}:
\begin{equation}
 \dot x=-x+W\tanh(x),\qquad
 W=\begin{pmatrix}
 1.5 & 2.9 & \alpha\\
 -1.5 & 1.18 & 0\\
 \gamma & -22 & 0.47
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad x\in\mathbb R^3.
\label{eq:network}
\end{equation}
The coordinates $x_i$ are neuron potentials, and $u_i=\tanh(x_i)$ are their activation outputs. The weights $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ respectively couple neurons $3\to1$ and $1\to3$. They are constant during a run. Thus the experiment changes network connectivity between settings, not a learning rate while training.

\subsection{A frozen exploratory reproduction}
The overview spans $\alpha\in[1.055,1.110]$ and $\gamma\in[10.1,11.3]$ at $81^2$ settings. The focus spans $[1.056,1.080]\times[10.14,10.55]$ at $181^2$ settings. Classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta uses $\Delta t=0.02$, a settling time of 2,000, and a measurement time of 800. Each increasing-$\alpha$ row begins at $x=(0.1,0.1,0.1)$ and then continues from the previous setting's terminal state. Rows are independent. The focused rectangle was selected from the published figure before outcomes existed.

A separate transect fixes $\alpha=1.064$ and samples 201 values of $\gamma\in[10.14,10.55]$. Each point independently starts from the same prescribed state, using $\Delta t=0.01$, settling time 4,000, and measurement time 1,000. It therefore checks whether the main observed structure survives a common initialization without inheriting grid continuation.

A maximum of $x_3$ defines an event, localized by cubic Hermite interpolation of the numerical endpoints. The retained event includes time and all three state coordinates. The screen searches the last 128 events for four repetitions of a primitive sequence of at most 32 peaks, with absolute tolerance $2\times10^{-6}$ and relative tolerance $2\times10^{-5}$. A zero label means no short period passed this screen. Finite-time Lyapunov estimates evolve one tangent vector, discard 100 units of alignment, and normalize growth by the remaining measurement time. They are finite-horizon measurements; a stable autonomous cycle has a neutral tangent direction.

\subsection{Verification of selected trajectories}
The frozen rules select the period-nine candidate nearest $(1.064,10.26)$, the farthest member of its eight-connected period-nine component, the nearest period-eighteen candidate, and a nearby no-short-period cell with positive finite-time exponent. All four are re-integrated from their captured terminal states at $\Delta t=0.01$ and $0.005$, each with settling time 4,000 and measurement time 1,200. These refinements test the captured regime, not basin independence. Original labels and changed classifications are both retained.

Independent adaptive DOP853 shooting solves $\phi_T(x_0)=x_0$ with an $x_3$-maximum phase condition, relative state closure at most $10^{-8}$, and the expected primitive peak count. Variational integration gives $M(T)=D\phi_T(x_0)$. One multiplier must be neutral and aligned with the flow; both transverse multipliers must have modulus below $1-10^{-5}$. Neutral error and flow residual must each be at most $10^{-6}$, with alignment at least $0.99$. Solver tolerances are $10^{-10}$ relative and $10^{-12}$ absolute. These are numerical orbit certificates, not interval-arithmetic proofs.

\subsection{The full waveform and the effect of saturation}
At the verified core, the complete state repeats every $37.63637538$ time units, with nine maxima of $x_3$ in that cycle. The separately integrated behavior probe reports one-period state repetition RMS $8.15\times10^{-10}$ from 2,248 dense-output pairs in its final half. Figure~\ref{fig:waveforms} displays every neuron and its output, so the parameter label is connected directly to network motion.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\caption{\textbf{Neuron potentials, activations, and paired perturbations.} The core is a numerically verified attracting cycle. The complete activation readout is componentwise injective for finite states:
$\tanh(x(t+T))=\tanh(x(t))$ exactly when $x(t+T)=x(t)$. It therefore preserves the full waveform's primitive period. Saturation changes amplitude and numerical visibility, not this exact correspondence. At the core, neuron 3's potential spans approximately $-16.20$ to $16.20$ in the recorded behavior window, while its activation approaches $\pm1$. Over the final 150--300 time units of the 300-unit probe, its population potential and activation standard deviations are respectively $8.664$ and $0.9876$; these have different units and are reported separately.

The core's dominant transverse multiplier is $0.06506$, while the outer point's is $-0.46514$. These describe different local perturbation responses even though both have nine peaks. They do not estimate basin volume, noise tolerance, or recovery from an arbitrary disturbance. The selected outer point reaches the right edge of the focus rectangle, so it cannot establish where that extension terminates. A nearby eighteen-peak orbit is consistent with doubled periodic organization, but this experiment does not continue an exact bifurcation branch connecting the two.

\subsection{The exterior control exposes a finite-horizon error}
The coarse-grid exterior has no detected short period and a finite-time exponent of $0.00558$. Both refinements instead detect a nine-peak sequence. Its captured behavior therefore cannot support an inside-versus-chaos claim. The change could reflect longer settling, numerical refinement, or their combination; the design changes both and does not isolate the cause. This failure is especially relevant near window boundaries, where a finite map can mistake a long transient for persistent irregular dynamics.

The perturbation traces also require the flow interpretation. At the core, a $10^{-6}$ perturbation reaches maximum same-time state separation $8.53\times10^{-5}$ over 300 units, despite the attracting transverse multipliers. A neutral phase displacement and transient amplification can coexist with orbital attraction. The saved nearest-orbit distances include finite orbit-sampling error and are not exact phase projections. An inference of useful disturbance rejection requires a dedicated phase-aware measurement.

\section{Independent controls show a large activity contrast}
EXP-005 is a prospective follow-up chosen after the EXP-004 failure and frozen before its own outcomes. It fixes the earlier core, the two endpoints of the independent transect, and the failed near-edge comparison. Each point independently restarts at $(0.1,0.1,0.1)$ at both $\Delta t=0.01$ and $0.005$, settles for 10,000 time units, and is measured for 2,000. The finite-time exponent uses the final 1,900 units after tangent alignment. The two distant controls meet the frozen irregularity rule at both steps: no short recurrence and exponent above $0.005$. This supports persistent irregularity over the tested horizons, not an asymptotic-chaos proof.

The core again has a verified nine-peak orbit: $T=37.63637538$, relative closure $8.55\times10^{-11}$, and transverse spectral radius $0.06506$. The failed near-edge point also receives an independent nine-peak certificate, with $T=37.33149959$, closure $6.46\times10^{-10}$, and transverse spectral radius $0.85603$. EXP-005 requires agreement between its two fresh runs; it does not require agreement with the original coarse grid. Its new certificate therefore does not erase the EXP-004 failure.

From each finest-step terminal state, the same $10^{-6}$ state perturbation is applied in direction $(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$. Figure~\ref{fig:controls} and Table~\ref{tab:controls} show the resulting 300-unit probes. The core's final activation separation is $1.53\times10^{-6}$, versus $1.41$ and $2.13$ at the lower and upper controls; their maximum state separations reach $29.67$ and $31.87$. These are actual changes in the network's outputs, with a contrast of about six orders of magnitude at the final checkpoint. They measure same-time repeatability for one declared direction and phase per setting, not population robustness, exact phase-aligned recovery, or useful task performance.

\section{What the neural shrimp tells us, and what to test next}
The shrimp selects a repeating activation waveform and accompanies a strong difference in measured sensitivity. Whether to enter or avoid it depends on the desired computation: a rhythm generator could use a periodic operating region, while a solver intended to settle to one answer could be impeded by cycling. No task endpoint is built into this network.

The clearest untested use of the \emph{shape} is tolerance to changes in connection weights. A transverse Floquet multiplier describes a small change of \emph{state at fixed weights}; window width describes where periodic organization persists as \emph{weights change}. Moving a network state does not move its weights across the shrimp map. The next tests should therefore separate those two disturbances:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Does the shape predict tolerance to weight drift?} Continue the verified branches and locate stability boundaries, retaining failures and unstable segments. At prospectively chosen body, extension, and boundary settings, perturb the two weights in matched directions and test multiple initial states and phases. Report doses both in raw units and a frozen dimensionless metric. Test whether boundary distance or window width predicts regime loss and waveform error beyond current period, amplitude, clean waveform quality, and Floquet multipliers on held-out settings.
\item \textbf{Can this improve rhythm control?} Freeze a reference activation waveform, allowable phase error, and separate evaluation disturbance streams. Compare a controller using geometry with ordinary parameter search and controllers given the same local cycle diagnostics, actions, observations, and total resources. Include random directions, a shuffled-boundary control, and zero-dose numerical checks. Measure time within waveform/phase tolerance, recovery, effort, and total cost including the map. Separately perturb states at fixed weights and measure phase drift and phase-aligned orbital recovery.
\item \textbf{Does the mechanism transfer?} Reproduce one small training or reasoning model before testing entry, exit, averaging, or stopping. Its full evolving state must include optimizer buffers or secondary latents as appropriate. Measure held-out predictions, correctness, settling time, and cost; recurrence alone does not establish useful computation.
\end{enumerate}

The present map reproduces recognizable shrimp-shaped organization and verifies selected neural orbits; it does not certify every pixel or the exact boundary topology. Compared with the published $1000\times1000$ scans, $\Delta t=0.01$, and settling time 10,000, our scan has lower resolution and shorter settling. Source initial states and row-reset conventions were unspecified; ours are explicit. The printed source transect equation appeared inconsistent with its displayed range, so we used the independently frozen vertical transect. These differences delimit reproduction rather than being hidden numerical substitutions.
