\begin{abstract}
Large-step gradient descent can sustain oscillatory training after fixed-point convergence becomes unstable.
We study how these dynamics are organized in learning-rate planes of small neural networks, combining
full-state orbit refinement, stability calculations and exploratory bifurcation analysis.

Ball-arithmetic certificates prove local existence, primitive period and full-state attraction for
\nCOneOrbits{} selected period-\nCOnePeriod{} orbits in a 13-parameter tanh network and a period-\nSOneRCertPeriod{} orbit in a \nSOneRCertDimension{}-parameter tanh network, at fixed layer-specific learning rates for the exact-real maps.
A separate certificate establishes a local supercritical Neimark--Sacker bifurcation in one scalar unequal-rate model.

A prospectively designed map study, independently cross-checked in a separate numerical implementation,
identifies \nConfirmed{} attracting periodic windows in two tanh networks with separate layer learning rates.
Their surrounding cells pass finite-horizon chaos diagnostics. Among cells labeled convergent by a published
loss-reduction score, \pNotFixedMin{} to \pNotFixedMax{} are not classified as fixed points after
\nUpdates{} updates. The score therefore combines several dynamical regimes.

Exploratory calculations identify a local organizing mechanism at selected period-two orbits: a complex pair of multipliers
crosses the unit circle, with a rotation angle that predicts the ensuing oscillation frequency.
The first Lyapunov coefficient predicts soft versus hard onset in \nDynConsistent{} of \nDynChecks{} tested
cases. Related crossings occur in a network trained on digit images and in low-dimensional factorization
models. An explicit scalar control isolates how unequal rates permit complex two-step dynamics. Near one predicted resonance, a locked region narrows with the expected asymptotic scaling, but its
connection to a selected distant window remains unresolved and the prespecified ancestry test did not pass.

Rotation features provide no robust additional prediction of loss or disagreement magnitude in the tested
pilots; adapted analyses retain locking effects on perturbation response and learning-rate-decay timing.
Loss-based estimation of the local complex multiplier is a candidate diagnostic whose prospective utility
remains untested. The results do not establish
a general route to chaos, a language-model benefit, or computer-assisted proofs of the reported window census.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Full-batch gradient descent can continue reducing neural-network training loss beyond the local quadratic
stability threshold. In the edge-of-stability regime observed by \citet{cohen2021}, sharpness stays near
$2/\eta$ while short-term loss fluctuations coexist with longer-term decrease. Related analyses address
self-stabilization under local regularity and spectral assumptions \citep{damian2023}, progressive sharpening
in second-order regression models \citep{agarwala2023}, and initial loss growth followed by convergence in the
catapult regime \citep{lewkowycz2020}. These descriptions do not define a universal sequence of training phases. Simplified models show period doubling and chaos as the step size grows
\citep{kalra2025,chen2024phases,hofmann2026}. Numerical studies report fractal trainability or loss-convergence
boundaries in selected learning-rate planes \citep{sohldickstein2024,torkamandi2025,liu2024}.
Related factorization results concern convergence regions in initialization space \citep{liang2026}.
Such success criteria do not by themselves distinguish full-state dynamical regimes.

We ask what the dynamics inside such maps are. We treat full-batch gradient descent with separate learning rates
for the hidden and output layers as a two-parameter family of maps on parameter space. For every cell of a
learning-rate grid we screen the parameter state at the end of a long run, refine selected periodic orbits with
Newton's method using an analytic Jacobian evaluated in floating-point arithmetic, and follow orbits and their stability boundaries through the plane.

\paragraph{Thesis and status.} We organize the results around one thesis: beyond the edge of stability, the
period-2 oscillation gives way to quasi-periodic training on an invariant circle, rational rotation numbers lock
into periodic windows, and the windows of the learning-rate plane are largely these locked orbits. Each result
below says exactly what passed. The cross-checked windows and the comparison with the binary score are confirmed.
Exploratory analyses support the local part of the thesis: complex-pair crossings occur in independently
initialized slices, the sampled crossings are mostly supercritical with dynamics that match the normal form, quasi-periodic
training starts with a rotation equal to the crossing angle, and the same crossing appears in a real-data network
and in a linear network. The global part is not established: a frozen test did not confirm that a rule-selected
window descends from its resonance, and no prespecified path into sustained chaos has been tested. We call the
crossings Neimark--Sacker candidates except where the nondegeneracy coefficient and dynamical checks were computed.

\paragraph{Prior work.} Periodic windows, period doubling, positive Lyapunov exponents and broadband power
spectra in learning dynamics, with the learning rate as bifurcation parameter, date back at least to
\citet{vandermaas1990}, who studied a three-unit autoassociator. Their delay-embedded chaotic attractors are thin
folded curves, effectively one-dimensional chaos of the period-doubling kind, unlike the invariant circles of the
two-step map studied here. Period-colored maps over optimizer hyperparameters have been drawn from a
two-dimensional projection of the error, without full-state verification \citep{sveleba2026}. \citet{hofmann2026}
studies finite-step gradient descent as a dynamical system in solvable models: the edge of stability as the first
(flip) bifurcation of the training map, the period-doubling route with windows, and, in a two-mode depth-two linear
network, a ``rotational edge'' beyond which the unstable multipliers of the aligned 2-cycle are complex and the
system saturates in a persistently misaligned oscillatory state. The edge is Hofmann's result; we show, in that
model and in nonlinear networks, numerical evidence for post-edge closed curves whose rotation approaches
the crossing angle, and for locking; the linear model requires explicit care with its continuous symmetry.
Empirical riddling-like sensitivity and learning-rate-dependent reproducibility under nearby-initialization
retraining are reported by \citet{ly2025}. Invariant curves and phase-locking Arnold tongues appear in a
multiplicative-weights learning rule for population games \citep{metaxas2026}, and chaos in learning a two-person
game goes back to \citet{sato2002}; both concern games rather than descent on one loss. For heavy-ball momentum, \citet{goujaud2025} construct
cycles on smooth strongly convex two-dimensional objectives that advance by $1/K$ of a turn per update.

\paragraph{This paper's contribution.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Computer-assisted proofs from exploratory selection: our \nCOneOrbits{} primitive period-\nCOnePeriod{} orbits in 13 dimensions and one primitive period-\nSOneRCertPeriod{} orbit in \nSOneRCertDimension{} dimensions are locally attracting in the full parameter state, at fixed layer-specific rates for the exact-real tanh maps. We also prove one local supercritical NS of a scalar period-2 orbit. The certificates have independent ball-arithmetic reviews.
\item Confirmed: \nConfirmed{} numerically validated attracting windows embedded in chaos in the learning-rate planes of two
small networks, repeated by an independent audit, and a cell-by-cell comparison showing that the published binary
score conflates fixed points, periodic orbits, chaos and drift.
\item Exploratory, local mechanism: a merged locus of slice-wise period-two complex-pair crossings; pointwise first
Lyapunov coefficient, candidate generalized Neimark--Sacker points and dynamical checks; rotation-number maps in which
quasi-periodic training begins at the crossing angle.
\item Exploratory, generality: the same crossing with a quantitatively predicted rotation in a real-data network
with one learning rate; soft onset and locking in symmetry-reduced observables beyond the rotational edge of a
linear network; distinct continuation outcomes from two bootstrap procedures and a numerically consistent local period-2 segment in a 72-parameter network.
\item Reported negatives: a frozen ancestry test that did not pass; no locking resolved in the real-data network;
no robust incremental value of rotation information for reproducibility or loss.
\item Code, receipts and a verification script that re-derive every figure, table and number.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}
\item No route to chaos: no prespecified connected path through sustained chaos has been tested.
\item No proof that the windows are Arnold tongues: the Birkhoff test is a diagnostic of projected orbits, and the
frozen ancestry test did not pass.
\item No claim about stochastic, minibatch or large-scale training, or other architectures and data beyond those
shown, and no training benefit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gradient descent as a family of maps}
\label{sec:background}

Let $L(\theta)$ be the full-batch training loss of a network with parameters $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^n$, split
into hidden-layer and output-layer blocks. Gradient descent with per-layer learning rates is the map
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1} = F(\theta_t) = \theta_t - P\,\nabla L(\theta_t), \qquad
P = \operatorname{diag}(\eta_h \mathbf{1}_{\text{hidden}}, \eta_o \mathbf{1}_{\text{output}}),
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
a family of maps indexed by the two rates $(\eta_h,\eta_o)$. Its Jacobian is $DF(\theta) = I - P H(\theta)$
with $H$ the Hessian of $L$. An orbit of period $p$ is stable when all eigenvalues (multipliers) of
$DF(\theta_{p-1})\cdots DF(\theta_0)$ lie inside the unit circle. Generically it loses stability when a
multiplier crosses the circle: at $+1$ (a fold candidate, subject to nondegeneracy and symmetry checks),
at $-1$ (a flip candidate) or as a complex pair $e^{\pm 2\pi i\omega}$ (a Neimark--Sacker candidate).
For a simple transverse complex crossing with all other multipliers strictly inside the unit circle,
no strong resonance $e^{2\pi i k\omega}=1$ for $k=1,2,3,4$, and nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient $d$, a
Neimark--Sacker bifurcation creates an invariant closed curve: attracting with amplitude growing like the square
root of the distance beyond the crossing when $d<0$ (supercritical), repelling and present before the crossing
when $d>0$ (subcritical) \citep{kuznetsovsacker2008}.

\paragraph{Why the candidates start at period 2.} $DF(\theta)$ is similar to the symmetric matrix
$I - P^{1/2} H P^{1/2}$, so at a fixed point all multipliers are real and local stability can be lost only through a real
unit-circle crossing at $+1$ or $-1$. The $+1$ case need not be a fold; symmetry and other degeneracies
can produce different bifurcations. At a nondegenerate minimum under a common positive scaling of $P$,
the first instability is a $-1$ crossing. The edge-of-stability period-2 branch is associated with that flip. For a period-2 orbit
$\{\theta_a,\theta_b\}$, the two-step Jacobian is similar to $S_b S_a$ with
$S_j = I - P^{1/2} H(\theta_j) P^{1/2}$. A product of two symmetric matrices has real eigenvalues when one factor
is positive definite (also when one is negative definite). Along post-edge cycles the factors can instead be
indefinite and fail to commute, allowing complex multipliers. Indefiniteness alone is not a sufficient condition. This is an elementary observation; complex unstable
multipliers of an edge-of-stability 2-cycle were derived for a two-mode linear network by \citet{hofmann2026}.

\paragraph{Circle maps and phase locking.} On an invariant circle the dynamics has a rotation number $\rho$,
the mean fraction of a turn per iterate. Rational rotation $m/q$ can produce a phase-locked periodic orbit.
Near a nondegenerate weak resonance ($q\geq5$), an appropriate resonant normal form predicts a tongue whose
width in angular detuning scales with radial detuning to the power $(q-2)/2$, when the leading resonant
coefficient is nonzero. This local scaling does not fix the width along arbitrary distant parameter slices.
Analytic circle maps sufficiently close to rotation admit analytic conjugacy to a rigid rotation under
arithmetic conditions on their irrational rotation number \citep{arnold1965}. Classical circle-map families exhibit devil's staircases
\citep{jensen1983,jensen1984}; these are hypotheses to test in a learning-rate plane, not automatic
consequences of a complex-pair crossing. Breakdown of an invariant circle is one possible route to chaos
\citep{curry1978,aronson1982,ostlund1983}. Locally near the period-2 bifurcation, $F$ exchanges two disjoint
components and a period-$q$ locked orbit of $F^2$ has primitive period $2q$ for $F$. This relation follows from
the local setting and is not independent evidence of resonance ancestry.

\paragraph{Optimizer state and momentum.} Our map study concerns plain gradient descent. For standard heavy ball,
$x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta\nabla L(x_t)+\beta(x_t-x_{t-1})$, each Hessian eigenvalue $h$ at a stationary point gives
\begin{equation}
\mu^2-(1+\beta-\eta h)\mu+\beta=0.
\end{equation}
At fixed $0<\beta<1$, a complex pair has modulus $\sqrt\beta<1$ and cannot cross the unit circle as $\eta$
varies. A nondegenerate minimum first loses local stability through a flip at
$\eta h_{\max}=2(1+\beta)$. A nonsymmetric augmented Jacobian therefore does not, by itself, imply a fixed-point
Neimark--Sacker mechanism. Periodic branches and adaptive optimizers require their own full-state analysis,
including moment variables and decay. Bias correction, schedules and minibatches introduce time dependence or
randomness; an instantaneous Jacobian along such a trajectory is not automatically a Floquet matrix.

\paragraph{Shrimps.} In two-parameter families of dissipative maps, periodic windows inside chaos often take a
characteristic ``shrimp'' shape, with a primary periodic body bounded by fold and flip curves and adjoining
period-doubling regions \citep{gallas1993,gallas1994}. We test for this
anatomy explicitly rather than assume it.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\input{generated-design-table.tex}

\paragraph{Networks and data.} Table~\ref{tab:design} lists the design of the map study. S1 reproduces the
published mean-field configuration of the fractal-trainability study \citep{sohldickstein2024}; S1R adds weight
decay; S2 is a 13-parameter tanh regressor fitted to points from a random tanh teacher. We refer to a network and
seed together, for example S2-28011. Training is deterministic full-batch gradient descent in float64.

\paragraph{Three different quantities.} The published binary score measures loss reduction: a run counts as
convergent when its loss, normalized by the initial loss and averaged over the last 20 of the first
\nScoreHorizon{} updates, is below one \citep{sohldickstein2024}. Our screening labels classify the parameter state
at the end of a finite run. Orbit refinement tests individual periodic orbits against numerical tolerances. The three need not agree, and we
compare them cell by cell.

\paragraph{Screening.} Every cell runs \nUpdates{} updates from the seed's initial parameters. The last \nTail{}
updates are screened for the smallest full-state recurrence period up to \nMaxPeriod{}: a period $p$ requires the
state to recur with componentwise tolerance $\xScreenAtol + \xScreenRtol\,|\theta|$ over the whole tail, which
spans at least \nMinRepeats{} repeats. The largest finite-time Lyapunov exponent (FTLE) is averaged over the last
\nFtleSteps{} updates after \nFtleAlign{} updates of tangent alignment. Labels are fixed point, periodic, chaotic
(FTLE above \nChaosThreshold{} per update), drift (parameter norm still growing by more than \pDrift{} over the FTLE
window), unresolved, or divergent (non-finite or larger than $\xDivergence$).

\paragraph{Numerically validated.} An orbit of period $p$ is numerically validated when damped Newton iteration
on $F^p(\theta)-\theta=0$, using the exact chain-rule Jacobian of $F^p$, reaches an undamped relative correction
below $\xCertNewton$ within \nCertIterations{} iterations; the converged point closes to within
$\xCertAtol + \xCertRtol\,|\theta|$ in every component; it lies within $\xCertCandidate$ (relative) of the
screened candidate; every proper-divisor iterate is separated from it by at least $\xCertDivisor$ (relative), so
the period is primitive; and it is attracting when the spectral radius of the monodromy matrix is below
$1-\xCertMargin$. These checks use floating-point arithmetic and stated tolerances; they do not enclose rounding error.
A reviewed correction to complex left-eigenvector pairing affects a separate sensitivity diagnostic. The
audit found no affected saved orbit decision or criticality coefficient; original records and the precise
exception-handling limitation are preserved in \path{docs/complex-left-condition-impact.md}.

\paragraph{Embedded windows and audit.} A connected component of equal-period cells qualifies as an embedded
window when its period is at least \nGateMinPeriod{}, it has at least \nMinComponent{} cells, no cell of its
exterior ring is a fixed point or has a proper-divisor period, at least \pRingFraction{} of the ring is
rerun-confirmed chaos (reruns of \nRingSteps{} updates with a \nRingFtle{}-update FTLE and a different tangent
vector, up to \nRingCap{} ring cells per component), and its representative orbit passes the numerical orbit and attraction checks.
The audit was a separate program that imports neither the batched engine, the orbit-refinement implementation nor the selection
helpers: it rewrote the one-cell map, the recurrence screen, the zoom rule and the component and ring rules,
rechecked every numerical orbit record with finite-difference Jacobians (all \nAuditAllOk{} of \nAuditChecked{} pass closure,
primitivity, stability and seed checks; spectral radii agree within $\xAuditMaxRadiusDiff$), re-simulated the
ring cells of every qualifying component, recomputed the gate on its own evidence, and found \nAuditMismatched{}
mismatches among the \nAuditReceiptFiles{} receipt files.

\paragraph{Continuation, boundary type and criticality.} From the center of a window, its numerically validated orbit is
continued along 64 rays in the rate plane until it loses stability, located by bisection, and the boundary is
typed by the crossing multiplier: fold candidate ($+1$), flip candidate ($-1$) or complex-pair candidate.
These multiplier labels alone do not establish the corresponding nondegeneracy conditions. The same procedure traces where the
period-2 orbit loses stability along slices of fixed output rate. Each slice is initialized separately; sorting
and deduplicating their crossings gives a merged locus, without establishing full-state branch identity across
output rates. At each selected slice-wise crossing we compute the first Lyapunov coefficient $d$ of the Neimark--Sacker normal form with
Kuznetsov's formula for $n$-dimensional maps, using the analytic dense two-step Jacobian evaluated in float64 and Richardson-extrapolated
differences of analytic Jacobian-vector products for the higher derivatives; the implementation reproduces the
explicit planar formula on random planar maps.

\paragraph{Rotation numbers and the Birkhoff test.} For a non-periodic cell we take the two-step samples
$z_k=\theta_{2k}$, project them on their first two principal axes, and define the rotation number as the mean
unwrapped angle increment in turns per two steps. Rotation-number maps run this on every cell of a grid (float64
on a GPU, \nUpdates{} screening updates followed by 6{,}000 two-step samples). For a numerically validated orbit of period $p$
we use $F^2$ for even $p$ ($q=p/2$ points) and $F$ for odd $p$ ($q=p$ points), sort them by angle in their principal
plane, and say the orbit passes when every step advances the angular rank by the same shift $m$ with $\gcd(m,q)=1$,
the ordering of a Birkhoff orbit of rotation $m/q$. The number of points $q$ is fixed by the period and the chosen iterate; the relation $p=2q$
applies only to the even-period case; what the test measures is the ordering and the shift. Orbits with 2 or 3 points pass by
construction. The null is the pass probability of the same test for uniformly random angles; for a group of orbits
we report the expected number of chance passes. The refined test also orders points along the shortest closed tour
through them and tests the points of $F^{2^{k+1}}$ ($k=1,2,3$), which can detect orbits born by period doubling
inside a locked window; it counts only tests on at least 5 points, and its null for each orbit is the sum of the
per-test pass probabilities (a union bound), so the summed null is an upper bound on the expected number of chance
passes.

\paragraph{Resonance ancestry.} For a window selected by a fixed rule (the $1/5$ window of S2-28011), we locate the
points of the crossing curve whose angle is $1/5$, check the onset there, and follow the locked period-10 interval
on six cross-sections at distances $0.0128/4^k$ beyond the curve, finding both of its numerical fold-candidate edges on each. Acceptance
rules were frozen before the network run: controls must pass first; the interval must be bounded by numerical fold candidates on every
level with shrinking width and amplitude; and a linear fit of its center over all levels must extrapolate to within
$\xAncTolerance$ decades of the predicted point. A second stage tries to connect the tracked interval to the window.

\section{Computer-assisted proofs of selected orbits and a local bifurcation}
\label{sec:certificates}

\noindent Status: exploratory target selection, evidence rung 3 for the statements below.
\subsection{Attracting periodic orbits}

The floating-point census motivates a separate proof task. For \nCOneOrbits{} selected S2 settings with fixed layer-specific learning rates, Arb ball
arithmetic encloses a unique zero of $H(x)=F^{\nCOnePeriod}(x)-x$ in a specified initial box, excludes all proper
divisors of \nCOnePeriod{}, and proves attraction. One target, S2-28011 component 140, belongs to the
historically qualifying window set. The other, S2-28011-zoom component 936, was the initial feasibility choice;
it does not qualify for that historical window claim. Its valid orbit proof is retained with that distinction.

The theorem concerns real arithmetic with the supplied dataset, rates, ridge and loss scale interpreted as
exact dyadic constants. It does not assert a periodic orbit of the binary64 implementation. An analytic ball
Jacobian encloses the derivative throughout the initial box and its iterated images. With an exact dyadic
approximate inverse $R$, strict Krawczyk inclusion and
$\|I-RH'(X)\|_\infty<1$ establish contraction of $x\mapsto x-RH(x)$ on $X$ and the nonsingularity of $R$.
Disjoint coordinate enclosures of $F^d(X)$ and $X$ exclude each proper divisor $d$. An interval monodromy $M$
and repeated squaring give
\begin{equation}
\|M_{\mathrm{window}}^{\nCOnePower}\|_\infty < \xCOneWindowBound{}\quad\text{and}\quad
\|M_{\mathrm{feas}}^{\nCOnePower}\|_\infty < \xCOneFeasibilityBound{},
\end{equation}
respectively, implying spectral radius below one at each enclosed orbit. The initial sequential-power attempt
failed to enclose attraction because of interval wrapping; that inconclusive attempt and the disclosed
repeated-squaring amendment remain available.

An independent review reproduced the certificates, checked historical input bindings and stored witnesses,
compared the analytic derivatives with separate second-order automatic differentiation of the objective, and
repeated the proofs with higher precision on smaller nested boxes. The proof trusts the mathematical
contraction argument, implementation and Arb/FLINT enclosure library; it is not formalized in a proof assistant.
It establishes neither surrounding chaos nor window geometry, invariant circles or training utility.
The authoritative artifacts and offline replay are documented in \path{certificates/README.md}.

The same unchanged checker also proves a primitive period-\nSOneRCertPeriod{} orbit in the full
\nSOneRCertDimension{}-dimensional S1R-28002 map, selected from the saved attracting segment described below.
For its enclosed two-step monodromy,
\begin{equation}
\|M_{\mathrm{S1R}}^{\nSOneRCertPower}\|_\infty < \xSOneRCertBound < 1.
\end{equation}
The independent outcome audit binds the original data, rates and selected phase to their exact dyadic
interpretations, replays every proof enclosure, and passes a separately declared whole-box proximity test
to that saved phase. Proximity does not assert that the saved floating-point state lies inside the proof box,
nor does it prove branch identity or continuous parameter continuation. This audit shares the previously

\subsection{A supercritical NS in the scalar unequal-rate model}
\label{sec:scalar-proof}

Consider $L(u,v)=\tfrac12(uv-d)^2+\tfrac\lambda2(u^2+v^2)$, with
\begin{equation}
d=\frac{5404319552844595}{2^{52}},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{5404319552844595}{2^{54}},\qquad
(\eta_u,\eta_v)=(\eta,4\eta).
\end{equation}
These are the exact dyadic interpretations of the pilot's nominal target 1.2 and ridge 0.3.
This selected case is separate from the illustrative amplitude-scaling example in Section~\ref{sec:generality}.
Let $G_\eta=F_\eta^2$. A three-variable Krawczyk inclusion encloses a unique root of
$(G_\eta(x)-x,\det DG_\eta(x)-1)=0$ in the recorded box, whose coordinate radius is
$2^{-40}+2^{-69}$. Disjoint enclosures of $F_\eta(x)$ and $x$ prove primitive period two.
The crossing rate obeys $\eta_*\in\tNSProofEta$.

At that root the complete planar monodromy has determinant exactly one and trace in
$\tNSProofTrace$; the trace enclosure excludes $-2,-1,0,2$, hence resonances of orders one through four.
Invertibility of $DG_\eta-I$ gives an implicit local orbit branch. Along it,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d|\mu|}{d\eta}\bigg|_{\eta_*}&\in\tNSProofSlope\subset(0,\infty),\\
\ell=\operatorname{Re}(\overline\mu c_1)&\in\tNSProofCoeff\subset(-\infty,0).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The right eigenvector has Euclidean norm one and its left row is normalized to pair to one with it
and to zero with its conjugate. Tensor/resolvent and planar scalar-coefficient formulas both enclose a
negative $\ell$. Together these bounds meet the generic supercritical NS theorem hypotheses
\citep{kuznetsovsacker2008} for this polynomial map.

For $\eta>\eta_*$ sufficiently close to the crossing, the theorem supplies a qualitative local attracting
invariant-curve consequence for $G_\eta$; under $F_\eta$ the two local components are exchanged.
It supplies no specified finite detuning, irrational rotation, global ancestry, basin size or training benefit.
The certificate concerns exact-real dynamics and trusts Arb/FLINT and the stated theorems.
Independent review uses rational polynomial differentiation, higher-precision nested enclosures and a separate
cubic normal-form calculation. The source-bound receipt and scope are in
\path{certificates/scalar-ns-receipt.json} and \path{certificates/scalar-ns-design.md}.
The review separately checked the original seed binding; the frozen replay alone does not authenticate
arbitrarily rewritten provenance metadata. This limitation and the independent checks are recorded in

\paragraph{Exact rescaling of the same case.}
Let $d_0$ denote the certified target above. For $d>0$, $\lambda=qd$, fixed rate ratio $r$,
and $S_k(x)=kx$ with $k=\sqrt{d/d_0}$, substitution in the cubic GD map gives
\begin{equation}
F_{d,qd;\eta,r}\circ S_k=S_k\circ F_{d_0,qd_0;k^2\eta,r}.
\end{equation}
Thus the certificate at $q=1/4$, $r=4$ transfers to every $d>0$ on that ray, with
$\eta_*(d)=\eta_*(d_0)d_0/d$ and orbit coordinates scaled by $k$.
At corresponding phases the monodromy is unchanged. The total modulus slope along the orbit branch
scales by $k^2$, and the cubic coefficient under the same unit-eigenvector convention scales by $k^{-2}$:
second and third state derivatives of $F^2$ scale by $k^{-1}$ and $k^{-2}$, respectively.
This is one dimensionless case in $(\lambda/d,\eta d,r)$, not an independent replication or
robustness to varying $q$ or $r$. The algebra and independent review are in

\paragraph{Oscillatory onset beyond the rotational edge of a linear network.} In the two-mode depth-two linear network of
\citet{hofmann2026}, the aligned 2-cycle loses stability at his rotational edge $a(s_1+s_2)=2$, which we reproduce to
$\xMMEdgeError$ for $s_2/s_1 = 0.7, 0.8, 0.9$, with crossing angles \tMMBirth{} and a transverse crossing
($d|\mu|/da$ = \tMMTransversality{}). Beyond the edge, rotation-invariant observables show an attracting closed curve of the
two-step dynamics. An exact rotation symmetry leaves a neutral multiplier at one, including with weight decay,
so the generic isolated-orbit Neimark--Sacker theorem requires symmetry reduction here. Without weight decay the second mode's minima form a neutral continuum and the circle amplitude grows
with exponent \tMMSlopeNoWD{}, a degenerate bifurcation; with weight decay 0.01 the exponent is \tMMSlopeWD{}, the
square-root growth consistent with supercritical onset in the symmetry-reduced dynamics, and the rotation number at an offset of
$\xMMSmallestOffset$ is within $\xMMRotationGap$ of the crossing angle (Figure~\ref{fig:generality}B and C). Beyond
the edge the rotation locks at \tMMLockedSeven{} for $s_2/s_1 = 0.7$, and at \tMMLockedEight{} in a fine scan at
$0.8$. The same crossing occurs in the two-weight scalar factorization $\tfrac12(uv-d)^2 + \tfrac12\lambda(u^2+v^2)$
with $\lambda = 0.2$ and unequal layer rates (crossing angle \xMMTwoWeightAngle{}; the circle amplitude grows by a
factor \xMMTwoWeightAmpRatio{} for a fourfold offset, close to the square-root value 2), found as one of

for two held-out couplings were predicted correctly in \nMMHeldoutCorrect{} of \nMMHeldout{} cases.

\paragraph{A scalar mechanism with an explicit equal-rate control.} For
$L(u,v)=\tfrac12(uv-d)^2+\tfrac\lambda2(u^2+v^2)$, $0<\lambda<d$, equal rates preserve $u=v=x$.
Set $c=1+\eta(d-\lambda)$ and $e=1-\eta(d+\lambda)$. The positive balanced 2-cycle for $c>2$ is
\begin{equation}
x_\pm=\frac{\sqrt{c+2}\pm\sqrt{c-2}}{2\sqrt\eta},\qquad
m_\parallel=9-2c^2,\qquad m_\perp=1+e^2+ec.
\end{equation}
The rescaled cubic cycle and longitudinal multiplier recover the balanced scalar formulas in
\citet[Propositions 2--3]{hofmann2026}; the transverse multiplier tests the full two-weight state.
Both multipliers are real, so this branch cannot have an NS crossing; its longitudinal flip is at
$\eta=(\sqrt5-1)/(d-\lambda)$ when the transverse direction remains attracting. Unequal rates can break the
common eigendirections of the symmetric step factors, allowing complex two-step multipliers. This explains a
branch-specific mechanism and does not exclude other equal-rate attractors.

A fixed-target exploratory scan over ridge and rate ratio gives \nScalarFlips{} flips and \nScalarNS{}
complex-pair crossings in \nScalarCases{} followed branches; all sampled equal-rate cases flip.
All complex crossings have negative computed criticality. In the representative case the crossing rate is
$\xScalarEta$, birth rotation $\xScalarRotation$ and coefficient $\xScalarCoeff$; three decreasing detunings give
an amplitude exponent of $\xScalarAmp$. Analytic composed-map derivatives agree with an independent
finite-difference calculation. These are numerical checks of local onset, not a complete phase diagram or a
rigorous invariant-circle theorem for that numerical representative. Section~\ref{sec:scalar-proof} gives a separate local proof for another selected scalar case. The compact receipt is \path{docs/explore-minimal-model-scalar-receipt.json}.

\paragraph{An exact structural reduction, with a negative solver diagnostic.}
For the same fixed-input full-batch model with ridge term $\lambda\|\theta\|^2/2$, write the first-layer weights

preserved in the reduced map and its monodromy is the reduced monodromy direct-summed with $q^pI$.
With a nonzero complementary subspace, an attracting reduced orbit attracts in the full map exactly when

changing input spaces or nonisotropic regularization.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Everything in this section is interpretation. The confirmed results in Section~\ref{sec:confirmed} stand on
their own, and the hypotheses here need their own prespecified tests.

The local part of the thesis now rests on several independent kinds of evidence: the crossing multipliers, the
normal-form coefficient and its dynamical checks, agreement of sampled rotation numbers with corresponding
crossing angles in two maps, and the same behaviour in a real-data network and in a solvable linear network. The selected scalar
case also has a local computer-assisted proof. The picture that fits is
the quasi-periodic organization familiar from circle maps and forced oscillators
\citep{curry1978,aronson1982,jensen1984}: a closed period-2 orbit can lose stability at a
supercritical NS crossing; under the local nondegeneracy assumptions, an attracting invariant circle is born,
with rotation approaching the crossing angle. Rational rotations can lock; and the circle may eventually break; this last step remains untested here. The global part, that the periodic windows of the learning-rate
plane are resonance tongues of this bifurcation, is supported by the Birkhoff test and by the post-hoc tongue
scaling, but its frozen test did not pass. The frozen estimator mixed non-asymptotic and asymptotic levels; a
confirmatory version should prespecify an asymptotic-regime estimator, and connecting a tongue to a distant window
needs two-parameter fold-curve continuation.

An early spectral diagnostic of learning dynamics is that of \citet{vandermaas1990}, who used
broadband spectra to recognize chaotic learning; here a spectral peak carries the rotation number, and an
autoregressive fit estimates the complex multiplier before the crossing in the tested closed-orbit references.

\paragraph{Open questions.} The odd-period windows did not pass the Birkhoff diagnostic; whether they are organized
as in periodicity hubs \citep{jones2012} is open. Fixed-start maps mix local criticality with basin accessibility
from one shared initialization, as the divergent arc shows, so statements about trainability maps need both. In the
real-data network only one of the examined period-2 branches crossed by complex multipliers, and how common such
branches are in larger networks is unknown.

\paragraph{What would constitute transfer to language models?} The practical hypothesis is that a few
unstable or weakly damped modes organize training even when parameter dimension is large. Matrix-free
Jacobian--vector products through the complete optimizer update can estimate those modes without constructing a
dense Jacobian. This hypothesis needs tests of numerical residuals, mode coverage, checkpoint-to-checkpoint drift
and predictive accuracy. Along nonperiodic or scheduled training, finite-horizon tangent growth is the appropriate
initial quantity; Floquet language is reserved for a sufficiently closed periodic reference orbit.
The fixed-batch transformer pilot establishes an architectural test path, but has not yielded a periodic reference
orbit. Neither the drift-aware event calibration nor the separate broader-data continuous-progress pilot has
shown incremental predictive utility. Separate
minibatch, optimizer-state, schedule and precision perturbations remain necessary. Relevant endpoints are prospective transition predictions and improved interventions at comparable
compute cost. Similar-looking loss oscillations would not establish either endpoint. A linear loss-based
multiplier estimate alone also does not identify nonlinear criticality or basin accessibility.

\paragraph{Inference is a separate question.} The bifurcations studied here concern optimizer updates in parameter
space. Freezing weights stops that map; sensitivity of fixed-weight predictions and feedback during generation
require separate measurements. Parameter symmetries can even leave predictions unchanged along weight motion.
A future inference test could ask whether training diagnostics predict sensitivity to arithmetic or weight
perturbations beyond loss and token-score margins. For a unique greedy choice with logit gap $m$, a uniform
logit-error bound $\epsilon<m/2$ preserves that choice at the given prefix. Empirical precision comparisons
are not rigorous error bounds, and divergent generated text alone does not establish chaos. No inference
experiment or benefit is reported here.

\paragraph{The tests that would settle the local claims.} The criticality prediction is a candidate for confirmation on fresh
losses: the sign of $d$ at each crossing predicts a circle of normal-form amplitude beyond a supercritical edge, and
a local unstable-circle scale before a subcritical crossing. The distant state, loss spike, recovery and
hysteresis also depend on global basins, ramp speed and noise and must be tested separately. An existence-and-replication test
would apply this, together with the onset and rotation criteria, to two prespecified independent losses across two
architectures, one of them unused so far, with no substitution. The ancestry test additionally needs a prespecified asymptotic-regime estimator, reliable continuation of
both numerical fold-candidate edges, and rules for unresolved or disconnected branches before protected holdouts are opened.

The immediate research goal is to determine how the longer primitive cycles arise and where their periodic
windows end, using connected continuation and qualified boundary tests. Selected orbit proofs do not establish
complete shrimp geometry or resonance ancestry. Any extension to larger models must demonstrate predictive
value beyond ordinary loss and curvature baselines.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The map study uses networks of 13 and 72 parameters on synthetic data; the real-data result is one branch of one
529-parameter network on one subset and task; the linear network is a solvable model. These map and bifurcation
experiments use deterministic full-batch float64 training. The transformer development pilot uses float32
with matched-state float64 checks and has no accepted periodic orbit. Screening labels are finite-time; chaotic labels rest on single-tangent exponents and
reruns. The orbit census uses floating-point validation; the three selected exact-real attracting orbits and
the scalar local NS in Section~\ref{sec:certificates} have rigorous enclosures. The first Lyapunov coefficient is a local, third-order
quantity; it predicts the birth of a small circle, not how far it survives. Rotation numbers and the Birkhoff test
use two-dimensional projections of curves in many dimensions. Merged crossing loci combine independently
initialized output slices and do not establish branch identity between them or generalized-NS points.
Most mechanism analyses were chosen after seeing
related outcomes. The consequence pilot measures held-out loss and perturbation response in small models, but establishes no
robust training advantage. Transfer to stochastic training, modern language models and useful interventions
remains open.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Edge of stability.} \citet{cohen2021} describe the edge of stability; \citet{damian2023} explain
self-stabilization of sharpness at the edge, whereas we follow what happens after the period-2 oscillation itself
loses stability; \citet{agarwala2023} show progressive sharpening to the edge in second-order regression models,
the approach to the regime we study beyond it; \citet{lewkowycz2020} describe a catapult phase at large rates, in
which the loss first grows and then settles in flatter regions, while our maps resolve the long-run dynamics of
rates where training does not settle. \citet{hofmann2026} derives the rotational edge whose far side we study.
\citet{chenbruna2023} analyze two-step GD, proving period-2 convergence in specified scalar and single-neuron
settings and testing related conditions in larger models. \citet{song2023} study alignment with a reduced
bifurcation diagram, with proofs for specified single-example models and neural-network experiments. Our orbit
tests concern closure and stability of the full parameter state. Further analyses include \citet{kalra2025}.
\citet{chen2024phases} reduce specified quadratic regression settings to a cubic map; their Li--Yorke
chaotic regime can also contain attracting periodic orbits. \citet{cohen2025central} model time-averaged deterministic GD and RMSProp
trajectories with central flows, with explicit limitations on rigor, stochastic training, momentum and
computational feasibility. Our full-state orbit and boundary tests complement this averaged description.

\paragraph{Periodic-orbit guarantees.} \citet{macdonaldvidal2026} give a higher-codimension flip normal form
(Theorem~4.1) and local period-two convergence under geometric hypotheses (Theorems~5.3 and~D.8), including
deep matrix factorization (Section~3.3). Theorem~D.8 constructs a cycle for each point of the manifold of sharpness-minimizing
minima. When that family is positive-dimensional, an individual cycle is not an isolated attractor in every
parameter direction; convergence to a member of the family remains meaningful. Our selected tanh certificates
instead enclose isolated, full-state attracting orbits at fixed layer-specific rates. We make no claim of priority
or a largest certified network.

\paragraph{Adaptive optimizers and larger models.} \citet{cohenadaptive2022} study full-batch and large-batch
adaptive methods at a preconditioned stability threshold. \citet{regischewi2026} model Adam in the joint
parameter--first-moment state, with the second moment as an auxiliary variable, and compare rod-flow
approximations across architectures and optimizers. \citet{fongyang2026} connect second-moment feedback to
active curvature and report gradient reversal, including in GPT-2 medium. The latter uses a fixed text batch
and describes approximate alternating motion around a drifting center; that description is distinct from
full-state periodic-orbit closure. Its minibatch experiments also show that noise and momentum can weaken
the reversal. Cohen et al. already examine transformer warmup, while the rod-flow study explicitly limits
its model to full-batch steady-state behavior. These studies establish relevant
prior work on oscillatory training in larger models. Our proposed extension must identify an additional
mechanism or predictive consequence; detecting oscillation in a transformer alone would not supply that contribution.

\paragraph{Chaos and fractals in training.} \citet{kong2020} analyze deterministic GD on multiscale objectives.
\citet{jimenez2026} relate finite-time perturbation expansion to epochs needed to reach task-specific accuracy
thresholds in full-batch MLP/CNN experiments. This is relevant prior utility evidence; our proposed geometry-specific
benefit needs an additional comparison. \citet{sohldickstein2024} study fractal trainability boundaries;
\citet{torkamandi2025} map loss-based convergence boundaries in a small character transformer trained with Adam;
\citet{liu2024} study learning-rate boundaries for cosine-perturbed quadratic objectives.
\citet{liang2026} analyze initialization-space convergence regions in regularized factorization, with
orthogonality restrictions for their matrix extension. \citet{ly2025} report empirical riddling-like and
functional sensitivity across training runs, including BERT fine-tuning; this concerns outcomes after
retraining from nearby initializations, not chaos during frozen inference. Learning dynamics in
games show chaos \citep{sato2002} and phase locking \citep{metaxas2026}, for adaptive rules rather than descent on
one loss.

\paragraph{Dynamical systems.} Shrimp-shaped windows are described by \citet{gallas1993,gallas1994}; circle maps,
Arnold tongues, devil's staircases and the breakdown of invariant circles by
\citet{arnold1965,jensen1983,jensen1984,curry1978,aronson1982,ostlund1983}; numerical bifurcation analysis of maps,
by \citet{kuznetsov2019}, with the local NS theorem and coefficient stated by
\citet{kuznetsovsacker2008}. Our contribution is the numerical characterization and testing of these mechanisms in the specified
training maps. A literature search cannot establish their absence from all earlier optimizer studies.
