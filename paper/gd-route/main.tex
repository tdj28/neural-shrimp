\begin{abstract}
Full-batch gradient descent with a large learning rate rarely converges to a minimum: it oscillates at the edge
of stability, cycles, or behaves chaotically. Maps of trainability over learning-rate planes summarize this as a
binary label, convergent or divergent, and so do not say which of these behaviors occurs where. We resolve the
dynamics behind such maps in two small tanh networks trained with separate learning rates for their two layers.

(1) In a prospectively designed and independently audited study, the learning-rate planes contain \nConfirmed{}
numerically certified attracting periodic orbits of the full parameter-space update map, with periods from
\nPeriodMin{} to \nPeriodMax{}, whose domains are surrounded mainly by rerun-confirmed chaos. Across six coarse
maps, \pNotFixedMin{} to \pNotFixedMax{} of the cells that a published binary convergence score labels
convergent are not fixed points after \nUpdates{} updates.

(2) In exploratory analyses, the windows follow a quasi-periodic route to chaos. On all \nNSCrossings{} traced
slices whose starting point is the edge-of-stability period-2 orbit, that orbit loses stability through a
Neimark--Sacker bifurcation of the two-step map. On one slice the crossing multiplier predicts a rotation number
of \xBirthAtSlice{}, and the first quasi-periodic cell rotates at \xRhoOnset{}. Of the \nWOConfRefinedOf{}
testable windows, \nWOConfRefined{} are well-ordered circle orbits with rotation $m/q$ and period $2q$ or a
doubling of it, against \xWOConfRefinedNull{} expected for random angles. Only \nDraftShrimp{} of \nConfirmed{}
windows meet draft criteria for classical shrimp anatomy.

Takeaway: in these networks, the region that a binary map calls trainable holds a structured transition from
oscillation to chaos, including quasi-periodic training and phase-locked windows. The mechanism is exploratory
and still needs a test on fresh networks and seeds; nothing here concerns stochastic or large-scale training or
establishes a training benefit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Training with a learning rate beyond the classical stability limit is common practice. At the edge of stability,
the sharpness of the loss hovers near $2/\eta$ while the loss still decreases over long horizons
\citep{cohen2021}, and simplified models show period doubling and chaos as the step size grows
\citep{kalra2025,chen2024phases,hofmann2026}. Over two-dimensional hyperparameter planes, the boundary between
trainable and divergent settings is fractal \citep{sohldickstein2024,torkamandi2025,liu2024,liang2026}. These maps
record whether training stays bounded and settles, not what it does.

We ask what the dynamics inside such maps are. We treat full-batch gradient descent with separate learning rates
for the hidden and output layers as a two-parameter family of maps on parameter space. For every cell of a
learning-rate grid we classify the long-run behavior (fixed point, periodic orbit with its period, chaos, drift,
divergence), certify periodic orbits with Newton's method on the exact Jacobian, and follow selected orbits and
their stability boundaries through the plane.

\paragraph{Prior work.} Periodic windows in one-dimensional learning-rate sweeps date back at least to
\citet{vandermaas1990}. Period-colored maps over optimizer hyperparameters exist without full-state verification
\citep{sveleba2026}. Continuation of flip and catapult thresholds has been carried out for one-dimensional
reductions of gradient descent \citep{hofmann2026}, which also derives complex unstable multipliers of a 2-cycle
in a two-mode linear network. Riddled basins and learning-rate-dependent reproducibility are documented by
\citet{ly2025}. Arnold tongues and Neimark--Sacker bifurcations are reported for learning dynamics in games
\citep{metaxas2026}, and heavy-ball momentum has cycles that rotate by $1/K$ \citep{goujaud2025}.

\paragraph{This paper's contribution.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A period-resolved description of the learning-rate plane of full-batch gradient descent in two small
networks, with \nConfirmed{} certified attracting windows embedded in chaos, confirmed by an independent audit.
\item A direct comparison with the published binary convergence score on the same cells, showing that most
cells it calls convergent do not converge.
\item Exploratory evidence that the windows are organized by a quasi-periodic route: Neimark--Sacker bifurcation
of the period-2 orbit, invariant circles with measured rotation numbers, phase-locked orbits with period $2q$ at
rotation $m/q$, and rotation-number staircases.
\item Open code, receipts and a verification script that re-derives every figure and quoted number.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}
\item No claim about stochastic, minibatch or large-scale training, other architectures or real data (a
real-data test is in progress; Section~\ref{sec:route}).
\item No claim that periodic or quasi-periodic training helps or hurts generalization or speed.
\item The mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:route} is exploratory; it has not yet been tested on fresh networks and
seeds.
\item Classical shrimp organization is shown for a small minority of windows only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gradient descent as a family of maps}
\label{sec:background}

Let $L(\theta)$ be the full-batch training loss of a network with parameters $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^n$, split
into hidden-layer and output-layer blocks. Gradient descent with per-layer learning rates is the map
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1} = F(\theta_t) = \theta_t - P\,\nabla L(\theta_t), \qquad
P = \operatorname{diag}(\eta_h \mathbf{1}_{\text{hidden}}, \eta_o \mathbf{1}_{\text{output}}),
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
a family of maps indexed by the two rates $(\eta_h,\eta_o)$. Its Jacobian is $DF(\theta) = I - P H(\theta)$
with $H$ the Hessian of $L$. An orbit of period $p$ is stable when all eigenvalues (multipliers) of
$DF(\theta_{p-1})\cdots DF(\theta_0)$ lie inside the unit circle, and it loses stability when a multiplier
crosses the circle: at $+1$ (fold), at $-1$ (flip, which doubles the period) or as a complex pair
$e^{\pm 2\pi i\omega}$ (Neimark--Sacker, which creates an invariant closed curve) \citep{kuznetsov2019}.

\paragraph{Why the route starts at period 2.} $DF(\theta)$ is similar to the symmetric matrix
$I - P^{1/2} H P^{1/2}$, so at a fixed point all multipliers are real and a fixed point can only lose stability
by fold or flip. The edge-of-stability oscillation is the period-2 orbit born at that flip. For a period-2 orbit
$\{\theta_a,\theta_b\}$, the two-step Jacobian is similar to $S_b S_a$ with
$S_j = I - P^{1/2} H(\theta_j) P^{1/2}$. A product of two symmetric matrices has real eigenvalues when one factor
is positive definite, but at the edge of stability each $S_j$ has a negative eigenvalue in the oscillating
direction, so complex multipliers, and with them a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation of the two-step map, become
possible. (This elementary argument is ours; the same observation appears in a literature check summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:related}.)

\paragraph{Circle maps and phase locking.} On an invariant circle the dynamics has a rotation number $\rho$, the
mean fraction of a turn per iterate. When $\rho = m/q$ is rational the motion can lock onto a periodic orbit; the
locked parameter sets are Arnold tongues, rotation numbers along a parameter path form a devil's staircase, and
the widest plateau between two locked rationals is typically their Farey mediant
\citep{arnold1965,jensen1983,jensen1984}. Breakdown of the circle leads to chaos \citep{curry1978,aronson1982,
ostlund1983,ruelle1971}. Because our circle belongs to the two-step map, a locked orbit at rotation $m/q$ has
period $2q$ for $F$.

\paragraph{Shrimps.} In two-parameter families of dissipative maps, periodic windows inside chaos often take a
characteristic ``shrimp'' shape bounded by fold and flip curves \citep{gallas1993,gallas1994,stoop2010,jones2012}.
We test for this anatomy explicitly rather than assume it.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Networks and data.} S1 is the published mean-field tanh configuration of the fractal-trainability
study \citep{sohldickstein2024}: width 8, inputs of dimension 8, no biases, as many standard-normal training points
as parameters, mean squared error. S1R is S1 with weight decay $10^{-3}$. S2 is a 13-parameter tanh regressor
(two inputs, three hidden units, biases, weight decay $10^{-3}$) fitted to 24 points from a random teacher. We refer
to a network and seed together, for example S2-28011. Training is deterministic full-batch gradient descent in
float64.

\paragraph{Maps.} Each map is a $256\times256$ grid of logarithmically spaced rate pairs (\nMaps{} maps of
\nCellsPerMap{} cells, coarse and zoomed). Every cell runs \nUpdates{} updates from the same initial parameters.
The last \nTail{} updates are screened for the smallest full-state recurrence period up to \nMaxPeriod{}; the
largest finite-time Lyapunov exponent (FTLE) is estimated with one tangent vector; cells are labeled fixed point,
periodic, chaotic (FTLE above \nChaosThreshold{} per update), drift (parameter norm still growing), unresolved or
divergent. The published binary score marks a run convergent when its normalized loss, averaged over the last 20
of the first 1{,}000 updates, is below one \citep{sohldickstein2024}.

\paragraph{Certification and embedded windows.} A connected component of equal-period cells qualifies as an
embedded window when its period is at least 3, no cell of its exterior ring is a fixed point or has a proper
divisor period, and at least half of the ring is chaotic on reruns. Its representative orbit is refined by damped
Newton iteration on the exact Jacobian and must close, be primitive and be attracting with margin $10^{-7}$. The
design, thresholds and analysis were pushed before outcomes were seen, and an independent audit repeated the
checks.

\paragraph{Continuation and boundary type.} From the center of a window, the certified orbit is continued along
64 rays in the rate plane until it loses stability, located by bisection, and the boundary is typed by the
crossing multiplier: fold ($+1$), flip ($-1$) or Neimark--Sacker (complex pair). The same procedure traces where
the period-2 orbit loses stability along slices of fixed output rate.

\paragraph{Rotation numbers and well-ordering.} For a non-periodic cell we take the two-step samples
$z_k=\theta_{2k}$, project them on their first two principal axes, and define the rotation number as the mean
unwrapped angle increment in turns per two steps. For a certified orbit with $q$ two-step points we sort the
points by angle and call the orbit well-ordered (a Birkhoff orbit of rotation $m/q$) when every step advances the
angular rank by the same shift $m$ with $\gcd(m,q)=1$. Orbits of 2 or 3 points pass trivially; only tests on at
least 5 points count as evidence. The null is the pass rate of the same test on uniformly random angles. A refined
version also orders points along the shortest closed tour through them and tests the points of
$F^{2^{k+1}}$ ($k=1,2,3$), which detects orbits born by period doubling inside a tongue; its null sums the
per-test rates.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Everything in this section is interpretation. The audited results in Section~\ref{sec:confirmed} stand on their
own, and the hypotheses here need their own prespecified tests.

The picture that fits the exploratory evidence is the quasi-periodic route familiar from forced oscillators and
circle maps \citep{curry1978,aronson1982,jensen1984}: the edge-of-stability oscillation is a period-2 orbit; past
a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation of the two-step map, training moves on an invariant circle with a rotation number
set by the crossing angle; rational rotations lock into Arnold tongues; and the circle breaks into chaos, with
period doubling inside tongues along the way. The argument in Section~\ref{sec:background} explains why this
route needs at least period 2 in gradient descent, and why per-layer rates are not required for it in principle.
Under this reading, the windows of Section~\ref{sec:confirmed} are mostly tongue windows, and classical shrimps
are a special case.

Three observations do not fit yet. The odd-period windows are not circle orbits; a one-dimensional
period-doubling route of the kind organized by shrimp hubs \citep{jones2012} is a candidate. Windows also coexist
with other attractors, so maps started from one initial point show which attractor that point reaches rather
than the full existence region of a window. And the evidence comes from two small networks.

A confirmatory test on fresh seeds and at least one new network would fix in advance: (a) a quasi-periodic band
between the period-2 region and chaos, with rotation numbers that start at the crossing angle; (b) locked windows
with period $2q$ at rotation $m/q$; (c) Neimark--Sacker loss of stability of the period-2 orbit at the band's
onset; and (d) an explicit null for any ordering claim about plateaus.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The networks have 13 and 72 parameters and are trained on synthetic data with deterministic full-batch updates
in float64. Screening labels are finite-time; chaotic labels rest on a single tangent vector and reruns. Rotation
numbers and well-ordering use two-dimensional projections of curves that live in 13 or 72 dimensions. The
mechanism analyses were chosen after seeing related outcomes, and the staircase and Neimark--Sacker analyses
cover two slices and one curve. No result addresses generalization, training speed, or modern training
practice.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Edge-of-stability dynamics and their simplified models include \citet{cohen2021}, \citet{kalra2025},
\citet{chenbruna2023}, \citet{song2023}, \citet{chen2024phases}, \citet{cohen2025central} and
\citet{hofmann2026}; chaos in deterministic training is discussed by \citet{kong2020} and \citet{jimenez2026}.
Fractal trainability boundaries are studied by \citet{sohldickstein2024}, \citet{torkamandi2025}, \citet{liu2024}
and \citet{liang2026}, and riddled basins and reproducibility by \citet{ly2025}. Shrimp-shaped windows and their
organization are reviewed in \citet{gallas1993,gallas1994,stoop2010,jones2012}; circle maps, Arnold tongues,
devil's staircases and torus breakdown in \citet{arnold1965,jensen1983,jensen1984,curry1978,aronson1982,
ostlund1983,ruelle1971}. A literature check using generic queries found no report of quasi-periodic training,
Arnold tongues or rotation-number staircases for optimizers acting on losses; \citet{hofmann2026} derives
complex multipliers of a 2-cycle without rotation numbers, and tongues are documented in learning dynamics for
games \citep{metaxas2026}. Every citation in this draft still needs checking at its primary source.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Code, plans and compact receipts live in the project repository; bulky raw maps live in a separate data store
referenced by SHA-256. Every figure is generated by a script in \path{paper/gd-route/figures/} that reads saved
evidence and writes a receipt with input hashes, the generator's hash, output hashes, alt text and the values it
displays. Every number in the text is a macro generated by \path{paper/gd-route/scripts/build_numbers.py};
\path{data/numbers.json} records each macro's source file, hash and computation, and
