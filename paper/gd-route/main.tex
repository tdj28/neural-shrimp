\begin{abstract}
Full-batch gradient descent with a large learning rate can oscillate, cycle or behave chaotically instead of
settling. Trainability maps over learning-rate planes record a binary convergent-or-divergent label, which
conflates these dynamical regimes. We resolve the dynamics behind such maps and organize the results around one
thesis: beyond the edge of stability, the period-2 oscillation gives way to quasi-periodic training and
phase-locked periodic windows.

(1) Confirmed in a prospectively designed, independently audited study of two small tanh networks with separate
learning rates for their two layers: the learning-rate planes contain \nConfirmed{} numerically certified
attracting periodic orbits (periods \nPeriodMin{} to \nPeriodMax{}) embedded in rerun-confirmed chaos, and
\pNotFixedMin{} to \pNotFixedMax{} of the cells a published binary convergence score labels convergent were not
classified as fixed points after \nUpdates{} updates.

(2) Exploratory: the edge-of-stability period-2 orbit loses stability along a traced curve where a complex pair of
two-step multipliers crosses the unit circle. The first Lyapunov coefficient is negative (supercritical) at
\nCritSuper{} of \nCritPoints{} points, and in \nDynConsistent{} of \nDynChecks{} dynamical checks the behaviour
matches its sign. Just beyond the curve the measured rotation number equals the crossing angle to a median
$\xContMedianMain$ and $\xContMedianRight$ in two maps. The same crossing appears in a 529-parameter network trained
on handwritten digits with one learning rate (predicted rotation \xRDPredicted{}, measured \xRDMeasured{}), and in
the two-mode linear network whose rotational edge was derived by Hofmann, where it is supercritical with locked
windows beyond it.

(3) Not established: under frozen rules, the ancestry test of a rule-selected window returned
``\tAncOutcome'' (post hoc, its locked region narrows with exponents \xAncWidthSlope{} and \xAncAmpSlope{}, against
1.5 and 0.5 for a resonance tongue); locking was not resolved in the real-data network; and locking and rotation
features added no robust predictive value beyond the Lyapunov exponent and sharpness.

Takeaway: the local Neimark--Sacker prediction is supported in these models; that the periodic windows descend
from its resonances is not yet established. Nothing here concerns stochastic or large-scale training or
establishes a training benefit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Training with a learning rate beyond the classical stability limit is common practice. At the edge of stability,
the sharpness of the loss hovers near $2/\eta$ while the loss still decreases over long horizons
\citep{cohen2021}; self-stabilization explains how sharpness is held there \citep{damian2023}, progressive
sharpening how it gets there \citep{agarwala2023}, and the catapult phase what happens to the loss at somewhat
larger rates \citep{lewkowycz2020}. Simplified models show period doubling and chaos as the step size grows
\citep{kalra2025,chen2024phases,hofmann2026}. Over two-dimensional hyperparameter planes, the boundary between
trainable and divergent settings is fractal \citep{sohldickstein2024,torkamandi2025,liu2024,liang2026}. These maps
record whether the loss falls and stays bounded, not what the parameters do.

We ask what the dynamics inside such maps are. We treat full-batch gradient descent with separate learning rates
for the hidden and output layers as a two-parameter family of maps on parameter space. For every cell of a
learning-rate grid we screen the parameter state at the end of a long run, certify selected periodic orbits with
Newton's method on the exact Jacobian, and follow orbits and their stability boundaries through the plane.

\paragraph{Thesis and status.} We organize the results around one thesis: beyond the edge of stability, the
period-2 oscillation gives way to quasi-periodic training on an invariant circle, rational rotation numbers lock
into periodic windows, and the windows of the learning-rate plane are largely these locked orbits. Each result
below says exactly what passed. The certified windows and the comparison with the binary score are confirmed.
Exploratory analyses support the local part of the thesis: the period-2 orbit acquires complex multipliers along
a curve, the crossing is supercritical on most of it with dynamics that match the normal form, quasi-periodic
training starts with a rotation equal to the crossing angle, and the same crossing appears in a real-data network
and in a linear network. The global part is not established: a frozen test did not confirm that a rule-selected
window descends from its resonance, and no prespecified path into sustained chaos has been tested. We call the
crossings Neimark--Sacker candidates except where the nondegeneracy coefficient and dynamical checks were computed.

\paragraph{Prior work.} Periodic windows, period doubling, positive Lyapunov exponents and broadband power
spectra in learning dynamics, with the learning rate as bifurcation parameter, date back at least to
\citet{vandermaas1990}, who studied a three-unit autoassociator. Their delay-embedded chaotic attractors are thin
folded curves, effectively one-dimensional chaos of the period-doubling kind, unlike the invariant circles of the
two-step map studied here. Period-colored maps over optimizer hyperparameters have been drawn from a
two-dimensional projection of the error, without full-state verification \citep{sveleba2026}. \citet{hofmann2026}
studies finite-step gradient descent as a dynamical system in solvable models: the edge of stability as the first
(flip) bifurcation of the training map, the period-doubling route with windows, and, in a two-mode depth-two linear
network, a ``rotational edge'' beyond which the unstable multipliers of the aligned 2-cycle are complex and the
system saturates in a persistently misaligned oscillatory state. The edge is Hofmann's result; we show, in that
model and in nonlinear networks, what lies beyond it: a supercritical Neimark--Sacker bifurcation with an invariant
circle whose rotation is set by the crossing angle, and locking. Riddled basins and learning-rate-dependent
reproducibility are documented by \citet{ly2025}. Invariant curves and phase-locking Arnold tongues appear in a
multiplicative-weights learning rule for population games \citep{metaxas2026}, and chaos in learning a two-person
game goes back to \citet{sato2002}; both concern games rather than descent on one loss. Heavy-ball momentum has
cycles that rotate by $1/K$ \citep{goujaud2025}.

\paragraph{This paper's contribution.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Confirmed: \nConfirmed{} certified attracting windows embedded in chaos in the learning-rate planes of two
small networks, repeated by an independent audit, and a cell-by-cell comparison showing that the published binary
score conflates fixed points, periodic orbits, chaos and drift.
\item Exploratory, local mechanism: a traced curve of complex-pair crossings of the period-2 orbit; its first
Lyapunov coefficient, generalized Neimark--Sacker points and dynamical checks; rotation-number maps in which
quasi-periodic training begins at the crossing angle.
\item Exploratory, generality: the same crossing with a quantitatively predicted rotation in a real-data network
with one learning rate; a supercritical Neimark--Sacker bifurcation with locking beyond the rotational edge of a
linear network; coexisting period-2 branches with their own crossings in a 72-parameter network.
\item Reported negatives: a frozen ancestry test that did not pass; no locking resolved in the real-data network;
no robust incremental value of rotation information for reproducibility or loss.
\item Code, receipts and a verification script that re-derive every figure, table and number.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}
\item No route to chaos: no prespecified connected path through sustained chaos has been tested.
\item No proof that the windows are Arnold tongues: the Birkhoff test is a diagnostic of projected orbits, and the
frozen ancestry test did not pass.
\item No claim about stochastic, minibatch or large-scale training, or other architectures and data beyond those
shown, and no training benefit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gradient descent as a family of maps}
\label{sec:background}

Let $L(\theta)$ be the full-batch training loss of a network with parameters $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^n$, split
into hidden-layer and output-layer blocks. Gradient descent with per-layer learning rates is the map
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1} = F(\theta_t) = \theta_t - P\,\nabla L(\theta_t), \qquad
P = \operatorname{diag}(\eta_h \mathbf{1}_{\text{hidden}}, \eta_o \mathbf{1}_{\text{output}}),
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
a family of maps indexed by the two rates $(\eta_h,\eta_o)$. Its Jacobian is $DF(\theta) = I - P H(\theta)$
with $H$ the Hessian of $L$. An orbit of period $p$ is stable when all eigenvalues (multipliers) of
$DF(\theta_{p-1})\cdots DF(\theta_0)$ lie inside the unit circle. Generically it loses stability when a
multiplier crosses the circle: at $+1$ (fold), at $-1$ (flip, which doubles the period) or as a complex pair
$e^{\pm 2\pi i\omega}$ (Neimark--Sacker). Under transversality and a nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient $d$, a
Neimark--Sacker bifurcation creates an invariant closed curve: attracting with amplitude growing like the square
root of the distance beyond the crossing when $d<0$ (supercritical), repelling and present before the crossing
when $d>0$ (subcritical) \citep{kuznetsov2019}.

\paragraph{Why the candidates start at period 2.} $DF(\theta)$ is similar to the symmetric matrix
$I - P^{1/2} H P^{1/2}$, so at a fixed point all multipliers are real and a fixed point loses stability by fold or
flip only. The edge-of-stability oscillation is the period-2 orbit born at that flip. For a period-2 orbit
$\{\theta_a,\theta_b\}$, the two-step Jacobian is similar to $S_b S_a$ with
$S_j = I - P^{1/2} H(\theta_j) P^{1/2}$. A product of two symmetric matrices has real eigenvalues when one factor
is positive definite, but at the edge of stability each $S_j$ has a negative eigenvalue in the oscillating
direction, so complex multipliers become possible. This is an elementary observation; complex unstable
multipliers of an edge-of-stability 2-cycle were derived for a two-mode linear network by \citet{hofmann2026}.

\paragraph{Circle maps and phase locking.} On an invariant circle the dynamics has a rotation number $\rho$, the
mean fraction of a turn per iterate. When $\rho = m/q$ is rational the motion can lock onto a periodic orbit; the
locked parameter sets are Arnold tongues, rooted at the points of the Neimark--Sacker curve where the crossing
angle is $m/q$, with widths that grow like the distance beyond the curve to the power $(q-2)/2$; rotation numbers
along a parameter path form a devil's staircase, and the widest plateau between two locked rationals is typically
their Farey mediant \citep{arnold1965,jensen1983,jensen1984}. Breakdown of the circle leads to chaos
\citep{curry1978,aronson1982,ostlund1983}. Because the circle in question belongs to the two-step map, a locked
orbit at rotation $m/q$ has period $2q$ for $F$; this relation is a consequence of the setting, not evidence.

\paragraph{Shrimps.} In two-parameter families of dissipative maps, periodic windows inside chaos often take a
characteristic ``shrimp'' shape bounded by fold and flip curves \citep{gallas1993,gallas1994}. We test for this
anatomy explicitly rather than assume it.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\input{generated-design-table.tex}

\paragraph{Networks and data.} Table~\ref{tab:design} lists the design of the map study. S1 reproduces the
published mean-field configuration of the fractal-trainability study \citep{sohldickstein2024}; S1R adds weight
decay; S2 is a 13-parameter tanh regressor fitted to points from a random tanh teacher. We refer to a network and
seed together, for example S2-28011. Training is deterministic full-batch gradient descent in float64.

\paragraph{Three different quantities.} The published binary score measures loss reduction: a run counts as
convergent when its loss, normalized by the initial loss and averaged over the last 20 of the first
\nScoreHorizon{} updates, is below one \citep{sohldickstein2024}. Our screening labels classify the parameter state
at the end of a finite run. Certification validates individual periodic orbits. The three need not agree, and we
compare them cell by cell.

\paragraph{Screening.} Every cell runs \nUpdates{} updates from the seed's initial parameters. The last \nTail{}
updates are screened for the smallest full-state recurrence period up to \nMaxPeriod{}: a period $p$ requires the
state to recur with componentwise tolerance $\xScreenAtol + \xScreenRtol\,|\theta|$ over the whole tail, which
spans at least \nMinRepeats{} repeats. The largest finite-time Lyapunov exponent (FTLE) is averaged over the last
\nFtleSteps{} updates after \nFtleAlign{} updates of tangent alignment. Labels are fixed point, periodic, chaotic
(FTLE above \nChaosThreshold{} per update), drift (parameter norm still growing by more than \pDrift{} over the FTLE
window), unresolved, or divergent (non-finite or larger than $\xDivergence$).

\paragraph{Numerically certified.} An orbit of period $p$ is numerically certified when damped Newton iteration
on $F^p(\theta)-\theta=0$, using the exact chain-rule Jacobian of $F^p$, reaches an undamped relative correction
below $\xCertNewton$ within \nCertIterations{} iterations; the converged point closes to within
$\xCertAtol + \xCertRtol\,|\theta|$ in every component; it lies within $\xCertCandidate$ (relative) of the
screened candidate; every proper-divisor iterate is separated from it by at least $\xCertDivisor$ (relative), so
the period is primitive; and it is attracting when the spectral radius of the monodromy matrix is below
$1-\xCertMargin$. This is a floating-point verification, not a computer-assisted proof.

\paragraph{Embedded windows and audit.} A connected component of equal-period cells qualifies as an embedded
window when its period is at least \nGateMinPeriod{}, it has at least \nMinComponent{} cells, no cell of its
exterior ring is a fixed point or has a proper-divisor period, at least \pRingFraction{} of the ring is
rerun-confirmed chaos (reruns of \nRingSteps{} updates with a \nRingFtle{}-update FTLE and a different tangent
vector, up to \nRingCap{} ring cells per component), and its representative orbit is certified and attracting.
The audit was a separate program that imports neither the batched engine, the orbit certifier nor the selection
helpers: it rewrote the one-cell map, the recurrence screen, the zoom rule and the component and ring rules,
rechecked every certificate with finite-difference Jacobians (all \nAuditAllOk{} of \nAuditChecked{} pass closure,
primitivity, stability and seed checks; spectral radii agree within $\xAuditMaxRadiusDiff$), re-simulated the
ring cells of every qualifying component, recomputed the gate on its own evidence, and found \nAuditMismatched{}
mismatches among the \nAuditReceiptFiles{} receipt files.

\paragraph{Continuation, boundary type and criticality.} From the center of a window, its certified orbit is
continued along 64 rays in the rate plane until it loses stability, located by bisection, and the boundary is
typed by the crossing multiplier: fold ($+1$), flip ($-1$) or complex pair. The same procedure traces where the
period-2 orbit loses stability along slices of fixed output rate; several traces are merged into one curve. At each
point of that curve we compute the first Lyapunov coefficient $d$ of the Neimark--Sacker normal form with
Kuznetsov's formula for $n$-dimensional maps, using the exact dense two-step Jacobian and Richardson-extrapolated
differences of exact Jacobian-vector products for the higher derivatives; the implementation reproduces the
explicit planar formula on random planar maps.

\paragraph{Rotation numbers and the Birkhoff test.} For a non-periodic cell we take the two-step samples
$z_k=\theta_{2k}$, project them on their first two principal axes, and define the rotation number as the mean
unwrapped angle increment in turns per two steps. Rotation-number maps run this on every cell of a grid (float64
on a GPU, \nUpdates{} screening updates followed by 6{,}000 two-step samples). For a certified orbit of period $p$
we take its two-step points ($q=p/2$ points for even $p$, $q=p$ for odd $p$), sort them by angle in their principal
plane, and say the orbit passes when every step advances the angular rank by the same shift $m$ with $\gcd(m,q)=1$,
the ordering of a Birkhoff orbit of rotation $m/q$. The number of points $q$, and therefore the relation $p=2q$, is
fixed by the period; what the test measures is the ordering and the shift. Orbits with 2 or 3 points pass by
construction. The null is the pass probability of the same test for uniformly random angles; for a group of orbits
we report the expected number of chance passes. The refined test also orders points along the shortest closed tour
through them and tests the points of $F^{2^{k+1}}$ ($k=1,2,3$), which can detect orbits born by period doubling
inside a locked window; it counts only tests on at least 5 points, and its null for each orbit is the sum of the
per-test pass probabilities (a union bound), so the summed null is an upper bound on the expected number of chance
passes.

\paragraph{Resonance ancestry.} For a window selected by a fixed rule (the $1/5$ window of S2-28011), we locate the
points of the crossing curve whose angle is $1/5$, check the onset there, and follow the locked period-10 interval
on six cross-sections at distances $0.0128/4^k$ beyond the curve, finding both of its fold edges on each. Acceptance
rules were frozen before the network run: controls must pass first; the interval must be fold-bounded on every
level with shrinking width and amplitude; and a linear fit of its center over all levels must extrapolate to within
$\xAncTolerance$ decades of the predicted point. A second stage tries to connect the tracked interval to the window.

At \nCritSuper{} of the \nCritPoints{} curve points the first Lyapunov coefficient is negative, so the crossing is
supercritical: quasi-periodic training is born continuously (Figure~\ref{fig:criticality}). At \nCritSub{} points,
at both ends of the curve, it is positive. The sign changes \nChenciner{} times, near $\log_{10}\eta_o$ = \tChenciner{};
these are generalized Neimark--Sacker (Chenciner) points. In \nDynConsistent{} of \nDynChecks{} dynamical checks the
behaviour matches the sign: at supercritical points the circle amplitude follows the square-root prediction (median
relative error \pDynMedianError{}, largest \pDynMaxError{} next to a sign change), and at subcritical points the state
jumps away beyond the curve while escape thresholds before it grow like the square root of the offset, with
hysteresis. In the 72-parameter S1R network, \nCritSOneRSuper{} of \nCritSOneR{} traced points are supercritical.

The fixed-start maps show quasi-periodic training beyond the supercritical arc. Where they show divergence just
beyond the curve, the cause is not the bifurcation: at \nBasinPoints{} curve points the fixed-start map is divergent
even just below the curve, where the period-2 orbit is stable, and \nBasinSuper{} of those points are
supercritical, with a small attracting circle reached when training starts near the 2-cycle. The divergent arc of
the fixed-start maps is a basin effect of the shared initialization, not the bifurcation.

\paragraph{Coexisting branches in S1R.} The 72-parameter S1R network has several coexisting period-2 orbits, each

\paragraph{Real data, one learning rate.} A 64-8-1 tanh network (529 parameters) trained with one global learning
rate and weight decay on 1{,}024 standardized UCI digit images (two-class task) has a certified period-2 orbit that
loses stability at $\log_{10}\eta = \xRDCrossing$ with dominant two-step multiplier \tRDMultiplier{}, a complex pair on

locked window was resolved; at rotations near 0.008 the relevant tongues have very large denominators.

\paragraph{Hofmann's rotational edge is a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation.} In the two-mode depth-two linear network of
\citet{hofmann2026}, the aligned 2-cycle loses stability at his rotational edge $a(s_1+s_2)=2$, which we reproduce to

two-step map. Without weight decay the second mode's minima form a neutral continuum and the circle amplitude grows
with exponent \tMMSlopeNoWD{}, a degenerate bifurcation; with weight decay 0.01 the exponent is \tMMSlopeWD{}, the
square-root growth of a supercritical Neimark--Sacker bifurcation, and the rotation number at an offset of
$\xMMSmallestOffset$ is within $\xMMRotationGap$ of the crossing angle (Figure~\ref{fig:generality}B and C). Beyond
the edge the rotation locks at \tMMLockedSeven{} for $s_2/s_1 = 0.7$, and at \tMMLockedEight{} in a fine scan at

with $\lambda = 0.2$ and unequal layer rates (crossing angle \xMMTwoWeightAngle{}; the circle amplitude grows by a
factor \xMMTwoWeightAmpRatio{} for a fourfold offset, close to the square-root value 2), found as one of
\nMMGridNS{} of \nMMGrid{} cases of a coupled four-weight family that ended in a complex-pair crossing; boundary types
for two held-out couplings were predicted correctly in \nMMHeldoutCorrect{} of \nMMHeldout{} cases.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Everything in this section is interpretation. The confirmed results in Section~\ref{sec:confirmed} stand on
their own, and the hypotheses here need their own prespecified tests.

The local part of the thesis now rests on several independent kinds of evidence: the crossing multipliers, the
normal-form coefficient and its dynamical checks, the continuity of the rotation number with the crossing angle in
two maps, and the same behaviour in a real-data network and in a solvable linear network. The picture that fits is
the quasi-periodic organization familiar from circle maps and forced oscillators
\citep{curry1978,aronson1982,jensen1984}: the edge-of-stability oscillation is a period-2 orbit; past a
supercritical crossing, training moves on an invariant circle with a rotation number set by the crossing angle;
rational rotations lock; and the circle breaks. The global part, that the periodic windows of the learning-rate
plane are resonance tongues of this bifurcation, is supported by the Birkhoff test and by the post-hoc tongue
scaling, but its frozen test did not pass. The frozen estimator mixed non-asymptotic and asymptotic levels; a
confirmatory version should prespecify an asymptotic-regime estimator, and connecting a tongue to a distant window
needs two-parameter fold-curve continuation.

The earliest spectral diagnostic of learning dynamics we know of is that of \citet{vandermaas1990}, who used
broadband spectra to recognize chaotic learning; here a spectral peak carries the rotation number, and an
autoregressive fit carries the complex multiplier before the crossing.

\paragraph{Open questions.} The odd-period windows did not pass the Birkhoff diagnostic; whether they are organized
as in periodicity hubs \citep{jones2012} is open. Fixed-start maps mix local criticality with basin accessibility
from one shared initialization, as the divergent arc shows, so statements about trainability maps need both. In the
real-data network only one of the examined period-2 branches crossed by complex multipliers, and how common such
branches are in larger networks is unknown.

\paragraph{The tests that would settle the local claims.} The criticality prediction is ready to freeze for fresh
losses: the sign of $d$ at each crossing predicts a circle of normal-form amplitude beyond a supercritical edge, and
a jump with square-root escape thresholds and hysteresis at a subcritical one. An existence-and-replication test
would apply this, together with the onset and rotation criteria, to two prespecified independent losses across two
architectures, one of them unused so far, with no substitution. The near-root ancestry test is close to ready once
its estimator is restricted to the asymptotic levels.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The map study uses networks of 13 and 72 parameters on synthetic data; the real-data result is one branch of one
529-parameter network on one subset and task; the linear network is a solvable model. Training is deterministic,
full batch and in float64. Screening labels are finite-time; chaotic labels rest on single-tangent exponents and
reruns. Certification is a floating-point verification. The first Lyapunov coefficient is a local, third-order
quantity; it predicts the birth of a small circle, not how far it survives. Rotation numbers and the Birkhoff test
use two-dimensional projections of curves in many dimensions. Most mechanism analyses were chosen after seeing
related outcomes. No result addresses generalization, training speed or modern training practice.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Edge of stability.} \citet{cohen2021} describe the edge of stability; \citet{damian2023} explain
self-stabilization of sharpness at the edge, whereas we follow what happens after the period-2 oscillation itself
loses stability; \citet{agarwala2023} show progressive sharpening to the edge in second-order regression models,
the approach to the regime we study beyond it; \citet{lewkowycz2020} describe a catapult phase at large rates, in
which the loss first grows and then settles in flatter regions, while our maps resolve the long-run dynamics of
rates where training does not settle. \citet{hofmann2026} derives the rotational edge whose far side we study.
Further analyses include \citet{kalra2025}, \citet{chenbruna2023}, \citet{song2023}, \citet{chen2024phases} and
\citet{cohen2025central}.

\paragraph{Chaos and fractals in training.} Chaos in deterministic training is discussed by \citet{kong2020} and
\citet{jimenez2026}; fractal trainability boundaries by \citet{sohldickstein2024}, \citet{torkamandi2025},
\citet{liu2024} and \citet{liang2026}; riddled basins and reproducibility by \citet{ly2025}. Learning dynamics in
games show chaos \citep{sato2002} and phase locking \citep{metaxas2026}, for adaptive rules rather than descent on
one loss.

\paragraph{Dynamical systems.} Shrimp-shaped windows are described by \citet{gallas1993,gallas1994}; circle maps,
Arnold tongues, devil's staircases and the breakdown of invariant circles by
\citet{arnold1965,jensen1983,jensen1984,curry1978,aronson1982,ostlund1983}; numerical bifurcation analysis of maps,
including the normal-form coefficients used here, by \citet{kuznetsov2019}. A literature check using generic
queries found no report of quasi-periodic training, Arnold tongues or rotation-number staircases for optimizers
acting on one loss.
